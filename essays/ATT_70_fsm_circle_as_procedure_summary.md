## Overview

> Classical geometry presents the circle, arc, segment, chord, and line as static ideal forms: Platonic objects that exist independently of any process that produces them. This paper argues that this presentation inverts the actual order of symbolic formation. These classical objects are not primordial. They are compressed symbolic residues of finite construction procedures — the stabilised endpoints of sequences of marking, sweeping, cutting, drawing, measuring, and symbolic stabilisation. The paper develops a Geofinite reconstruction of the circle and its associated geometric forms, introducing the Geofinite Trace Function, the concept of symbolic cost, and a constructive programme for Geofinite geometry. The key result is that a diagram is not the geometry — it is the compressed document of a finite construction process.

## Architectural Note

ATT_70 opens the constructive programme for Geofinite geometry. It takes the most familiar objects of classical geometry — the circle, arc, line, segment — and shows that each is a downstream compression of a finite procedure, not an ideal form that procedures merely approximate. The paper is simultaneously:

1. **A foundational critique** — every classical geometric "object" is reanalysed as a stabilised symbolic residue.
2. **A formal reconstruction** — the Geofinite Trace Function provides a named, structured formalism for relating construction procedure to symbolic trace, uncertainty, and cost.
3. **A programme paper** — it opens six research tasks for the future development of Geofinite geometry.

## The Five Geofinite Commitments

The paper opens by stating five foundational commitments that govern the reconstruction:

1. **No idealised objects** — geometric forms are finite symbolic constructions, not Platonic entities
2. **Measurement is primary** — all geometry begins with a finite act of marking or measuring
3. **Procedures generate traces** — construction procedures produce symbolic trajectories, not ideal curves
4. **Projections produce diagrams** — two-dimensional diagrams are projections of richer three-dimensional construction trajectories
5. **Formulae are compressions** — symbolic formulae such as s = rθ are compressed representations of finite procedures, not statements about ideal objects

## The Generonic Boundary

A finite mark crosses the Generonic Boundary when it stabilises as a symbol. Before the boundary: physical action, tool movement, material trace. After: symbolic object with provenance, uncertainty, and cost. Classical geometry begins at the far side of this boundary — after the compression — and treats the result as primary. Geofinitism restores the boundary to view and asks: what was the procedure that crossed it?

## The Alphonic Limit in Geometry

The Alphonic Limit applies to geometry exactly as it applies to arithmetic and measurement:

$$\mathcal{N}_\alpha^{(3D)} \sim (s, V_\alpha, U_\alpha, P_C)$$

where:
- *s* is the finite symbolic mark (the geometric "point" or trace segment)
- *V_α* is the finite volume sphere at the Alphonic Limit — the minimum spatial uncertainty
- *U_α* is the uncertainty structure
- *P_C* is the provenance of the construction

**A point is not a dimensionless entity. It is a finite symbolic mark** — the smallest stable trace obtainable through first-order measurement. No geometric construction can produce a mark smaller than V_α without invoking higher-order modelling.

## The Line as Finite Pipe

In classical geometry, a line is an extension without width — a one-dimensional ideal in a plane. In Geofinitism:

$$\ell_G \sim T_\alpha^{(3D)}(s, V_\alpha, U_\alpha, P_C)$$

A line is a **finite pipe** — a three-dimensional symbolic trace with:
- Physical width (at minimum, V_α)
- Uncertainty bands on both sides
- Provenance: which tool, which hand, which procedure produced it
- Symbolic cost: how many marks, how much uncertainty accumulation

The classical line is a **flattened idealisation** of this finite pipe — useful for computation, but achieved by suppressing width, uncertainty, and provenance entirely.

## The Arc as Curved Pipe

An arc is produced by a sweeping procedure — a compass, a string, a rotation. Geofinitally:

$$\gamma_G \sim T_\alpha^{(3D)}(\theta_{sweep}, r_G, V_\alpha, U_\alpha, P_C)$$

An arc is a **curved finite pipe** — a swept symbolic trace inheriting uncertainty from each step of the sweeping action. The classical arc (a portion of a perfect circle defined by angle and radius) is the Platonic compression of this swept procedure. The sweeping action is the primary reality; the arc formula is its downstream compression.

## Segments and Sectors

A segment or sector is not first an ideally bounded region. It is a **bounded construction with uncertain boundary**:

$$S_G \sim (\gamma_G, \ell_G, U_{\partial S}, P_C)$$

The boundary uncertainty U_∂S is irreducible — it inherits from the uncertainty of the traces that bound the region. Classical geometry treats the boundary as a dimensionless line (zero width). Geofinitism restores the boundary's actual width.

## The Construction Procedure and Its Trajectory

Every geometric construction is a finite sequence of procedural steps. The **construction trajectory** Γ_C records this sequence as a symbolic time series:

$$\Gamma_C(t) = [C_t, C_{t-\tau}, C_{t-2\tau}, \ldots]$$

This is a **delay embedding** of the construction sequence — a technique from dynamical systems theory (Takens embedding) applied to geometric construction. The delay reconstruction recovers the hidden procedural structure from the final symbolic trace. A classical diagram D_C suppresses this trajectory:

$$D_C \sim \Pi_{2D}(\mathcal{C}_{proc}) \quad \text{with } K \text{ hidden}$$

A Geofinite diagram records it:

$$D_G \sim (\Pi_{2D}(\mathcal{C}_{proc}), K, U_\alpha, P_C)$$

The two-dimensional projection is the same in both cases. What differs is whether the symbolic cost K, uncertainty U_α, and provenance P_C are preserved or suppressed.

## Symbolic Cost

Every construction procedure carries a **symbolic cost** K(C_proc). The paper identifies eight cost types:

| Cost type | What it measures |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Mark count | Number of individual marks required |
| Sweep count | Number of compass/rotation sweeps |
| Cut count | Number of boundary constructions |
| Intersection count | Number of intersection operations |
| Uncertainty accumulation | Total U_α accumulated through the procedure |
| Provenance depth | How many layers of prior construction the procedure depends on |
| Projection loss | How much trajectory information is lost in the 2D projection |
| Compression ratio | Ratio of procedural steps to final symbolic formula length |

A classical diagram suppresses all eight. A Geofinite diagram records them. **A circle divided into many small arcs is not merely a finer version of the same object** — it is a different symbolic construction with greater cost, additional marks, increased uncertainty accumulation, and altered provenance.

## Compression into Formula

The final stage of geometric compression is the formula. For arc length:

$$s = r\theta$$

This compresses a sweeping procedure into a symbolic relation between arc length, radius, and angle. Similarly:

$$A = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$$

compresses a sector construction into an area formula.

**These formulae are not rejected. They are reclassified.** A formula is a narrative-symbolic compression of a construction process:

$$F_C \sim \mathcal{S}(\mathcal{C}_{proc})$$

where S is the symbolic compression operator. A Geofinite formula preserves provenance and uncertainty:

$$F_G \sim (F_C, U_F, P_C, K)$$

For arc length specifically, the Geofinite formula is:

$$s_G \sim (r, \theta, U_s, P_C, K_s)$$

rather than s = rθ as an unqualified ideal relation. The equality sign is replaced by a tilde relation: s ~ rθ, indicating a model-mediated symbolic correspondence within declared limits. This is not a weakening of mathematics. It is a strengthening of its measurement accountability.

## The Geofinite Trace Function

The paper introduces a named function to formalise the reconstruction:

$$\mathfrak{T}_\alpha : (\mathcal{C}_{proc}, P_C) \longrightarrow (\Gamma_C, \mathcal{N}_\alpha^{(3D)}, U_\alpha, K)$$

The **Geofinite Trace Function** maps a construction procedure and its provenance into: the construction trajectory, the finite symbolic trace at the Alphonic Limit, the uncertainty structure, and the symbolic cost. Specific instances:

| Construction | Geofinite Trace |
|-------------|-----------------|
| Circle | $\mathfrak{T}_\alpha(\mathcal{C}_{circle}, P_C) \sim (\Gamma_{circle}, \gamma_G, U_\alpha, K_C)$ |
| Line | $\mathfrak{T}_\alpha(\mathcal{C}_{line}, P_C) \sim (\Gamma_{line}, \ell_G, U_\alpha, K_\ell)$ |
| Arc | $\mathfrak{T}_\alpha(\mathcal{C}_{arc}, P_C) \sim (\Gamma_{arc}, \gamma_G, U_\alpha, K_\gamma)$ |

The classical diagram is then:
$$D_C \sim \Pi_{2D}(\mathfrak{T}_\alpha(\mathcal{C}_{proc}, P_C))$$

The classical formula is:
$$F_C \sim \mathcal{S}(\Pi_{2D}(\mathfrak{T}_\alpha(\mathcal{C}_{proc}, P_C)))$$

**The classical object is downstream of the Geofinite trace** — always the last step, never the first.

## Results of the Enquiry

The paper states ten formal results:

1. Classical geometric objects may be understood as compressed symbolic residues of construction procedures.
2. A circle divided into arcs and segments is not first a Platonic partition, but a finite procedural trace.
3. The classical line is a flattened idealisation of a finite symbolic pipe.
4. The classical arc is a flattened idealisation of a finite curved pipe.
5. Sectors and segments are bounded by finite uncertain traces, not by dimensionless ideal boundaries.
6. The Alphonic Limit prevents infinite subdivision from being treated as first-order measurement.
7. A two-dimensional diagram is a projection of a richer construction trajectory.
8. Delay embedding provides a formal way to think about reconstructing procedural geometry from symbolic time series.
9. Formulae such as s = rθ and A = ½r²θ are compressed symbolic summaries of finite construction processes.
10. The Geofinite Trace Function provides a formal structure for relating construction procedure, symbolic trace, uncertainty, cost, and diagrammatic projection.

## Philosophical Discussion

The philosophical significance of the reconstruction lies in the **recovery of procedure**.

Modern symbolic mathematics presents geometry as a world of static ideal forms. These forms are powerful because they allow efficient manipulation, generalisation, and proof. But they also obscure the finite processes by which geometric symbols arise.

The Geofinite view does not deny the usefulness of idealisation. **It denies that idealisation is foundational.** The foundational act is measurement-construction. Accordingly:

- A point is not first an ideal zero-dimensional entity. It is a finite symbolic mark.
- A line is not first an ideal extension without width. It is a finite trace.
- An arc is not first a pure curve. It is a swept symbolic pipe.
- A segment is not first an ideal region. It is a bounded construction with uncertain boundary.
- A formula is not first a truth about a Platonic object. It is a compressed relation among symbols generated through procedures.

The deeper claim: **A diagram is not the geometry. It is the compressed document of a finite construction process.**

This parallels the earlier Geofinite critique of the ket (ATT_09) and the Heaviside function: in each case, modern notation compresses a dynamic process into a static object. When historical texts instruct the reader to "draw, cut, extend, describe, or construct," they preserve something that modern notation has hidden. The action is not merely pedagogical — it is part of the origin of the symbol.

## Constructive Programme for Geofinite Geometry

ATT_70 opens a six-task constructive programme for future development:

1. **Catalogue** classical geometric entities according to their procedural origins — points, lines, circles, arcs, chords, sectors, triangles, polygons, conics, and surfaces, each reconstructed as finite symbolic procedures.
2. **Define Geofinite counterparts** for classical objects: a line becomes a finite pipe, an arc a curved pipe, a boundary an uncertainty band, a region a bounded symbolic construction.
3. **Develop projection maps** between classical and Geofinite forms: Π_ideal : G_F → G_C (projection) and R_GF : G_C → G_F (reconstruction).
4. **Formalise symbolic cost** K(C_proc) and study how cost changes with subdivision, refinement, projection, and formulaic compression.
5. **Explore delay-embedding representations** of construction sequences, treating classical geometric figures as final projections of symbolic trajectories.
6. **Connect Geofinite geometry** with the FSM arithmetic engine and Alphonic Projection Layers — numerical representations, geometric diagrams, and quantum state symbols as different projections of finite symbolic construction processes.

This programme does not abolish classical geometry. It places classical geometry inside a wider representational account.

## Conclusion

> *"The circle is not first an ideal object. It is a procedure whose symbolic trace has been stabilised, projected, compressed, and formalised. The same is true of the arc, the segment, the chord, and the line."*

Modern mathematics has gained great power by compressing procedures into static symbolic forms. But this power has come with a loss: the finite construction process has been hidden. Geofinitism restores that process to the centre.

The result is not a rejection of classical geometry, but a deeper account of its symbolic foundations:
- A *classical line* is useful. But a Geofinite line remembers that it is a pipe.
- A *classical arc* is useful. But a Geofinite arc remembers that it is a swept trace.
- A *classical diagram* is useful. But a Geofinite diagram remembers that it is a projection.
- A *classical formula* is useful. But a Geofinite formula remembers that it is a compression.

Geofinite geometry recovers what modern notation has compressed: **the act by which the symbol first came to be.**
