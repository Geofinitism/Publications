## Three Key Ideas

### 1. The Fine-Structure Constant Is a Cubic Compression Term, Not a Dimensionless Primitive

Standard classical relations, rearranged algebraically, produce:

> m_e / e² ~ f_Rydberg μ₀^(3/2) ε₀^(1/2) / α³

In the classical basin, this is a consistency relation among constants. In the Geofinite basin, the left-hand side is the **charge–mass interactional identity** (m_e : e² — a single measured entity, not a ratio of independent primitives), and α³ in the denominator is a **cubic compression term** mediating between that identity, Rydberg-scale recurrence, and electromagnetic response. Dimensionlessness does not make α representation-independent: it is still written in a base, depends on a chain of measurements, and carries provenance.

### 2. Every Symbol Has Finite Geometry — and the Exponents Reveal the Missing Dimensions

Each symbol in the fine-structure relation — m_e, e, μ₀, ε₀, f_Rydberg, α — is a Nexil: a finite symbolic unit with volume, uncertainty, and provenance. When the geometry is restored:

> α ~ (V_interaction / V_symbolic)^(1/3)   →   α³ ~ V_interaction / V_symbolic

The fine-structure constant is a **ratio of volumes** — the physical interactional volume of the electron charge–mass process divided by the symbolic volume of its representation. The exponents are traces of missing geometry: μ₀^(3/2) compresses along three half-dimensions; ε₀^(1/2) compresses along one; together they sum to 2 (the 1/c² structure) but are split unevenly — a **signature of how measurement projects onto different axes of the symbolic container**. This is not an SI artifact. It is metrological accountability.

### 3. Spin Is a Flattened Narrative — the Stern–Gerlach Result Follows from Saddle-Point Geometry

The discrete splitting of a beam in the Stern–Gerlach experiment is standardly interpreted as evidence of spin — a quantum degree of freedom with no classical analogue. Geofinitism offers a different account: the splitting is the consequence of **saddle-point geometry**. A finite, asymmetric, inseparable charge–mass interaction (not a point particle, not symmetric) passing through a saddle-point field is forced to exit through one of exactly two valleys — not because of quantisation, but because of the topology of the saddle. Spin is a flattened narrative: a convenient symbolic compression of a complex, finite, asymmetric, saddle-point dynamical process. Like the ket and Heaviside function, it is not wrong — it is flattened. And the 3/2, 1/2, and cubic α³ in the fine-structure relation are precisely its static traces.

## Five Reflection Questions

1. **The Charge-Mass Identity**: ATT_75 claims that m_e and e are not independent properties but "symbolic projections of a single measured interactional identity." What experimental evidence — past or hypothetical — would it take to test whether charge and mass are truly separable? What would falsify the FSM claim of their inseparability?

2. **The Cubic Clue**: The fine-structure constant appears as α³ in the denominator of the charge-mass relation. If α ~ (V_interaction/V_symbolic)^(1/3), what does V_interaction represent physically? What does V_symbolic represent? How would you design a measurement that accesses these volumes separately?

3. **Asymmetric Exponents**: The exponents 3/2 (for μ₀) and 1/2 (for ε₀) sum to 2, matching the 1/c² structure of the electromagnetic wave equation. ATT_75 reads this asymmetry as a "signature of measurement projection onto different axes." What would it mean for a measurement apparatus to project unevenly onto μ₀ vs. ε₀ axes? Can you construct a gedanken experiment that would probe this asymmetry?

4. **Saddle-Point Discreteness**: The Third Reading argues that the Stern–Gerlach discrete split follows from saddle-point topology (two exit valleys), not quantisation. If this is correct, what predictions would differ from the standard spin account? Are there experimental configurations — perhaps with non-saddle field geometries — where the two accounts would diverge?

5. **Metrological Dependence of α**: ATT_75 claims that in another epoch with a different Alphonic Limit, α might be different — not because physics changes, but because the geometry of representation changes. How does this compare to claims in physics that fundamental constants might vary across cosmological time? Is the Geofinite claim stronger, weaker, or orthogonal to the standard cosmological varying-constants programme?
