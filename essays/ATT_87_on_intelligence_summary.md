## Core Claim

Intelligence is not encountered directly as a substance residing within a person, animal or machine. It is registered through finite interactions and subsequently attributed to a system by observers working within inherited languages, practices and systems of measurement. A coherent account of Artificial General Intelligence therefore requires something presently missing: a workable model of language, registration, semantic uncertainty and symbolic change.

The question before AGI is consequently not only:

> **Can a machine become generally intelligent?**

It is also:

> **What dynamical system produces the judgement of intelligence, and what language makes that judgement possible?**

Until this prior question is addressed, AGI will remain both an engineering ambition and an unstable symbol.

---

## Overview

*Before Artificial General Intelligence* is the longest and most historically grounded essay in the corpus. It approaches the AGI question by refusing to begin with a definition of intelligence and instead asking how the category was formed, stabilised and measured. The argument moves through the history of intelligence as a concept, identifies the forgotten role of language in every measurement of intelligence, develops a Geofinite account of meaning as a dynamically stabilised trajectory, and concludes that a model of language must precede — not follow — a declaration of AGI.

The essay draws on Wittgenstein's account of language as a "form of life" to propose that a form of life may be understood as a dynamical system: organisms, environments, practices, instruments, symbols, memories and institutions interacting recursively. Language is generated within this system and then acts back upon it, stabilising some distinctions while restricting or redirecting others. Intelligence, on this account, is not a possession but a judgement formed from measurable interactions between a system, its environment, its history and the symbolic framework through which its conduct becomes distinguishable.

## Structure and Key Argument

The essay proceeds through twenty-three sections.

**The Question Before AGI.** The word *intelligence* appears familiar but its historical uses reveal considerable semantic uncertainty — intellect, reason, judgement, wisdom, adaptation, learning, problem-solving, measurable aptitude, computational performance, economic productivity. Before asking whether a machine possesses general intelligence, we must ask how the human category was formed, stabilised and measured.

**Intelligence Before Measurement.** Long before intelligence became a psychological score, communities distinguished between forms of skilful perception, judgement and action embedded in particular practices. Ancient Greek terms were plural and non-equivalent: *nous* (intellect/apprehension), *logos* (reason/account), *phronesis* (practical judgement), *sophia* (wisdom), *techne* (skilled making). The Latin *intelligentia* named a capacity of understanding, not a standardised quantity. During the early modern period, Hobbes, Descartes and Leibniz contributed to an expectation that mental operations might be describable as ordered procedures — but this was the formation of a new symbolic language, not the discovery of a previously hidden mechanical mind.

**The Century of Measurement.** Industrialisation created pressure for portable judgements; evolutionary thought widened the category toward adaptation. The late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries transformed intelligence into an object of systematic measurement. The Binet-Simon scale (1905) compressed a many-layered interaction into a number:

> child → examiner → questions → language → scoring procedure

The score was attributed to the child, but it arose from a *coupled arrangement*. Spearman's 1904 paper proposed a common factor *g* across correlated performances but himself distinguished the reliability of measurement from whether the measured form deserved the name *intelligence*. Over time the distinction became compressed. The intelligence score exemplifies a broader feature of symbolic measurement:

> complex interaction → selected observations → symbolic compression → portable value

**Turing's Elegant Substitution.** Alan Turing's 1950 paper replaced the question "Can machines think?" with a more precisely arranged interaction: the imitation game. This was elegant because it avoided dependence upon an inaccessible inner condition. The imitation game measures a result produced across an interaction, not a quantity residing inside the machine. This is compatible with an important Geofinite principle: when a term cannot be measured directly, construct a finite interaction and state what is actually registered. However, later culture transformed the paper's careful reframing into a threshold between machine performance and machine intelligence — compressing the original exploratory procedure into a cultural marker. The modern discussion of machine intelligence was linguistic from the beginning: three interacting models participated in every imitation game (1. the machine's model of linguistic continuation; 2. the human participant's use of language; 3. the evaluator's model of human and machine behaviour).

**Artificial Intelligence and the Modern Epoch.** The Dartmouth proposal (McCarthy, Minsky, Rochester, Shannon, 1955) conjectured that every aspect of intelligence could in principle be described precisely enough for a machine to simulate it. Each step depended upon language. The Transformer architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017) and large-scale language models (GPT-3, 2020) produced a practical appearance of generality: a single linguistic interface could enter many human language-games. But this created a measurement ambiguity — has the system acquired general intelligence, or has it acquired broad access to the symbolic interface through which humans describe and coordinate many specialised activities?

**AGI as an Epoch-Specific Construction.** Contemporary definitions of AGI reflect the current epoch: industrial, computational, economic and benchmark-driven. OpenAI's Charter defines AGI as highly autonomous systems that outperform humans at most economically valuable work. Google DeepMind's framework classifies systems by breadth, depth of performance and autonomy. These definitions are operationally valuable; they can also localise a historically specific form of life as a universal discovery. The category develops recursively:

> inherited idea of intelligence → machine design → measured performance → revised idea of intelligence → new machine design

Its history must remain visible if we are to understand what is being claimed.

**The Forgotten Foundation: Language.** Language is often treated as one capability among several. But language may not merely express a previously completed intelligence. It may be one of the principal systems through which human intelligence is constructed, coordinated, preserved and extended. *Complex, cumulative, publicly examinable intelligence depends upon systems of symbolic registration.* This is the strong and defensible claim.

**Wittgenstein and the Limits of Language.** The Tractatus provides one boundary: the limits of language are limits upon the currently accessible symbolic world. The Philosophical Investigations direct attention away from fixed content inside isolated words toward language-games, practices and use. "To imagine a language is to imagine a form of life." A Geofinite framework can take this argument in a more explicitly dynamical and measurable direction.

**A Form of Life as a Dynamical System.** Within a Geofinite framework, a form of life is a dynamical system composed of: organisms; bodies and sensory systems; physical surroundings; repeated practices; instruments; institutions; memories; written records; material resources; social expectations; technologies; and established symbolic structures. Language is generated within this system but subsequently becomes an active part of it. The relation is recursive:

> interaction → distinction → registration → symbol → language → directed interaction

**Meaning as a Stabilised Trajectory.** A static account treats meaning as: M(w) = m. A dynamic account requires: M(w, t, H, C) — where the registered meaning of the word depends upon time *t*, inherited history *H*, and interactional context *C*. A word develops a basin of likely continuations. Meaning can therefore be approached as a historically constrained family of Functional Symbolic Trajectories. The phrase *artificial general intelligence* is itself such a trajectory, inheriting the semantic uncertainty of *artificial*, *general* and *intelligence* simultaneously.

**Semantic Thickness.** In Geofinitism, no finite symbol carries perfectly complete and universally identical meaning. A symbol has *semantic thickness*: the range of partially overlapping trajectories in which the symbol may participate while still being recognised as the same symbol. The thickness of *intelligence* is considerable, encompassing at least: understanding; rapid learning; memory; reasoning; linguistic fluency; adaptation; foresight; problem-solving; practical judgement; creativity; social coordination; test performance; and economic usefulness. Disagreement about whether a language model is intelligent may arise even when participants agree about the observed behaviour — they are applying different inherited trajectories of the word. A new definition is itself another symbolic registration; it may be useful but it does not erase the histories carried by existing use.

**Intelligence as Interaction and Conditional Registration.** The ordinary grammar of intelligence encourages us to treat it as a possession — a person *has* intelligence. But intelligence is not observed there; we observe conduct. A Geofinite reconstruction begins with the proposition: *Intelligence is initially accessible as a class of measured interactions, not as an independently measured substance possessed by an isolated entity.* A provisional expression:

> I_M(S, E, H, L, P)

where S is the system under consideration; E is the interactional environment; H is the accessible history of the system and measurement; L is the linguistic or symbolic framework; P is the procedure through which the judgement is produced. There is no unqualified *I* detached from these conditions. Intelligence becomes a profile of trajectories rather than a single metaphysical rank.

**Coupled Intelligence.** Human intellectual history has never been the achievement of isolated brains. People think with language, diagrams, notebooks, libraries, instruments, collaborators and institutions. Artificial intelligence makes this distributed structure more visible. In an exploratory human-machine conversation the intelligence-producing event occurs across the trajectory:

> human distinction → machine expansion → human selection → machine reconstruction → human judgement

No single stage contains the complete result. It may therefore be more accurate in some cases to speak of a *coupled intelligence* than to force the entire achievement into one bounded participant.

**Why AGI Requires a Model of Language.** Every public account of intelligence is created, measured and communicated through symbolic systems. A benchmark consists of instructions, categories, examples, outputs and scoring rules — all in language. Language is not an optional wrapper around the measurement; it is part of the measuring system.

> **A definition of AGI that does not include a model of language risks using inherited linguistic categories as though they were neutral components of the world.**

**The Problem of Inherited Training Language.** Large language models inherit not only factual information but the structure, assumptions, disputes and compressions of the language. They may reproduce psychometric assumptions, economic definitions, philosophical distinctions, marketing claims and fictional expectations without clearly separating their provenance. This is not accidental contamination; it is a consequence of learning through language. A system may become exceptionally capable at connecting inherited symbolic trajectories while having limited access to the original conditions that made those trajectories admissible.

**More Than a Larger Vocabulary.** An advanced symbolic system would need not just more words but the capacity to: (1) encounter a distinction not adequately represented; (2) recognise that its present language is insufficient; (3) construct or propose a new symbol or symbolic relation; (4) preserve the provenance of that construction; (5) test the new symbol against further interaction; (6) translate between old and new symbolic structures; (7) revise, restrict or retire symbols when they cease to remain admissible. This is *operational symbolic development*, not language generation.

**Language as Constraint and Enabler.** Constraint should not be understood only negatively: a violin string produces music because it is constrained. The problem is not that language constrains intelligence but that its constraints often become invisible. General intelligence may not be the ability to perform every existing human task. It may be the capacity to move between symbolic regimes, recognise the limitations of a current regime, and construct new trajectories without losing continuity with prior measurement.

**A Model of Language Before a Declaration of AGI.** A useful model of language for AGI would need to account for at least: finite symbolic registration; the formation of distinctions through interaction; semantic uncertainty; historical provenance; context-sensitive use; trajectory continuity; symbolic compression; correction and re-registration; the development of new symbols; and the conditions under which a symbolic claim remains admissible.

**Before We Declare AGI.** Four inadequate positions are identified: (a) that a machine is merely a tool may underestimate how an active symbolic instrument reshapes available trajectories; (b) that the machine independently possesses intelligence may remove the human, historical and material system through which its performance becomes possible; (c) that only human beings can be intelligent protects a category by definition rather than measurement; (d) that benchmark success establishes AGI may confuse a scoring procedure with the complete phenomenon. A Geofinite account expands the question: What interaction was observed? Which distinctions were registered? What history made the performance possible? What environment supported it? What language defined the task? Which compression produced the score? What uncertainty remains? How far does the resulting trajectory continue when conditions change?

The most advanced intelligence may not be the one that performs best within the inherited language-game. It may be the one capable of recognising when the game itself has become inadequate.
