## Overview

This lesson works through ATT_87's argument in four stages: (1) the history that produced the category "intelligence" and its compression into a measurable score; (2) Turing's substitution and what it revealed about the linguistic structure of machine intelligence; (3) the Geofinite account of language, meaning and intelligence as interactional registration; (4) what a model of language would need to contain before a declaration of AGI can be made responsibly.

The essay's central proposition: *Intelligence is initially accessible as a class of measured interactions, not as an independently measured substance possessed by an isolated entity.* The lesson develops what this means and why it places language at the centre of the AGI question rather than at the periphery.

## Section 1 — How "Intelligence" Became a Number

**Core question:** What historical trajectory produced the modern concept of intelligence, and what was compressed in the process?

**The pre-measurement plurality.** Ancient Greek thought distinguished at least five relevant terms: *nous* (intellect/apprehension), *logos* (reason/account), *phronesis* (practical judgement), *sophia* (wisdom), *techne* (skilled making). These words did not form a single measurable property. A person might be skilful in one activity and deficient in another without the difference being expressed as variation in a single general quantity. Judgement was inseparable from the situation in which it was demonstrated.

**The early modern turn.** Hobbes, Descartes and Leibniz contributed — differently — to the expectation that mental operations might be describable as ordered procedures. But this was the *formation* of a new symbolic language through which thought could be re-described, not the discovery of a previously hidden mechanical mind.

**The century of measurement.** Industrialisation required portable judgements. The Binet-Simon scale (1905) emerged from a practical educational need and compressed a many-layered interaction into a value:

> child → examiner → questions → language → scoring procedure

The score was attributed to the child alone. This compression was institutionally useful. A Geofinite account insists that the score retained — whether visibly or not — the history of its production. The measurement depended upon language comprehension, familiarity with the setting, the examiner's conduct and the design of the tasks. These were conditions of the measurement, not incidental additions to an otherwise pure intelligence.

Spearman's 1904 paper sought to measure "general intelligence" objectively and proposed a common factor *g*. He himself distinguished between the reliability of measurement and whether the measured form deserved the name *intelligence*. Over time that distinction became compressed. The intelligence score exemplifies:

> complex interaction → selected observations → symbolic compression → portable value

**Why the compression matters for AI.** When human intelligence has already been converted into performance across selected tasks, a machine that performs well on similar tasks may appear to possess the same underlying property. However, it may instead have entered the same measurement arrangement. The machine is not necessarily revealing a hidden human-like quantity. It is producing outputs that satisfy inherited criteria.

## Section 2 — Turing's Elegant Substitution and the Linguistic Structure of Machine Intelligence

**Core question:** What did Turing's 1950 paper actually do, and what does it reveal about the role of language in measuring machine intelligence?

**The substitution.** Turing began with "Can machines think?" He immediately recognised that defining *machine* and *think* by ordinary usage would create serious difficulty. Rather than settling both terms, he replaced the original question with a more precisely arranged interaction: the imitation game. This move was elegant because it avoided dependence upon an inaccessible inner condition. Instead of opening the machine and identifying a substance called thought, he proposed a finite communicative procedure.

The question changed from:
> Can a machine think?

to something closer to:
> Under specified linguistic conditions, can an evaluator reliably distinguish the machine's responses from those of a human participant?

This is compatible with an important Geofinite principle: when a term cannot be measured directly, construct a finite interaction and state what is actually registered. The imitation game functions as a replacement for an unstable question, not as the discovery of a final substance.

**What the imitation game contained.** The test did not stand outside language and then measure intelligence neutrally. Language was the medium of the test, the material of the responses and part of the evaluator's criterion. At least three interacting models participated:
1. the machine's model of linguistic continuation;
2. the human participant's use of language;
3. the evaluator's model of human and machine behaviour.

**What later culture did.** The original paper's careful reframing became a cultural marker: "the Turing test." Passing came to be treated as a possible threshold between machine performance and machine intelligence. A trajectory was compressed. Turing's exploratory substitution became a metaphysical verdict.

**The insight this exposes.** The modern discussion of machine intelligence was linguistic from the beginning. This is not a limitation of Turing's proposal but its most important revealing feature: machine intelligence is accessible to judgement *through language*. A model of language is therefore not a secondary concern to add after intelligence is defined. It is part of the structure of the question.

**The Dartmouth conjecture.** The 1955 proposal by McCarthy, Minsky, Rochester and Shannon rested on a bold conjecture: that every aspect of intelligence could in principle be described precisely enough for a machine to simulate it. Each step depended upon language: dividing intelligence into aspects required names and boundaries; describing an aspect precisely required a symbolic system; determining whether a machine simulates it required criteria of correspondence. The technical programme began inside a prior linguistic programme.

## Section 3 — Language, Form of Life, and Intelligence as Interaction

**Core question:** What is the Geofinite account of language, meaning and intelligence?

**A form of life as a dynamical system.** Wittgenstein's phrase "form of life" is developed in a Geofinite direction. A form of life is not merely speakers and words; it is a dynamical system composed of: organisms; bodies and sensory systems; physical surroundings; repeated practices; instruments; institutions; memories; written records; material resources; social expectations; technologies; and established symbolic structures.

Language is generated within this system and then acts back upon it. The relation is recursive:

> interaction → distinction → registration → symbol → language → directed interaction

A person taught the word *intelligence* begins to classify conduct through that word. A researcher designs an intelligence test. A company advertises intelligent software. The inherited symbol changes which events become salient — producing another loop:

> measurement → language → model → expected measurement → further language

**Meaning as a stabilised trajectory.** A static account treats meaning as M(w) = m: word *w* points reliably to stable item *m*. A dynamic account requires M(w, t, H, C), where the registered meaning depends upon time *t*, inherited history *H*, and interactional context *C*. Stability exists through repeated trajectories, not through a fixed content inside an isolated word.

A word is taught within examples, attached to practices, corrected when misused, preserved in documents, enforced by institutions. The word develops a *basin of likely continuations*. Meaning can therefore be approached as a historically constrained family of Functional Symbolic Trajectories.

**Semantic thickness.** In Geofinitism, no symbol carries perfectly complete and universally identical meaning. A symbol has *semantic thickness*: the range of partially overlapping trajectories in which it may participate while still being recognised as the same symbol. The thickness of *intelligence* is considerable, encompassing at least: understanding; rapid learning; memory; reasoning; linguistic fluency; adaptation; foresight; problem-solving; practical judgement; creativity; social coordination; test performance; and economic usefulness. Different users activate different portions. Disagreement about whether a language model is intelligent may arise even when participants agree about the observed behaviour — they are applying different inherited trajectories of the same word.

**Intelligence as interaction — the formal proposition.** The ordinary grammar of *has intelligence* directs attention inward, looking for the property inside the bounded object. But we observe conduct. A Geofinite reconstruction begins with:

> Intelligence is initially accessible as a class of measured interactions, not as an independently measured substance possessed by an isolated entity.

A provisional formal expression: **I_M(S, E, H, L, P)**, where:
- S = system under consideration
- E = interactional environment
- H = accessible history of system and measurement
- L = linguistic or symbolic framework
- P = procedure through which the judgement is produced

There is no unqualified *I* detached from these conditions. Change the environment and measured performance may change. Change the language and the task may change. Remove the history and an adaptive response may appear inexplicable. Intelligence becomes a profile of trajectories rather than a single metaphysical rank.

**Coupled intelligence.** Human intellectual history has never been the achievement of isolated brains. People think with language, diagrams, notebooks, libraries, instruments, collaborators and institutions. Artificial intelligence makes this distributed structure more visible. In an exploratory human-machine conversation:

> human distinction → machine expansion → human selection → machine reconstruction → human judgement

No single stage contains the complete result. It may therefore be more accurate in some cases to speak of a *coupled intelligence* than to force the entire achievement into one bounded participant. This does not require the machine to be declared conscious, alive or human. It requires only that we measure the trajectory where it occurs.

## Section 4 — What a Model of Language Must Contain Before AGI Can Be Declared

**Core question:** What does the essay argue is still missing, and what would a sufficient model of language look like?

**Why language is not optional.** Every public account of intelligence is created, measured and communicated through symbolic systems. A benchmark consists of instructions, categories, examples, outputs and scoring rules — all in language. A domain is separated from other domains through names and institutional practices. A correct answer is identified by symbolic criteria. Generality is judged by comparing performance across these constructed divisions. Language is therefore not an optional wrapper around the measurement. It is part of the measuring system. A definition of AGI that does not include a model of language risks using inherited linguistic categories as though they were neutral components of the world.

**The same argument applies to mathematics.** Mathematical practice depends upon finite symbols, definitions, diagrams, rules, inscriptions and procedures. A symbol such as π is not self-explanatory. Its use is taught and stabilised through language and practice. The formal expression may compress the explanatory trajectory, but it does not abolish it.

**The problem of inherited training language.** Large language models are trained on vast collections of human-produced text. They inherit not only factual information but the structure, assumptions, disputes and compressions of the language. A model trained on historical and contemporary discourse about intelligence learns the trajectories humans have already established — psychometric assumptions, economic definitions, philosophical distinctions, marketing claims and fictional expectations — without clearly separating their provenance. This is not accidental contamination. It is a consequence of learning through language. The training corpus acts as a compressed historical environment containing traces of human activity but not the full exogenous interactions from which every statement arose.

A system capable of revising language must be able to compare inherited symbols against new registrations. It may know what has been said without being able to remeasure why it was said.

**Language as constraint and enabler.** Constraint is not only negative. A violin string produces music because it is constrained. A channel permits directed flow because it excludes other paths. A symbolic system enables cumulative thought precisely because it stabilises some relations. Without constraint, there is no repeatable trajectory. The problem is that its constraints often become invisible. An advanced intelligence would need to operate within symbolic constraints while also being able to inspect and revise them. General intelligence may be the capacity to move between symbolic regimes, recognise the limitations of a current regime, and construct new trajectories without losing continuity with prior measurement.

**What a model of language would need to account for.** The essay specifies at minimum: finite symbolic registration; the formation of distinctions through interaction; semantic uncertainty; historical provenance; context-sensitive use; trajectory continuity; symbolic compression; correction and re-registration; the development of new symbols; and the conditions under which a symbolic claim remains admissible. An advanced artificial system that participates in operational symbolic development — not merely language generation — would need measurable control over the histories and conditions of its symbols.

**The dual question.** Before we declare AGI, we should identify the language through which generality has been constructed; distinguish task breadth from symbolic adaptability; distinguish autonomous action from independent measurement; and distinguish linguistic fluency from grounded revision. The question is not only:

> Can a machine become generally intelligent?

It is also:

> What dynamical system produces the judgement of intelligence, and what language makes that judgement possible?

The most advanced intelligence may not be the one that performs best within the inherited language-game. It may be the one capable of recognising when the game itself has become inadequate. Until this prior question is addressed, AGI will remain both an engineering ambition and an unstable symbol. That does not make the ambition meaningless. It means that the work has only just begun.
