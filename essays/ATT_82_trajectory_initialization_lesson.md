## Purpose

ATT_82 is the shortest and most operationally direct essay in the FSM series. It draws a single, clear prescriptive consequence from the FST framework: declare the frame before motion begins. The lesson focuses on making this discipline practical — first through careful reading, then through exercises that force you to apply it.

## Part I — Before You Read

Read ATT_81 first if you have not already. ATT_82 begins precisely where ATT_81 ends: ATT_81 described what a functional symbolic trajectory is; ATT_82 says what you must therefore do before launching one.

Hold one question as you read: What is the difference between a weak frame and no frame? Is underdeclared worse than undeclared?

## Part II — Reading Guide

### The Opening Move

The essay opens with the ATT_81 definition quoted back to the reader, then pivots with a single word: *prescriptive*. This is the essay's structure in miniature — descriptive theory yields operational consequence. Before you continue, make sure the definition is live in your mind: an FST is a finite, stabilised pathway carrying representation, constraint, local uncertainty, and history through use. It is not a label; it is a dynamic object.

### Symbolic Mind-Reading

Read the "symbolic mind-reading" passage carefully. The argument is that in the absence of an explicit frame, the receiver — any receiver — must fill in the missing scaffolding by assumption. The four things that must be assumed:
1. The precise definition of "solution" (what stabilisation means)
2. The measurement regime (what resolution and scale apply)
3. The admissible uncertainty (what δ the speaker is accepting)
4. The historical provenance (what has been compressed or omitted)

Ask: in a typical conversation you had today, how many of these were explicitly declared? How many were supplied by assumed shared context? What happens when that assumed context differs between speaker and receiver?

### The Tuple

Study **(C, α, H, δ)** until you can define each component without looking:

- **C** = admissibility constraint (what counts as valid here)
- **α** = Alphonic limit (resolution/scale of symbol stabilisation)
- **H** = historical provenance (what has been compressed/omitted)
- **δ** = declared uncertainty (neighbourhood of possible reconstructions)

Notice: this is the same tuple that structures the FST definition in ATT_81. Initialisation is not imposing an external framework; it is making explicit the structure that every FST already presupposes implicitly.

### The Three Levels

Read the three levels of protection carefully and note their scope:

1. **Trajectory integrity** — protects the argument or communication *itself* from drifting undetected
2. **Receiver protection** — protects the human or AI on the receiving end from having to guess
3. **Genuine progress** — protects the possibility of solving hard problems that have been kept artificially hard by underdeclared framing

The third is the most philosophically ambitious. The P vs. NP example is precisely chosen: not a random hard problem, but one where the Geofinitist argument (P19) has already argued that the classical formulation lacks an axiom of finite measurement. ATT_82's initialisation discipline would have been the tool that made that missing axiom visible.

### The LLM Application

Read the LLM passage twice. The claim is precise and testable: current LLMs are fluency-optimised *within* the supplied frame. If the frame is weak, the model fills it by inference from training distribution — which is exactly mind-reading at scale. The output looks complete because the model is excellent at generating fluent, consistent responses. But the consistency is internal to the underdeclared frame, not to the problem you actually intended.

The proposed discipline: before proceeding, the model should surface its initialisation and ask for confirmation. This is the Geofinitist AI interaction protocol.

## Part III — Exercises

**Exercise 1 — Apply the tuple.**
Take a question or task you are currently working on — any domain. Write out the initialisation **(C, α, H, δ)** for it explicitly. C: what conditions would count as a satisfactory answer? α: at what level of resolution are you working (formal proof? rough estimate? conceptual sketch)? H: what prior work are you assuming and what are you deliberately setting aside? δ: what is the acceptable error margin or range of valid answers?

This exercise is harder than it sounds. Most of us have strong intuitions about C and weak intuitions about α, H, and δ. Note where you struggle.

**Exercise 2 — Diagnose a failure.**
Recall a recent miscommunication — in conversation, email, or a piece of writing that was misread. Identify which component of **(C, α, H, δ)** was underdeclared. Was C unclear (people disagreed about what "success" meant)? Was α mismatched (one party was working at a different resolution)? Was H missing (assumed shared context that wasn't shared)? Was δ implicit (one party expected precision the other didn't intend to provide)?

**Exercise 3 — LLM test.**
Choose a non-trivial question and submit it to any LLM *without* declaring the frame. Note the response. Then re-submit with an explicit **(C, α, H, δ)** preamble — state what counts as a valid answer, at what level of detail, what background you're assuming, and what uncertainty is acceptable. Compare the two responses. What changed? Was the second response better? In what specific way?

**Exercise 4 — P vs. NP connection.**
ATT_82 claims that P vs. NP has remained unresolved partly because the initial framing has been allowed to remain partially undeclared. Read P19 (*P vs. NP and the Missing Axiom of Finite Measurement*) for the specific argument. Then write one paragraph connecting the missing-axiom diagnosis in P19 to the initialisation-tuple prescription in ATT_82. What would an explicitly initialised formulation of P vs. NP look like?

**Exercise 5 — Self-application.**
ATT_82 closes by claiming the essays in its volume are themselves written under explicit initialisation. Apply the **(C, α, H, δ)** analysis to ATT_82 itself:
- **C**: what counts as a successful reading of this essay?
- **α**: at what resolution is the argument pitched?
- **H**: what prior essays is this assuming you have read?
- **δ**: what uncertainty remains in the claims made?

Is ATT_82 itself fully initialised? Where, if anywhere, does it rely on the reader's mind-reading?

## Part IV — Key Points to Retain

1. **The safety layer is not optional** once FSTs are recognised as the actual medium of thought and communication. The refusal to initialise is a choice to rely on mind-reading.

2. **The initialisation tuple is (C, α, H, δ)** — admissibility constraint, Alphonic resolution, historical provenance, declared uncertainty. These four components structure every FST implicitly; initialisation makes them explicit.

3. **The failure mode** is not silence but fluency: uninitialised trajectories feel coherent precisely because the receiver fills in the missing scaffolding from context. The error is invisible.

4. **The LLM case** is not metaphorical — it is the most practically urgent instance. Models are trained to be fluent within supplied frames; they will generate complete-feeling responses on underdeclared frames, inheriting every hidden assumption.

5. **Initialisation connects to progress**: the hardest open problems are often hard because their framing contains undeclared measurement commitments. Explicit initialisation is the tool for making those commitments visible.
