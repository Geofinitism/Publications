# ATT_91 — On the Greene-Pascal Limit

**Running Header:** The Greene-Pascal Limit
**Pages:** 24
**Status:** Stable

---

## Core Claim

The sayable has both a floor and a ceiling region. The floor is the Alphonic Limit — the smallest difference registrable as non-single under a given apparatus. The ceiling region is the Greene-Pascal Limit: the zone where a string of symbols first coheres into a Functional Symbolic Trajectory (FST) that can be held by the Dynamic Semantic Lattice (DSL). This is not a wall but a region defined by a condition — coherence — and coherence requires wider language to hold it. The paper is a placement, not a proof: its purpose is to name the region and to make a path for others to find.

---

## Overview

Two historical figures touched the Greene-Pascal Limit from opposite sides without naming it. Robert Greene, a fellow of Clare College Cambridge writing in 1712 and 1727, encountered the physical ceiling: the words of Newtonian natural philosophy did not touch the observed world — matter and void, however carefully defined, did not reach the gold bar that fractures the bone. Blaise Pascal, writing in France in the 1650s, encountered the symbolic ceiling: looking at the two infinities (the vast heavens and the infinitely small), he saw that the symbol could continue but the person doing the dividing remained finite. Greene at the physical ceiling; Pascal at the symbolic ceiling. Both touched the region. Both were left behind by the trajectory that won.

The paper argues further that Gödel (in his 1951 Gibbs Lecture at Brown) and Wittgenstein (working through Gödel's construction in 1938 at Trinity College Cambridge) were each circling the same region without naming it. It documents several historical crossings that were productive but then forgotten: Wallis's introduction of the ∞ symbol (1655), Euler's *Introductio in analysin infinitorum* (1748), and the redefinition of the metre through krypton-86 wavelength (1960) and then light-speed fraction (1983). In each case the region was crossed and the crossing made invisible — the symbol continued, the underlying trajectory compressed away.

The essay closes by noting that modern tools — compression algorithms, signal theory, non-linear embedding, large language models — have made the Greene-Pascal region operational in a way it never was before. LLM hallucination is reread as a trajectory error: the symbolic path remains locally smooth while losing contact with the intended constraint structure.

## Structure and Key Argument

**Two Trajectories** traces Greene's objection (division without end never reaches the world; the words were not touching the observed world) and Pascal's encounter with two infinities (the vast and the small; the symbol for infinity can be written and manipulated, but the infinite itself cannot be measured). Greene at the physical ceiling: the words do not touch the thing. Pascal at the symbolic ceiling: the symbol can continue, but the person cannot follow.

**The floor and the region** introduces the formal distinction. The Alphonic Limit is the floor of the sayable — the smallest difference that can enter a symbolic trajectory, dependent on apparatus and conditions. The Greene-Pascal Limit is not the ceiling above the floor but the region of coherence — where a string of symbols begins to cohere, where it can be placed into the Dynamic Semantic Lattice and held as a Functional Symbolic Trajectory. Below the region: no hold. Above the region: a symbol that has lost too much of its chain for the lattice to reconstruct coherence (locally fluent, grammatically well-formed, but the wider language can no longer carry it). The crucial point is stated: **the coherence needs wider language to hold it.** The limit is always conditioned by the lattice, the licence, and the provenance.

**Gödel and Wittgenstein at the region** reads the 1951 Gibbs Lecture carefully. Gödel offers a disjunction (either the evident axioms can never be comprised in a finite rule, or there exist absolutely unsolvable Diophantine problems) and declines to say which holds. He warns his audience not to expect mathematical rigour of the philosophical inferences. He says he is "not in a position now" to discuss whether the meaning of a proposition about all integers consists only in the existence of a general proof. That reservation, the paper argues, is exactly the reservation of a man at the Greene-Pascal region. Wittgenstein in 1938 asks where the proof of non-provability is — it is in the metatheory, conditional on consistency, and the system cannot discharge that condition. Both are asking what the words *provable*, *true*, *all*, *mind*, *machine*, *objective*, *infinity* can carry; and the answer is: they sit at the region, asking for wider language that the lattice does not yet hold.

**Crossings that were forgotten** documents three cases: Wallis's ∞ (1655) — an act of ordination, a licence to write the unbounded as though it were a thing; Euler's *Introductio* (1748) which sealed F = ma and let every variable be zero and every symbol lack extent; the metre redefined (1960, 1983) so that the number was chosen as an integer by the authority of experts, and any prior measurement uncertainty was eradicated. In each case the crossing was productive. The danger is not that the symbol was false. The danger is that the symbol became the only story we can see.

**The region made operational** reads the LLM as a compression: the training corpus reduced to a file you can carry. The model holds the shape of what it has compressed, and the shape is astonishingly good — but the corpus is gone. Hallucination is a trajectory error: the symbolic path remains locally smooth while the constraint structure has already slipped. The wider language (the prompt, the context, the training distribution) can no longer hold the trajectory into coherence.

**The region and the ground** states the deeper point: the gap between the symbol and what it compresses is not a failure. It is the condition of the symbol existing at all. If there were no gap, there would be no symbol. The gap is generative — it is why there is anything to measure. The Greene-Pascal region is where the gap becomes visible.
