## Overview

This lesson works through P26's argument in three stages: (1) the history that produced the integer 299 792 458; (2) the 1983 reversal and what it actually changed; (3) the structural consequences for physics and the language of measurement.

The paper's central claim is precise: **Measurement produced the value. The Committee set the certainty.** The lesson develops what this distinction means and why it matters for the Geofinite framework.

## Section 1 — Before 1983: The Ordinary Direction of Empirical Correction

**Core question:** What was the status of the numerical value of *c* before 1983, and how did the metre and second come to carry it?

**The pre-1983 direction:**

> interaction → measurement → number with uncertainty → possible revision

This is the ordinary direction of science. A measurement produces a number that remains downstream of the observation. A better experiment could, in principle, require the number to change.

**The metre's material trajectory.** The 1889 platinum-iridium prototype had finite physical extent. Temperature, engraved lines, material stability, optical comparison, and the condition of the artefact all entered the measurement. Every transfer from the prototype to another standard carried a chain of comparison and uncertainty. Nothing in the material event offered an infinitely exact metre.

**Two definitions that transferred material standards into counts:**
- 1960: 1 metre = 1 650 763.73 wavelengths of krypton-86 radiation. The metre came first; the count was selected to preserve the existing metre as closely as available measurements permitted.
- 1967: 1 second = 9 192 631 770 periods of caesium-133 radiation. Again, an inherited unit was fitted to a count so the new realisation would preserve the second already in use.

**The 1975 recommended value.** By the early 1970s, optical frequency (carrying the second) could be connected to optical wavelength (carrying the metre), allowing c = λf to be formed. The 15th CGPM (1975) recommended c = 299 792 458 m s⁻¹ with an estimated relative uncertainty of ±4×10⁻⁹. The direction of reference was still explicit:

> realised metre + realised second → measured value of c

The uncertainty disclosed the status of the number. It told us that the symbolic result had been produced through finite comparison and remained open to another interaction with the world. The measurement could still answer back.

## Section 2 — 1983: The Reversal

**Core question:** What exactly changed in 1983, and what did not?

**The decision.** The 17th CGPM (1983) adopted a new definition of the metre: the length of the path travelled by light in vacuum during 1/299 792 458 of a second. The Committee's own resolution records the reasoning: measurements of the speed of light had become limited principally by the realisation of the existing metre. Setting the integer as exact would produce no appreciable discontinuity in the unit of length.

**What the Committee did not do.** It did not perform an experiment that eliminated the remaining uncertainty. It set the number so that uncertainty could no longer attach to it.

The transition in two sentences:

> Measurement produced the value.  
> The Committee set the certainty.

**Before and after the reversal:**

| Before 1983 | After 1983 |
|-------------|------------|
| 299 792 458 m s⁻¹ is a measured recommendation capable of revision | 299 792 458 m s⁻¹ is an exact defining relation |
| The number is downstream of measurement | The number is upstream — it governs measurement |
| Uncertainty stands above the numerical value | Certainty is placed in the numerical relation |
| A better experiment could change the number | No SI experiment can change the number |
| Discrepancies require revision of the value | Discrepancies must be assigned to realisation, apparatus, pathway, or theory |

**The Figure 1 trajectory:**

| 1889 | Material bar represents the metre |
| 1960 | Krypton count set to preserve that metre |
| 1975 | 299 792 458 m s⁻¹ recommended with ±4×10⁻⁹ |
| **1983** | **Committee sets the same integer as exact** |
| — | Direction of authority reverses |
| — | Exact number and second now define the metre |
| — | All later uncertainty judged against the set number |

**Why "set" and not "fixed".** Official metrological language usually says the value was *fixed*. *Fixed* sounds passive and technical, as though a loose part had been secured. *Set* retains the actor, the action, and the resulting state: a committee sets a value; a clock is set against a reference; something set in stone is removed from ordinary revision. Exactness here is the consequence of setting, not the consequence of measurement reaching perfection.

## Section 3 — Structural Consequences

**Core question:** What follows, structurally, from placing an exact number inside a measuring system?

**Where the uncertainty went.** The physical world did not become exact in 1983. Laboratories still had to realise the second, generate and stabilise radiation, establish a path, account for environmental conditions, operate instruments, and retain comparisons. Every interaction remained finite. What changed was where uncertainty was *permitted to appear*.

The new hierarchy:

> c ≡ 299 792 458 m s⁻¹ exactly  
> ↓  
> all material realisations, instruments and observations carry uncertainty

Uncertainty did not disappear. It was expelled from the defining number and redistributed across the measured world.

**A God-like number inside the system.** The defining integer is protected from empirical correction. It is declared exact. It supplies a reference against which every admissible SI measurement of length must be made coherent with it. This is not a claim about worship or agency; it is a claim about *the geometry of authority*. The defining integer is placed beyond the reach of the measurements it governs. If a conflict appears, the surrounding account must change while the central relation remains untouched.

Before 1983, there was no certainty of this kind in the measured value. There was only a highly refined estimate with a stated uncertainty. The Committee created a perfect number inside the symbolic system and then made the system refer material uncertainty to that perfection.

**The responsible statement.** The familiar sentence "the speed of light is exactly 299 792 458 m s⁻¹" collapses two forms of authority: administrative authority over the measuring language, and authority over nature. The paper proposes the full provenance-bearing claim:

> *Within the International System of Units, the numerical value of the speed of light in vacuum was set by resolution of the General Conference on Weights and Measures at exactly 299 792 458 m s⁻¹. The value was selected to preserve continuity with the previously realised metre and cannot be revised by further SI measurement while the present definitions remain in force.*

This sentence is longer because the history is part of the meaning.

**Closure and the direction of later physics.** When foundational symbols are exact and protected:

> set symbolic foundations → observation does not fit → additional entity, correction or framework → foundations remain set

Dark matter is a structural illustration: observed galactic motions do not agree with the established description when only registered matter is included, so an additional entity is inferred to preserve the larger framework. The setting of c does not cause this, but it exemplifies the geometry. Physics can accumulate paradoxes, invisible entities, and increasingly elaborate models while continuing to describe its starting relations as certainties. A framework whose foundational symbols cannot answer to measurement may be extraordinarily productive, but it also narrows the routes through which an anomaly can return to those foundations.

**The bar's compressed trajectory.** The old platinum-iridium bar has not vanished from this story. Its registered extent passed into the krypton count, from there into the measured numerical value of c, and from there into the exact integer through which the metre is now defined. The bar disappeared from the final sentence, but its trajectory remains compressed within the number. This is FSM compression: the measurement trajectory is carried forward in the symbol, even when the symbol no longer displays it.
