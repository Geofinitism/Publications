# M26 — Finite Symbolic Dynamics: Lesson

**Monograph:** M26 — *Finite Symbolic Dynamics: Function, Resolution, and the Terminus of Symbolic Growth*  
**Running title:** Finite Symbolic Dynamics  
**Author:** Kevin R. Haylett  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Lesson purpose:** To bring the reader into the operating practice of Finite Symbolic Dynamics — working through the conditions under which a symbolic trajectory earns its growth, the two distinct termini that govern stopping, the discipline of numerical representation as a test case, and the constraints of functional economy that govern the grammar itself.

## Section I: Functional Trajectories and Their Termination

A Functional Symbolic Trajectory is not merely a finite sequence of states. It is a finite historical pathway that performs a declared operation under identifiable conditions. Its growth is justified by function. Its local stopping condition is functional sufficiency — not the exhaustion of possible distinctions. This section works through what that means and what it demands of symbolic practice.

**Exercise 1.** The monograph opens with the central question: "What makes a symbolic trajectory functional, and what permits it to stop growing?" Write a careful preliminary answer to this question using only the vocabulary of Part I (no appeal to the numerical or grammatical sections yet). Your answer should address: (a) what distinguishes a functional trajectory from a merely finite sequence; (b) what the declared operation of a trajectory contributes to its identity; (c) what the criterion "Δ(symbolic structure) >> Δ(functional gain)" is measuring; and (d) what it would mean for the growth of a descriptive FST to be treated as itself a dynamical observable.

**Exercise 2.** The Container FST (Γ_C = {S_0, T_1, S_1, ..., T_n, S_n}) is proposed as a way of representing a research programme as an accumulating trajectory. A derived FST D_j = C(Γ_C^[a:b]) compresses a window of this container; crucially D_j ≠ Γ_C^[a:b]. (a) Explain the significance of D_j ≠ Γ_C^[a:b] — what is preserved by the derived FST that would be lost if they were treated as identical? (b) Consider a case where a researcher writes a summary of three earlier monographs to prepare the next one. Map this onto the Container FST framework: what is S_0, what are T_i, what is D_j, and in what sense does D_j become an input to Γ_{j+1}? (c) What would it mean for a Container FST to enter a recurrent structure, and is that a failure condition or a legitimate dynamical event?

**Exercise 3.** The monograph establishes that |Γ_2| > |Γ_1| does NOT imply F(Γ_2) > F(Γ_1) — a larger trajectory is not automatically more functionally adequate. (a) Construct a specific example (from any domain: physics, language, computation, or mathematics) where adding symbolic structure to a model reduces its functional adequacy by obscuring the relations that performed the necessary work. (b) The monograph treats functional adequacy as a relation M ⟺^Γ R that is trajectory-mediated. Explain why the trajectory label is attached to the relation arrow rather than to either M or R. (c) A model is updated: M_t →^Γ M̂_{t+1}, and then M̂_{t+1} is compared to M_{t+1}. What does this comparison test, and what does it require to be declared in advance?

**Exercise 4.** The monograph distinguishes the Alphonic terminus from the functional terminus. The Alphonic terminus marks where no finer admissible distinction is available; the functional terminus marks where further elaboration exceeds present functional need. (a) Construct a thought experiment in which a researcher reaches the Alphonic terminus of their measuring instrument before reaching the functional terminus of their model. What follows? (b) Construct a thought experiment in which the functional terminus is reached well before the Alphonic terminus. What follows? (c) The functional terminus is described as elastic: it can be reopened by new measurement, failed function, or changed operating conditions. Design a specific scenario in which each of these three reopening conditions applies, and trace how the trajectory would proceed in each case.

**Exercise 5.** The Epicycle Problem is described as what happens when local additions to a framework improve local description while straining global structural coherence. The appropriate response is to inspect the generating commitments rather than add another partition. (a) Identify a historical case in any scientific or mathematical domain where epicyclic proliferation of this kind occurred. Trace the additions and explain what generating commitment, when reconsidered, resolved or reframed the problem. (b) The monograph says "complexity growth is diagnostic." What does it diagnose, and what action does it recommend? (c) Apply this to the FSM/FSD framework itself: what would count as a warning sign that the framework has entered epicyclic proliferation, and what procedure does the monograph propose to address this?

## Section II: Finite Number Representation as a Functional Test Case

The numerical chapters are not a digression. They constitute a deliberate stress test of the FSD framework: a domain where the physical instantiation is visible (beans, ink marks, abacus beads, electronic registers), the operations are explicit (count, carry, shift), and the failure modes of infinite extension are well-defined. The question is whether the FSD framework can describe arithmetic without appeal to infinite numerical precision or ungrounded abstraction.

**Exercise 6.** Chapter 8 establishes that a finite positional register is fully characterised by R_{b,m,e} = (d_0,...,d_{m-1};b,m,e), and that numerical resolution = f(base, character depth, scale). The base alone does not determine the resolution. (a) Explain why a base-10 numeral with two significant digit positions, a base-10 numeral with nine positions, and a base-10 numeral with one hundred positions are *not* members of the same resolution class, even though they share the same base. (b) The monograph rejects the equivalence: "fixed base + indefinite character extension ≡ finite measured number." Explain what is wrong with this equivalence from the FSM perspective. (c) A researcher reports a measurement as 3.14159265 × 10^2. What does the FSD framework require to be declared alongside this number for it to constitute a full finite symbolic registration?

**Exercise 7.** Chapter 10 treats arithmetic as an operational symbolic trajectory. The successor operation, carry, and shift are explicit state transitions rather than invisible background operations. (a) Write the full FST for the addition of 9,999,999,999 + 1 in a base-10 ten-character register: show each state N_i and each transition T_i, including the carry chain and scale shift. (b) Chapter 10.3 shows that the same FST can be instantiated through ink marks, abacus beads, beans, and electronic registers. What is preserved across these instantiations, and what is not? What does FSD call the preserved element? (c) The monograph states that "the result is not assumed to exist first as an infinitely resolved number that the register later approximates. The admitted object of analysis is the finite transition itself." What is the consequence of this for how a computed result should be reported and used downstream?

**Exercise 8.** Chapter 11 introduces *symbolic collision* (Π_{m'→m}(x) = Π_{m'→m}(y)) and the foundational rule that "symbolically generated character depth does not retroactively increase measurement depth." (a) Describe a specific case in experimental physics where a calculation produces additional significant figures beyond what the original measurement supports, and these additional figures are then reported as if measurement-supported. Explain what the FSD framework says is happening and what should be done instead. (b) A scale shift in a nine-character register changes the least available distinction from 10^{e-8} to 10^{e-7}. Construct a scenario where this change matters for a scientific conclusion — where the loss of one order of resolution at the base-level distinguishing scale changes the interpretation of a result. (c) The residue of a symbolic collision ρ_{m,m'} = 𝒱(R_{b,m',e}) − 𝒱(Π_{m'→m} R_{b,m',e}) is described as "finite and depending on the declared comparison depth m'." Why does making this residue explicit matter for the FSD account? What does standard numerical analysis do with it instead?

**Exercise 9.** Chapter 9 introduces the "numerical Alphon" as a provisional term for an admissible digit-state at one positional character space, and notes that the term *may* earn stability if applications show it performs useful repeated work. The chapter also leaves open whether a full "numerical Nexil" notation earns its place. (a) Explain the FSD criterion by which a new term earns terminological stability. Why is demonstrating that a concept is meaningful not sufficient? (b) Apply this criterion to the "numerical Alphon" as introduced: at the point of introduction, has it earned stability? What would its eventual stability or elimination depend on? (c) The Nexil is defined as a first-order finite symbolic registration generated from measurement at the relevant Alphonic context. Why is it important that the Nexil should not automatically be assigned to every downstream computational state? What would be lost if it were?

**Exercise 10.** Chapter 12 establishes that a deterministic finite symbolic system must eventually terminate, revisit a previous state, or enter a recurrent structure — and that within FSD, recurrence is a property of the symbolic dynamics to be characterised rather than dismissed as a finite-precision artefact. (a) The Local Dynexil bundle D_t^{(k,τ)} = [N_t, N_{t-τ}, ..., N_{t-kτ}]_{U,P} is applied to a numerical FST to examine recurrence, local drift, symbolic collisions, and sensitivity to finite perturbation. Design a specific computational experiment that uses this tool to test whether a particular arithmetic trajectory is recurrent or drifting, and describe what would count as a positive and negative result. (b) The monograph raises whether "the same local descriptor remains useful across numerical, linguistic, quantum, and fluid trajectories." What would it mean for the Dynexil to *fail* this cross-domain portability test, and what action would that require?

## Section III: Functional Economy and the Grammar of FSD

Part III turns the lens of FSD back onto itself. The question of when a trajectory may stop growing applies not only to physical or numerical trajectories but to the symbolic framework under development. The grammar of FSD must itself satisfy the functional economy it recommends: terms must perform work, vocabulary is a dynamical resource, and increasing elaboration must be justified by demonstrated function.

**Exercise 11.** The monograph defines the *elastic functional region* between over-compression and over-elaboration: a term in the over-compressed region replaces a trajectory with a noun that no longer carries distinctions needed for function; a term in the over-elaborated region preserves or invents more distinctions than the declared function requires. (a) Give an example of over-compression in FSM/FSD terminology — a term or notation that performs so much compression that it hides distinctions required for correct use. Explain what distinction it hides and what would need to be restored. (b) Give an example of over-elaboration — a possible extension of FSM/FSD terminology that would introduce more distinctions than any presently declared function requires. Explain why introducing it now would be premature under the functional economy constraint. (c) How does the location of the "functional region" differ across problems, measurements, and operating conditions? What does this mean for any attempt to establish a permanent terminological standard?

**Exercise 12.** Chapter 14 proposes the provisional functional grammar: register→relate→transform→retain provenance→test function→stop or continue. This sequence "does not replace the existing terminology. It constrains it." (a) Apply this sequence to a specific FSM/FSD concept — for example, the Nexil, the Dynexil, or the Alphonic terminus — and show how the sequence constrains how that concept may be used in a valid FST description. (b) The minimal vocabulary includes the Dynexil defined as "a bounded local dynamical projection or delay-structured descriptor. Its role is to reopen local trajectory structure that a static state representation may compress." Explain why this definition places the Dynexil downstream of the measurement event and forbids treating it as an update rule. (c) The list of minimal vocabulary terms is described as a working grammar, not a protected ontology, and terms may be merged, removed, or restricted. What would constitute sufficient evidence to merge the Nexil and the Dynexil into a single term? What would constitute sufficient evidence to remove the Dynexil from the minimal vocabulary?

**Exercise 13.** Chapter 15 introduces *functional stability across transformations*: a relational feature K is more stable when K(Γ^(1)) ~ K(Γ^(2)) ~ ... ~ K(Γ^(q)) across admissible representations. The resolution sensitivity measure S_R(m) = d(Γ_m, Γ_{m+1}) tracks how much a trajectory feature changes as character depth varies. (a) Design a specific test for resolution sensitivity in a numerical trajectory (for example, the iteration of a nonlinear map at character depths m=6,7,8,9). What would high and low resolution sensitivity indicate, respectively, about the macroscopic structure of the trajectory? (b) The First Registered Distinction Time T_D = min{t : ℛ(X_t) ≠ ℛ(Y_t)} is introduced as a finite alternative to infinitesimal Lyapunov separation. What does this quantity measure that a classical Lyapunov exponent does not? What does it fail to measure that a Lyapunov exponent does? (c) Is T_D dependent on the numerical character depth m? If so, how, and what does this dependence mean for comparing T_D values across systems?

**Exercise 14.** Chapter 16 states a readiness condition for external application: the framework can relate declared exogenous registrations, finite transformations, local trajectory descriptions, and downstream comparisons without first requiring uncontrolled lexical or formal growth. The chapter proposes the Navier–Stokes problem as the next application. (a) Evaluate whether the FSD framework as presented in M26 meets its own readiness condition for the Navier–Stokes investigation. What is present, and what (if anything) remains explicitly marked as open? (b) The principle "no hidden infinite precision" requires that the working trajectory apply the admitted numerical registration at each declared transition, with higher-depth computations treated as separate finite trajectories rather than hidden truth. Describe in detail what this requires of a computational implementation of a finite-difference fluid simulation. What would a conventional implementation do differently, and what would it be hiding? (c) The state descriptor for the Navier–Stokes programme is given as S_i^n = (local registered variables, α, R_{10,9,e}, U_i^n, P_i^n, A_i^n). Identify each component and explain what each contributes to the admissibility of the transition S_i^n → S_{i+1}^n.

## Section IV: Principles, Research Questions, and the Open Programme

The monograph ends with fifteen provisional principles, ten research questions, working notes for the Navier–Stokes transition, and a deliberately open conclusion. This section asks the reader to engage with the monograph's own programme — treating its claims as the functional trajectory they are, with a locally sufficient terminus that the Navier–Stokes investigation will test.

**Exercise 15.** Chapter 19 presents fifteen provisional principles. Select five of the fifteen that you find most consequential for the Navier–Stokes programme. For each: (a) state the principle; (b) explain what specific constraint it places on the Navier–Stokes investigation; (c) identify a conventional computational fluid dynamics practice that this principle would require to be changed, made explicit, or justified. Explain whether you think the change is a practical constraint or a structural clarification.

**Exercise 16.** Chapter 18 presents ten research questions arising from Part III. The final and broadest question is: "Can FSD remain functionally compact as the range of its applications grows?" (a) Explain what "functional compactness" means in the FSD sense — it is not the same as brevity or parsimony. (b) Propose a specific operational criterion by which an expanding framework could be judged to have maintained or lost functional compactness. (c) Apply this criterion to the FSD framework as it stands at the end of M26: has it maintained functional compactness through the development from Parts I to III? What evidence from the monograph supports your assessment?

**Exercise 17.** The Working Notes at the end of M26 set out "the held position from which the next monograph begins." The notes specify constraints on the Navier–Stokes study across five areas: spatial representation, numerical calculation, measurement-derived versus computationally evolved states, Dynexil usage, and vocabulary monitoring. (a) For each of the five areas, state the specific constraint as you understand it from the Working Notes. (b) The notes conclude: "The Navier–Stokes programme is therefore not only an application of FSD. It is the first substantial external stress test of whether the language developed in Parts I–III has reached a useful local functional terminus." What does it mean for this investigation to be a *stress test of the language* rather than merely an application of the physics? (c) The notes state that vocabulary growth should be monitored and new terms introduced only where the existing grammar cannot describe a repeated and important operation without ambiguity. Write a brief protocol for how such monitoring could be conducted systematically throughout a computational study.

**Exercise 18.** Chapter 20 concludes: "The condition for stopping is not that nothing more can be said, but that nothing more presently needs to be said for the declared function to hold." (a) Apply this principle reflexively to the monograph itself: what is the declared function of M26, and what is the evidence that the monograph has reached a local functional terminus with respect to that function? (b) What does it mean that "the global trajectory remains open" while "the local trajectory may stop"? Give a concrete example from the history of any scientific or mathematical programme where this distinction mattered — where a local stopping was correct and productive, and where a local stopping was premature and needed to be reopened. (c) The monograph presents two complementary termini: measurement refinement → α, and symbolic elaboration → Γ\*_functional. Write a brief account of how these two termini interact in a specific research situation: one in which the Alphonic terminus and the functional terminus are reached at different points and by different routes, and explain what the researcher should do in each case.
