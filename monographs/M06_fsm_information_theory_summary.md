## Overview

M06 develops a preliminary formal comparison between Shannon information theory and a Geofinite treatment of information as **finite symbolic containership**. Where Shannon treats the symbol as a dimensionless distinguishable alternative — a selection from a probability distribution — M06 insists that a symbol is a **finite geometric containment object**: instantiated through measurement at a generonic boundary, carrying an irreducible uncertainty geometry, and moving through use, reconstruction, and constraint.

The monograph is openly declared as a working research draft — a scaffold for later refinement rather than a finished system. It is structured in two chapters:

- **Chapter 1** — Geofinite Information and Symbolic Containership: the formal comparison with Shannon, the Geofinite Information Object, base-dependent containment geometry, and the Equivalent Alphonic Pi construction
- **Chapter 2** — Functional Symbolic Trajectories and Metrological Anchoring: the complementary claim that a symbol not only contains but *moves*; the trajectory formalism; the SI second as a worked example; slow nouns as stabilising trajectories

The closing principle of the monograph: **A symbol contains. A symbol moves. A measured symbol anchors. A repeated symbol becomes a trajectory. A constrained trajectory becomes meaning.**

## Chapter 1 — Geofinite Information and Symbolic Containership

### The Historical Starting Point

Shannon's mathematical theory of communication (1948) abstracted information from all physical and geometric content. A symbol is a distinguishable alternative; entropy measures uncertainty over selections. This produced an extraordinarily powerful and general framework. M06 asks: **what was excluded at the moment of abstraction?**

The answer: the finite geometry of the symbol itself. Shannon's formalism removes containment structure, provenance, and path-loss in order to become mathematically tractable. M06 does not deny Shannon's effectiveness within its own domain; it asks what cannot be recovered after the projection.

### Key Formal Definitions

**Definition 1.1 (Generonic Map):** Γ_{α,B}: Ω → S — a finite-resolution, base-B-dependent map from the pre-symbolic space Ω to the finite symbol set S, parametrised by the Alphonic Limit α. Not a probability distribution; not a deterministic function; a bounded measurement projection.

**Definition 1.2 (Finite Geometric Alphabet):** A^G_B = {(s_B, C_B(s_B)) | s_B ∈ S_B} — a finite symbol set in which each symbol s_B carries an explicit containment geometry C_B(s_B) and depends on base B.

**Definition 1.3 (Generonic Path Loss):** L_Γ(s) = Γ^{-1}_{α,B}(s) — the non-recoverable fibre: the set of pre-symbolic paths that all map to the same symbol s. Shannon entropy, reinterpreted in this framework, measures **non-recoverable path-loss after finite symbol instantiation** — not just uncertainty over selectable outcomes.

**Definition 1.5 (Geofinite Information Object):** I_G = (A^G_B, p, C, Γ_{α,B}, α) — the complete information-theoretic object, comprising the geometric alphabet, an optional probability distribution, the containment geometry assignment, the generonic map, and the Alphonic Limit.

Shannon information is then recovered as a projection:
$$\Pi_S(I_G) = (A_B, p)$$
The projection Π_S discards containment geometry, the generonic map, and the Alphonic Limit. It is precisely this projection that makes Shannon information powerful and precisely why it cannot recover finite symbolic geometry.

### Containment Priority (Principle 1.2)

> At the Alphonic Limit, measurement is not first the refinement of precision. It is the finite containment of a relation such that the relation can be distinguished at all.

This **reverses the usual order**. Shannon: distinction comes first (select a message). Geofinite: containment comes first (a symbol contains the uncertainty geometry of a finite measurement). Distinction is then a *property* of contained relations, not the primitive foundation.

### The Equivalent Alphonic Pi (EAP) Construction

The EAP asks: what does circular geometry look like when expressed relative to an alphonic unitary of base B? This yields a base-dependent value EAP(B) that explores the threshold at which a base provides sufficient symbolic resources for planar circular closure.

Key result — **Result 3: Base 4 is the First EAP Closure Threshold:**
$$B_{min} = \lceil 10/\pi \rceil = 4$$

The heuristic hierarchy:
- B = 2: distinction/opposition only
- B = 3: mediation/relational discrimination
- B = 4: first planar geometric closure
- B ≥ 4: stable symbolic circular containment

Binary distinction alone cannot close a circle. This is a concrete instance of the Geofinite claim that **base invariance of abstract value does not imply base invariance of containment geometry**.

### The Sphere as Prime Limit

The sphere is the minimum isotropic containment structure — the form that makes no privileged orientation. Finite measurement, which cannot resolve direction below the Alphonic Limit, naturally produces spherical rather than point distributions. The **Spherical Uncertainty Distribution** replaces the point distribution of classical measurement: a sphere of radius proportional to measurement uncertainty, not a delta function at an exact value.

## Chapter 2 — Functional Symbolic Trajectories and Metrological Anchoring

### The Central Claim

Chapter 1 established: a finite symbol does not merely distinguish; it **contains**. Chapter 2 adds: a finite symbol does not merely contain; it **moves**.

A **Functional Symbolic Trajectory** is the formal object describing how a symbol — word, number, equation, unit definition — changes its representational relation, constraint structure, uncertainty region, and provenance history across uses, contexts, and time.

### Formal Definition

A symbol s at stage t is characterised by the tuple:
$$T_s(t) = (M_t, R_t, K_t, U_t, H_t)$$

where:
- M_t: the mark or inscription (the observable form of the symbol)
- R_t: the representational relation to the wider semantic phase space
- K_t: the constraint or admissibility structure acting on the trajectory at stage t
- U_t ⊆ M: the local uncertainty region (neighbourhood of possible reconstructions)
- H_t: the history, provenance, or prior path through which the trajectory has been stabilised

The trajectory is the finite sequence: T_s: T_s(t₀) → T_s(t₁) → ··· → T_s(tₙ).

Not all components are always known or recoverable. Provenance, in particular, is frequently lost. The loss of H_t is the mechanism of silent promotion in M05: endogenous results are treated as exogenous when their provenance is forgotten.

**Principle 2.1 (Trajectory Non-Invariance):** Abstract value invariance under base conversion does not imply invariance of finite symbolic trajectory. Different base representations may preserve abstract value while altering symbolic length, compression, visual form, tokenisation, memory, computational handling, and semantic inheritance.

### Slow Nouns as Stabilising Trajectories

A **Slow Noun** (Definition 1.5) is a symbol whose semantic containment basin stabilises only through repeated use, refinement, and negotiated reconstruction. Formally:
$$d(B_{w_{t+1}}, B_{w_t}) \rightarrow 0$$
under an appropriate semantic distance d, while never becoming perfectly fixed.

Examples: *Alphon*, *Generon*, *Spherical Uncertainty Distribution*, *Geofinitism*, *containership*, *Alphonic Pi*.

These terms are not quick labels. They are slow containment objects — their meaning accumulates through the trajectory of their use within the programme, not from a single definition.

### Metrological Anchoring

A **metrologically anchored** trajectory is one that has been grounded at some point through a formal measurement definition — a Nexil-level contact with the Geofinite Continuum. The SI second is the worked example:

The SI second traverses: physical process (caesium-133 atomic transition) → symbolic convention (9,192,631,770 periods) → institutional agreement (BIPM) → instrumentation (atomic clocks) → textbooks → software → theoretical inheritance.

At each stage the symbol moves. Its trajectory is not merely a change of form — it is a change of R_t, K_t, U_t, and H_t. The metrological anchor (the atomic transition definition) is the fixed point in the trajectory from which other stages inherit stability.

### Five-Pillar Diagnostic as Trajectory Measurement

The Five Pillars framework (ATT_49) functions within M06 as a **trajectory measurement instrument**: asking at any point in an exposition which symbolic trajectories are being constructed, which constraint regimes are active, and where the exposition loses admissibility and drifts into unconstrained narrative projection.

### Language Models and Symbolic Trajectory

LLMs operate on tokens — finite symbolic marks without explicitly represented containment geometry, provenance, or constraint structure. Under M06's framework:
- Token embedding is a compressed representation of the containment basin — not the basin itself
- Semantic drift in generation is the trajectory losing its admissibility constraint K_t
- Slow nouns are difficult for LLMs because their basins are narrow and locally negotiated; the model pulls them toward nearby established attractors
