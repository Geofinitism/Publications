# Registration of Extent
## Toward a Finite Symbolic Account of Measurement, Decoherence, and Dynamical Stability

**Author:** Kevin R. Haylett · **Date:** 2026 · **Pages:** 39 · **Series:** Foundational Measurement Series

---

## Abstract

This monograph develops a finite symbolic account of measurement in which registration, rather than a pre-assumed travelling entity, is taken as foundational. The argument begins from a simple distinction: the measured world is encountered as systems with extent, whereas the symbols used to record measurement — a click, a count, a coordinate, a binary value — do not preserve the extent of the physical interaction that produced them. FSM therefore begins with extent, interaction, finite distinction, relational trajectory, stabilisation, and registration, and only afterwards admits symbolic representation.

From this starting point the monograph reconstructs several familiar concepts of physics in a different order: zero-extent points are replaced by finite distinguishable registrations at an Alphonic limit; isolated beads are connected into relational strings; nonlinear phase-space reconstruction provides a means of describing stable dynamical organisation without requiring an infinitely divisible background space; attractors are treated as stable organisations of continuing dynamics; and averaging is shown to be a potentially destructive compression of trajectory.

The same method is then applied to the language of quantum mechanics. The path integral is unfolded as a symbolic compression of distinguishable trajectory contributions accumulated before final registration. Superposition is reconsidered as unresolved dynamical accessibility. The two-slit experiment is reformulated as a single extended interacting system whose boundary conditions are altered by the aperture configuration. Decoherence is developed as a change of dynamical regime that occurs when a relational organisation stable at one scale is incorporated into a wider coupled system and transformed toward a coarser, robust registration.

The result is not presented as a completed replacement for quantum theory. It is a foundational measurement programme whose purpose is to provide an admissible route from the measured world of extent to the symbolic world of recorded outcomes, and thereby to create a basis from which experimentally discriminating predictions may later be derived.

## Prefatory Note

This work belongs to the foundational measurement series within FSM. Its direction is deliberately methodological: to begin earlier than the established ontology of classical or quantum physics — at the point where an extended interaction becomes a registration and where that registration becomes a symbol. The central question: **what is lost when an extended interactional event is compressed into a point-like symbol?** The monograph develops the physical registration trajectory opened in *A Theory of Finite Representation, Part I* (M29), following it through nonlinear dynamics, phase-space reconstruction, path language, quantum measurement, the two-slit experiment, decoherence, and the emergence of robust macroscopic registration.

## Chapter Summaries

### Chapter 1: Registration Before Representation (pp. 4–5)

FSM reverses the usual order of physical explanation, which grants the representational object first and introduces measurement later. The foundational sequence proposed is:

> extent | interaction | finite distinction | trajectory | stabilisation | registration | symbol

The vertical bar is used relationally — it marks an admissible connection whose meaning depends on provenance and conditions of measurement, not an assertion of equality. The five commitments are: extent (measured interactions occur somewhere, last some finite duration, involve non-dimensionless systems), interaction (registration arises from one extended system changing in relation to another), finite distinction (whatever is recorded must be distinguishable at some finite limit), trajectory (registrations occur in ordered relations, not as timeless isolated objects), and stabilisation (the result must persist sufficiently for a subsequent system to register it). The methodological principle: **Registration precedes representation.**

#### §1.1 The physical event and the symbolic result

A physical event changes a detector sufficiently for an electronic threshold to be crossed. The output is represented as 0 or 1. Neither mark, by itself, reconstructs the extent, duration, or sequence through which that distinction became stable. The compression: extended interaction → stable physical registration → finite symbol. FSM insists that the provenance remain visible.

### Chapter 2: The Registration Boundary (pp. 6–7)

The boundary between physical registration and symbolic representation is not a boundary in nature but a boundary in a chain of interactions at which a particular description terminates and another begins. The "end" of the measurement is relative to the chosen symbolic trajectory: what is terminal for one analysis is intermediate for another (e.g. a detector click may become the first bead in another chain: click → electronics → auditory → neural registration). This suggests the **generonic boundary**: the point in a chosen measurement chain at which an analogue or materially distributed interaction is rendered as a finite symbol. The boundary is operationally real because instrumentation performs it, but it need not correspond to a fundamental ontological division in the world.

### Chapter 3: From Invisible Points to Finite Beads (pp. 8–9)

Classical geometry begins with the dimensionless point. Within FSM this is not an admissible physical primitive because a zero-extent point cannot itself be measured. The replacement is a finite distinction at an Alphonic limit — a **bead** (•), a pedagogical image for a smallest admissible distinguishable registration. An isolated bead is not sufficient; registrations must be related:

> •₀ → •₁ → •₂ → ··· → •ₙ

This gives the **bead string** — not a continuous line hidden beneath the beads, but a finite relation among distinguishable registrations whose order, spacing, delay, and history matter. Complexity arises through relation rather than infinitesimal subdivision. This permits dynamics without requiring a physically instantiated continuum of unmeasured intermediate points.

### Chapter 4: Trajectory Before State (pp. 10–11)

A measurement at one instant is an impoverished description of a dynamical system. The object of interest is not the isolated state xₜ but the relational sequence xₜ₋₂τ → xₜ₋τ → xₜ. The trajectory is the carrier of dynamical information. A finite three-coordinate delay embedding:

> **X**ₜ = [s(t), s(t−τ), s(t−2τ)]

The coordinates are not granted as invisible axes populated by ideal points; they are finite symbolic relations among measured values. Complex notation z = a + ib is retained as an efficient relational encoding (especially where phase and delay are involved) without the ontological inference that a measurable system possesses a physically real coordinate and an ontologically imaginary coordinate.

#### §4.1 The string can fold

Once delayed relations are represented in three coordinates, the symbolic string can fold through reconstructed phase space. Recurrence, divergence, convergence, cyclic behaviour, unstable regions, and attractor geometry can become visible in the finite representation — providing a route from finite registrations to geometric dynamical structure without first admitting a continuum as physically real.

### Chapter 5: Attractors as Stable Organisation (pp. 12–13)

An attractor is often described as the state toward which a system settles — but a limit cycle or strange attractor is not a fixed state; the system may continue changing indefinitely while remaining confined to a stable organisation of trajectories. The reformulation: **an attractor is a stable organisation of continuing dynamics**. For a nonlinear system ẋ = F(**x**) with attractor A, **x**(t) → A while no individual **x**(t) becomes permanently fixed. For the circular limit cycle x(t) = cos t, y(t) = sin t, the attractor is the unit circle x² + y² = 1, but ⟨x⟩ = ⟨y⟩ = 0, placing the average point at (0,0) — a point the system never occupies. **Stable organisation ≠ stable value.** The average is a symbolic construction derived from the stable dynamics, not the stable dynamics itself.

### Chapter 6: Averaging as Trajectory Compression (pp. 14–15)

Averaging removes relational detail. For a full trajectory Γ = {**x**(t)}, one may construct transition statistics, occupancy distribution, moments, and finally a mean — each stage a compression removing relational detail. For a nonlinear function F, F(⟨x⟩) ≠ ⟨F(x)⟩ in general. This inequality expresses that nonlinear relations act on the trajectory before the average is taken. If the trajectory is compressed too early, the dynamics can be changed or destroyed. Methodological statement: **The trajectories must do their work before their symbolic compression.**

#### §6.1 Measurement as a many-to-one operation

Many distinct lower-scale interactions can produce the same detector state (γ₁ → R₀, γ₂ → R₀, γ₃ → R₀). Two layers of compression: dynamical compression → symbolic compression. The first is performed by the physics of the instrument; the second by the representational system.

### Chapter 7: Unfolding the Path Integral (pp. 16–17)

The path integral K = ∫ 𝒟[x] e^{iS[x]/ℏ} is unfolded conceptually as "relational trajectory contributions accumulated before final compression." Terms are provisionally unfolded: path → finite distinguishable trajectory; phase → finite relational delay or orientation; complex value → compressed finite embedding; integration → finite accumulation of relational contributions; all paths → available distinguishable trajectory classes. Within FSM, if two paths cannot be distinguished below the admissible resolution, they cannot be treated as separately measured trajectories. The question changes from whether an infinity of paths physically exists to: **what finite relational dynamics are being compressed by the notation?**

### Chapter 8: The Problem of Quantum Language (pp. 18–19)

Quantum experiments are marked by finite registrations (clicks, spots, threshold crossings, spectral lines, discrete detector states, binary outputs) yet the standard description is built using structures permitting indefinitely fine distinction: ℝ, ℂ, ℋ, ψ, ∫ 𝒟[x]. FSM treats this as a provenance problem: the measured event precedes the symbolic construction. Mathematical relations can begin to sound like physical locations and behaviours (states "live in" Hilbert space; systems are "in" superposition; wavefunctions "collapse"). The guiding question: **What measured trajectory preceded this symbol?**

#### §8.1 Discrete registration and continuous reconstruction

Quantum mechanics recognises discrete outcomes while representing them within continuous mathematical spaces. FSM regards that move as a historical and mathematical construction requiring justification rather than as a primitive fact. **Finite registration ≠ physically instantiated infinitely divisible state space.**

### Chapter 9: Superposition as Unresolved Dynamical Accessibility (pp. 20–21)

FSM declines the projection of output symbols 0 and 1 backwards into the pre-registration world. Instead: suppose a wider nonlinear system possesses two eventual registration basins B₀ and B₁, separated by a finite dynamically sensitive region Σ. The state B₀|Σ|B₁ does not mean 0 + 1; it means the wider system has not yet stabilised into either terminal symbolic basin. The corresponding registration chain: Σ → {B₀ → 0, B₁ → 1}. The description: **Neither symbolic registration has yet stabilised.** Superposition notation may represent a **compressed account of unresolved dynamical accessibility** rather than the literal co-existence of symbolic outcomes.

### Chapter 10: The Two-Slit Experiment as an Extended System (pp. 22–23)

FSM begins with the experimental arrangement as an extended system 𝒮 = {source, apertures, materials, surroundings, detector, …}. Two apertures open gives 𝒮_{AB}; closing one gives 𝒮_A or 𝒮_B. Therefore 𝒮_{AB} ≠ 𝒮_A ≠ 𝒮_B — closing a slit changes boundary conditions and the wider interacting system, not merely removes one route from a pre-existing set of paths. For a nonlinear system: ℛ(𝒮_{AB}) ≠ ℛ(𝒮_A) + ℛ(𝒮_B) in general. The experimentally observed non-additivity can be investigated as a property of the configured system before it is interpreted as evidence for a travelling object simultaneously taking multiple paths.

#### §10.1 No assumed journey from A to B

Within FSM, an unmeasured line from source to detector is not admitted merely because classical geometry can draw it. The best available representation is a finite chain of registrations •₀ → •₁ → ··· → •ₙ. The intervening relation is reconstructed from the best finite evidence available.

### Chapter 11: Pattern of Registrations and Registration of a Pattern (pp. 24–25)

In a low-flux accumulation experiment, the detector first registers local events r₁, r₂, …, r_N, subsequently binned spatially as N(x). A smooth distribution may then be fitted or plotted. The distinction: **pattern of registrations ≠ registration of a pattern.** The smooth curve constructed from many registrations need not be projected backwards as the thing that travelled through the apparatus. The full chain kept explicit: extended interacting system → finite dynamical trajectory → registration event with extent → symbolic coordinate/value → many symbolic registrations → counts and bins → smooth represented distribution.

### Chapter 12: The Click as an End Bead (pp. 26)

The detector click is a boundary object showing how a continuing physical interaction becomes a terminated symbolic trajectory: ··· → •_{n-2} → •_{n-1} → [•_{click}]. The click is the end bead relative to a particular analysis; it is not an absolute physical endpoint. The same event may become the first bead in another chain: [•_{click}] → •_{electronics} → •_{auditory} → •_{neural} → ···. Measurement is not necessarily where the world stops changing; it is where a selected trajectory is considered sufficiently stable to be registered and represented.

### Chapter 13: System Enlargement and Decoherence (pp. 27–28)

Coupling a system S_A (having stable organisation A(S_A)) to another system S_B gives S_A|S_B → S_{AB}. The enlarged system has different coupling terms, constraints, accessible trajectories, and therefore potentially different attractors. There is no general requirement that A(S_A) remain an attractor of S_{AB}: A(S_A) ≠ A(S_{AB}). **FSM decoherence conjecture:** A dynamically stable relational organisation at one interactional scale may become unstable when incorporated into a wider coupled system, whose dynamics then converge toward a coarser stable organisation capable of producing a finite registration.

#### §13.1 Nested enlargement

The enlargement continues: S_A → S_{AB} → S_{ABC} → S_{ABCD} → ···. Each stage may possess a different phase-space organisation. The conventional question "where does the quantum system become classical?" may be badly posed: the system is repeatedly changed by coupling, and there may be no single ontological boundary, only successive dynamical transformations across scale.

### Chapter 14: Measurement as Physical Coarse-Graining (pp. 29–30)

The detector is a nonlinear dynamical system whose stable macroscopic outputs are deliberately engineered: γ₁, γ₂, γ₃ → R₀; γ₄ → R₁. Full sequence: {γᵢ} → Rⱼ → j. The first arrow is dynamical compression across scale; the second is symbolic compression. This relocates part of the measurement problem into the actual design and dynamics of instrumentation.

#### §14.1 Scale and loss of recoverable distinction

Let ℓ denote a fine distinguishable scale and L the characteristic scale of a macroscopic registration, with L ≫ ℓ. Many lower-scale relational configurations become indistinguishable once mapped into the same L-scale state. The loss of fine structure is the loss of recoverable distinction within the enlarged system and chosen representation — not the metaphysical destruction of information.

### Chapter 15: Collapse as Representational Discontinuity (pp. 31)

The language of collapse presents a dramatic transition |ψ⟩ → |0⟩. FSM: the interacting sequence may instead be S_A → S_{AB} → S_{ABC} → ··· → R₀ → 0. Each arrow may contain finite dynamical change. The apparent discontinuity arises because a high-dimensional, extended interactional trajectory is compared with the compact symbol produced at the end. **FSM conjecture:** Collapse is at least partly a representational discontinuity generated when an extended dynamical process is compressed into a finite symbolic state. The phrase "at least partly" is essential — the conjecture does not assume all experimentally observed discontinuity is representational, but provides a framework for investigating physical and symbolic transitions separately.

### Chapter 16: One World of Extent (pp. 32)

Rather than asking "where does the quantum world become classical?", FSM asks: "how does a fine-scale relational dynamical organisation transform as it is progressively coupled into wider systems until a stable registration becomes possible?" This requires only one world of extent — systems within systems, interacting across scales: S₁ → S₁₂ → S₁₂₃ → ···. There are changes in stability, attractor structure, accessible trajectory, and registration, but no need at the foundational level to place an invisible mathematical wall between quantum and classical ontology. The distinction may instead emerge from the representational consequences of scale, coupling, and stabilisation.

### Chapter 17: Registration as Foundational (pp. 33–34)

The provisional FSM registration principle:
1. Physical measurement begins with interacting systems of extent.
2. Interaction produces finite distinguishable changes.
3. Relations among these distinctions form trajectories.
4. Coupling changes the dynamical organisation of the wider system.
5. Stable macroscopic organisation produces registration.
6. Registration is subsequently compressed into a symbol.

The order matters: if representation is placed first, physical explanation is tempted to inherit the ontology of the representation. If registration is placed first, every symbolic construction is required to retain provenance back to a finite interactional event.

#### §17.1 The emerging trajectory

The full sequence developed in the monograph: extent → interaction → finite distinction → relational bead string → finite delay embedding → nonlinear dynamical organisation → system coupling → instability/attractor change → macroscopic stabilisation → registration → finite symbol → statistical and mathematical representation.

### Chapter 18: Two Methodological Routes (pp. 35)

The two approaches displayed directly:

**Conventional representational route:** symbolic/state-space primitive → mathematical evolution → measurement problem → registered outcome.

**FSM measurement route:** extent → interaction and finite distinction → dynamical stabilisation → registration → symbolic representation.

FSM brings measurement operations upstream of the symbolic framework rather than placing them downstream of an already-specified state space.

### Chapter 19: Predictions Must Follow the Model (pp. 36)

The work stops deliberately before specifying the experimental programme. The methodological sequence: model → prediction → experimental design → registration → comparison. The experimental task is to identify a condition under which the registration-based nonlinear model and a conventional travelling-entity model do not make the same expectation. Possible experimental variables include geometry, separation, material boundary conditions, detector coupling, scale, delay, interaction density, or controlled changes in the registration chain. The purpose of this work is to provide an admissible finite starting point from which an experimentally distinguishable prediction may eventually be made.

### Conclusion (pp. 37)

The central issue examined is the registration of extent. FSM takes the opposite route to conventional physics: begins with extent; admits finite distinction; connects distinguishable registrations into relational strings; uses delay and phase-space reconstruction to recover dynamical organisation; treats attractors as stable organisations rather than stable values; treats averaging as potentially destructive compression of trajectory; unfolds the path integral as retaining trajectory distinctions until a later stage; reconsiders superposition as unresolved dynamical accessibility; treats the two-slit apparatus as a single configured system; interprets detector registration as physical coarse-graining; proposes decoherence as a change of dynamical regime.

The resulting sequence is economical: **extent → interaction → distinction → trajectory → stabilisation → registration → symbol.** The next question is experimental: if registration rather than travelling wave or photon is taken as foundational, what measurable prediction follows?

### Appendix A: Working Vocabulary (pp. 38)

Ten terms defined: Alphonic limit, Bead, Bead string, Registration, Symbol, Generonic boundary, Dynamical compression, Symbolic compression, Unresolved dynamical accessibility, FSM decoherence conjecture.

### Appendix B: Provisional Statements for Later Testing (pp. 39)

Six provisional statements as research markers: (1) A detector registration is better modelled as the stable outcome of an extended nonlinear interaction than as the direct revelation of an ideal point-state. (2) A change in experimental boundary conditions alters the whole configured system and may alter its attractor structure. (3) A two-slit registration pattern should be investigated first as a property of the whole extended system, not as proof of a primitive travelling entity taking more than one path. (4) Apparent collapse may contain a representational discontinuity introduced by the mapping from extended dynamics to a compact symbol. (5) Decoherence may be experimentally reconstructible as a transition between dynamical organisations across system enlargement and scale. (6) If the registration model is foundational rather than merely interpretive, at least one experimental condition should eventually be found under which it predicts a registration distribution or dynamical transition distinguishable from a conventional travelling-entity account.
