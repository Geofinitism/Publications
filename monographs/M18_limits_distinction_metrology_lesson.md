## Core Lesson

### 1. The Generonic Boundary and the First Distinction

Every scientific measurement begins at the *generonic boundary*: the point at which an unresolved physical interaction produces a physically retainable distinction. The elementary empirical event is not the world disclosing itself but a *transition within a registering system*.

The complete trajectory from interaction to observable is:

> W̃ —G→ ΔR —E→ sᵢ —M→ Õᵢ

W̃ is the unresolved interactional world; G is the generonic boundary; ΔR is the physical alteration of the measuring system; sᵢ is the encoded finite symbol; Õᵢ is the model-defined observable. This chain should never be collapsed into a single word — "measurement" — without retaining visibility of each link.

The foundational methodological inequality is:

> **stepwise measurability ≠⇒ stepwise ontology**

The fact that a detector returns discrete clicks does not establish that the world consists of corresponding discrete objects. A further inferential trajectory is required, and that trajectory must remain recoverable.

**Exercise 1.** A photomultiplier tube registers individual voltage pulses. Describe what has been established at the generonic boundary (ΔR), and what further commitments are required to move from "a pulse was detected" to "a photon was observed".

### 2. Plurality, Extent and the Finite State

A single registration establishes one distinction. Measured *plurality* requires at least two registrations remaining non-identical and separately admissible within the complete measurement arrangement:

> D̃₂: r̃₁ ≠ r̃₂, with d(r̃₁, r̃₂) > δ_system

The separability threshold δ_system gathers source, apparatus, detector response, noise, encoding convention and classification rule. It is not a naked geometrical distance.

Every physically instantiated symbol has extent. A finite state carries:

> S̃ᵢ = (Gᵢ, tᵢ, δᵢ, Hᵢ, Cᵢ)

— geometry, time-placement, uncertainty, provenance history and consensus conditions. An extensionless point may be used in formal calculation but cannot be a physically instantiated primitive: it supplies no basis for physical separation, retention or inspection.

**Exercise 2.** Two detector events in a particle track chamber are separated by 50 μm. The system's δ_system under current conditions is 80 μm. What does FSM say about the status of the claimed two-particle interpretation?

### 3. Measurement as Selective Compression and the Observable

Measurement is both revelation and reduction:

> Ĩ → ΔR → sᵢ, where sᵢ ≠ Ĩ

The symbol is a finite product of the interaction, not a complete reproduction of it. A diffraction pattern preserves relational periodicities while requiring reconstruction before atomic coordinates can be assigned. A thresholded event preserves occurrence while discarding sub-threshold variation.

The *observable* in quantum mechanics is a constructed quantity, not a surface feature:

> interaction → apparatus change → signal → datum → observable

Within FSM, the observable is conditioned by the communal rules determining what counts as a measurement, how the apparatus is calibrated, which transformations are accepted, and which uncertainties are retained.

**Exercise 3.** An analogue-to-digital converter samples a voltage signal at 10 kHz with 12-bit depth. List three items of information about the source interaction that the resulting symbol stream cannot contain.

### 4. The Semantic Agent

A *semantic agent* is a named model component constructed from registered regularities to which causal, explanatory or behavioural agency is subsequently assigned within scientific language. The construction trajectory is:

> {registrations} → stable relation → parameter → name → property bearer → causal agent

The problem is not the introduction of the name. It is the *gradual detachment* of that name from its measurement provenance. Once detached, the agent appears to stand alone and to explain the registrations from which it was derived — reversing the actual inferential direction.

The *Greene–Pascal semantic split* names the act of dividing a relational registration into separately named explanatory components. Greene's insight is that symbolic division can continue indefinitely under its own rules; Pascal's insight is that language cannot ground every term through further terms. Together they identify how symbolic decomposition may continue after physical resolution has failed:

> w̃orld → m̃atter + ṽacuum → ···

The ellipsis marks a real capacity — not a metaphor. The ink continues when the instrument can no longer produce independently resolved marks.

**Exercise 4.** The quantum field vacuum is assigned zero-point energy, virtual particle fluctuations and a cosmological constant contribution. Applying the Greene–Pascal analysis, identify which of these are stable relations reconstructed from registrations, which are semantic extensions from model structure, and which represent possible splits whose measurement provenance is unclear.

### 5. Charge, Mass and Point-Likeness

The charge–mass distinction is not directly observed as two separate substances. Experiments register responses within arrangements involving fields, accelerations, trajectories and detectors. The model organises these through parameters q and m. A critical FSM inequality applies:

> **symbolic separability ≠⇒ measured physical separability**

The symbols q and m can occupy independent positions in an equation. A physical claim that charge and mass are independently existing properties requires a corresponding distinction within measurement. The purpose of FSM analysis is not to deny the utility of the separation but to expose its commitments.

The "point particle" illustrates the same pattern. The justified statement is: no internal structure was distinguished under specified conditions and within a stated uncertainty. The stronger model statement is: the represented entity has no extent. These are not equivalent:

> no resolved internal structure → extensionless point-agent (9.1)

FSM retains the formal point as a permitted instrument in symbolic calculus while refusing to identify it with a physically instantiated primitive.

**Exercise 5.** An electron scattering experiment reports that the electron has no resolved substructure above a momentum transfer of Q = 100 GeV/c. Write the FSM-compliant version of this result, retaining the instrument, energy-range and resolution indexing, and compare it with the model statement "the electron is a point particle".

### 6. The Stern–Gerlach Provenance Study

The Stern–Gerlach experiment is M18's central case study because the passage from physical registration to semantic agent can be reconstructed with unusual clarity.

**Step 1 — The complete interaction:**
> source + prepared silver beam + collimation + field geometry + flight interval + detector → finite deposition distribution

No single component of the left side is dispensable. The result belongs first to the complete interactional system.

**Step 2 — From distribution to binary symbol:**
A classification operation C converts the extended spatial distributions into {+, −}:
> finite spatial distributions —C→ {+, −}

The exactness of the binary belongs to the symbolic output of C, not to the physical distributions, which retain width, intensity variation and possible overlap.

**Step 3 — The inferential chain to spin-½:**
> two outcome regions → two-valued magnetic response → angular-momentum component → electron spin → spin-½ particle

Each arrow adds a commitment. The fraction ½ enters through the angular-momentum algebra, the chosen measurement direction and Planck's reduced constant — it is a relation within the model, not an isolated fraction physically deposited upon the detector.

**Step 4 — The transfer of agency:**
> apparatus-system interaction → particle → spin → outcome

The final expression is shorter and easier to use. Its compression is precisely what gives it explanatory force. But the property-agent did not enter through a separate detector channel — it was constructed as a means of organising the apparatus-wide relation and placed behind that relation as its cause.

**Exercise 6.** A colleague says "the Stern–Gerlach experiment directly observed two spin states". Construct a provenance-preserving replacement statement that retains the scientific content while making the transformations visible.

### 7. Resolution, Precision and Accuracy

Three metrological concepts must be kept distinct:

| Concept | Definition | FSM significance |
|---|---|---|
| Spatial resolution (δ_instrument) | Minimum separation at which two features can be distinguished | Sets the physical separability condition |
| Positional precision (δ_parameter) | Repeatability or estimated uncertainty of a fitted feature's location | Can be smaller than δ_instrument through statistical fitting |
| Model-relative accuracy | Agreement with an independently warranted reference | Depends on model and calibration choices |

The invalid identification is:

> δ_distinction = δ_parameter (14.2)

Repeated measurements can narrow δ_parameter without narrowing δ_instrument. The symbolic stream gains digits while the physical resolving event may remain unchanged. The rhetoric of "astonishing accuracy" promotes this confusion by allowing numerical agreement to carry ontological confidence:

> numerical agreement —evaluative language→ ontological confidence (16.1)

The FSM replacement: |x̃_calculated − x̃_measured| ≤ δ̃, where each term retains its provenance.

**Exercise 7.** An X-ray crystallography experiment reports atomic positions with δ_parameter = 3 pm. The instrument's spatial resolution is δ_instrument ≈ 50 pm. A popular account says "scientists have imaged individual atoms with picometre precision". Identify the equivocation and write the FSM-compliant statement.

### 8. The Greene–Pascal and Alphonic Limits

**The Greene–Pascal Limit** is not a fixed number. It is a function of the complete measurement and interpretive system:

> G_P = G_P(I, M, E, C, H)

I = instrument; M = model; E = encoding process; C = consensus conditions; H = measurement history. The limit marks the boundary beyond which continued symbolic decomposition no longer corresponds to independently sustained physical distinctions. At this boundary, the ink continues but the independently measured marks do not.

**The Alphonic Limit** (α) is more foundational. It concerns the proposed minimum finite physical extent at which a distinction can be instantiated, preserved and admitted into a symbolic system. It is not the current frontier of any instrument — it concerns what physical distinction itself requires:

> α ≤ G_P(I, M, E, C, H) (18.1)

An improved instrument may reduce G_P, but this does not mean α has moved. The instrumental boundary may approach the foundational boundary without identifying it.

**Three possible relations between the limits:**

| Relation | Expression | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Distinct limits | G_P > α | A region exists between finest admissible reconstruction and minimum possible symbol |
| Asymptotic | G_P → α | Boundary approachable but not finally demonstrable |
| Identical | G_P ≡ α | Failure of measured decomposition and failure of physical symbolic instantiation are one event from opposite directions |

The framework does not select among these. The decision requires careful analysis of spatial and temporal distinction, instrument dependence, storage, reconstruction and the conditions under which a result ceases to be a physical plurality and becomes only a symbolic one.

**Exercise 8.** A theoretical model predicts structure at a scale of 10⁻²⁰ m. The current instrumental Greene–Pascal Limit for the relevant arrangement is approximately 10⁻¹⁸ m. Using the inequality α ≤ G_P, explain what can and cannot be concluded about the Alphonic Limit from this prediction and this instrumental frontier.

### 9. The Agency-and-Provenance Audit

The audit is a systematic research method. For any quantum concept:

1. Begin with the physical alteration of the apparatus (ΔR).
2. Follow every transformation: encoding → signal → datum → observable.
3. Record when a noun was introduced, which properties were attached to it, and when the noun became the subject of a causal verb.
4. Identify what disappeared: apparatus geometry, response functions, thresholds, rejected events, reconstruction assumptions, calibration history, classification rules.
5. Locate the Greene–Pascal Limit for this specific practice.

The provenance record for a semantic agent takes the form:

> Ã = (N, R, E, M, K, U)

N = name; R = class of supporting registrations; E = encoding trajectory; M = mathematical model; K = added commitments; U = unresolved alternatives and uncertainties.

The audit purpose is *discriminating reconstruction*, not collective dismissal. It would be a mistake to declare all quantum agents unreal in one gesture. Different agents — electron, photon, wavefunction, vacuum state — have different measurement trajectories and must be evaluated separately.

**Exercise 9.** Apply the agency-and-provenance audit to the concept "the wavefunction collapsed". Identify the registrations, the encoding chain, the added commitments and the unresolved alternatives.

### 10. Finite Symbolic Dynamics and the Cost of the Agent

FSD offers an alternative starting point. Instead of beginning with hidden point-objects, it begins with finite provenance-bearing states and admissible transitions:

> Γ̃ = (S̃₀, S̃₁, …, S̃ₙ)

The constructive order is: finite trajectories → stable relational invariants → optional semantic agent. The word *optional* is important. FSD does not prohibit names; it requires names to remain attached to the trajectory class from which they were constructed.

The complete cost of a semantic agent is not the ink required to write its name. It is the discarded history of apparatus, interaction, threshold, calibration, reconstruction, consensus and uncertainty that allowed the name to stabilise. FSM does not abolish the agent — it returns the agent to its provenance.

At the Greene–Pascal Limit, symbolic decomposition continues while separately measured distinction begins to fail. At the Alphonic Limit, distinction itself reaches its proposed physical boundary. A symbolically divisible world is not thereby an indefinitely measurable world.

> **The future task is not to stop inference, but to ensure that every inference continues to carry the trajectory of its construction.**
