## Central Proposition

Registration is the finite symbolic instantiation produced when an interaction crosses a generonic boundary and becomes locally available as a finite symbol for continuation. Re-registration is the generonic event through which an externally instantiated symbol becomes newly instantiated within another symbolic occasion. Reading, understanding, memory, communication, and scientific measurement are each species of this single finite event.

The monograph establishes that the world does not enter the symbolic realm as meaning, content, or abstract type — it enters as registration. Every symbol that persists, every text that is read, every scientific claim that is transmitted is a chain of finite re-registrations. The closing statement: *"Registration is the finite hinge between interaction and text. Re-registration is the finite hinge by which text continues."*

---

## Key Definitions

**Registration:** The finite symbolic instantiation produced when an interaction crosses a generonic boundary and becomes locally available as a finite symbol for continuation.

**Re-registration:** The generonic event through which an externally instantiated symbol becomes newly instantiated within another symbolic occasion or system; RA ≠ RB; RA ~A RB.

**Generonic boundary:** The functional transition (GA: X → SA) through which an interaction becomes locally available as a finite symbol. Not a transparent window — the interaction I does not pass through unchanged.

**The Generon:** A finite arrangement capable of producing registrations. Defined by four capacities: participates in an interaction; distinguishes a symbolic response from the background; stabilises the response as a finite symbol; makes the symbol available for continuation.

**Registration tuple:** R = (m, A, C, τ, α, δ, H, A) — mark, registering arrangement, conditions, duration, Alphonic constraint, uncertainty, provenance, admissibility.

**Thick registration:** Thick(R) = {m, A, C, τ, α, δ, H, A} — all eight components. Compression: Comp(R) = m (mark alone). Registration drag: DR = Thick(R) \ Comp(R) — the omitted lineage when a mark is compressed.

**Symbolic lineage:** LS = (R1, R2, ..., Rn) where Ri ~A Rj — admissible relatedness, not type identity. Replaces the classical type/token distinction.

**Functional Symbolic Trajectory (FST):** T = (R1, R2, ..., Rn; C, δ, H, A) — an ordered sequence of registrations with full contextual thickness.

## Part-by-Part Summary

### Part I — The Missing Event (Chapters 1–2)

**Chapter 1: You Are Reading These Words**  
Opens with the reader in the act of reading. The first movement is not the arrival of meaning but the act of measurement: the page provides structured opportunity for symbolic reconstruction, not transmitted content. The page-space and reader-space are distinct: Spage ≠ Sreader. What the reader performs is re-registration and reconstruction, not reception. Familiar words carry compressed registration history.

**Chapter 2: The Compression Hidden in Ordinary Language**  
Compression is unavoidable: ordinary communication compresses full trajectories into transmissible marks. The compressed form R' = Comp(R) = m retains the mark but omits apparatus, conditions, duration, Alphonic constraint, uncertainty, and provenance. Disciplined compression retains unfoldable lineage; undisciplined compression produces registration drag without recovery. The full registration event R = (m, A, C, τ, α, δ, H, A) is what ordinary language suppresses. M14 restores it.

### Part II — Registration and the Generonic Boundary (Chapters 3–5)

**Chapter 3: The Generonic Boundary**  
The generonic boundary GA: X → SA is the functional transition through which interaction becomes symbolic availability. The fuller relation: RA = GA(I | CA, τA, αA, δA, HA, AA). The boundary does not operate as a transparent window — the same interaction produces different registrations in different arrangements. Boundaries are distributed (not a single location), nestable (I → R1 → R2 → ... → Rn), and create symbolic responsibility for what is made available. The Generon defined by its four capacities. Shared interaction with distributed boundaries: I, GA, GB produce SA, SB independently.

**Chapter 4: Registration**  
Names the event that has been missing from accounts of language, measurement, and cognition. Registration is event and residue simultaneously — the act of crossing the generonic boundary and the finite symbol that results. Registration precedes representation: the classical order (property → identical symbol) is replaced by interaction → registration → representation → model. Registration is selective (R ≠ I): the full interaction is never captured. Alphonic condition: the registration must satisfy the Alphonic constraint of the registering arrangement (R ⪰ αA); sub-threshold interactions produce no symbolic response. Registration (Re) is exogenous; derived symbol (Sd) is endogenous. Registration is not interpretation — it is the finite event that makes interpretation possible.

**Chapter 5: The Anatomy of a Registration**  
Unpacks Thick(R) = {m, A, C, τ, α, δ, H, A}:
- m (mark): the finite symbolic residue, the compressed outcome
- A (registering arrangement): the generonic geometry that shaped the registration; two arrangements, same interaction, different marks
- C (conditions): the environmental and contextual frame during the registration event
- τ (duration): the temporal extent of the registration process; registration has finite temporal depth
- α (Alphonic constraint): the minimum symbolic resolution required for a registration to be admitted
- δ (uncertainty): constitutive, broader than error bar; reflects the finite resolution of the registering arrangement
- H (provenance): the registration history of the arrangement; (m, H1) ≠ (m, H2) — same mark, different provenance, different registration
- A (admissibility): the criteria by which the registration is accepted into a symbolic domain

### Part III — Re-registration (Chapters 6–8)

**Chapter 6: Re-registration as Renewed Symbolic Instantiation**  
Defines re-registration formally: RB = GB(RA | CB, τB, αB, δB, HB, AB). Identity claim: RA ≠ RB (not the same registration in a different location, but a new finite instantiation). Admissibility relation: RA ~A RB (admissibly related within a domain). Re-registration is indexed by system and time. Modalities of re-registration: visual, auditory, tactile, machine-mediated, socially ratified. Copying is a chain of re-registrations R1 → R2 → ... → Rn, each producing a new finite instantiation. Every re-registration changes the registering system; the system after registration ≠ the system before.

**Chapter 7: Reading as Exogenous Measurement**  
Reading is the paradigm case of exogenous re-registration: the text provides externally instantiated symbols; the reader's generonic system produces new instantiations from them. Layered reading trajectory: familiar words compress rapidly (Rt → BS); unfamiliar words require extended re-registration at graphical, phonetic, lexical, semantic, and contextual levels. The expectation field P(Rt+1 | Rt, Rt-1, ..., Hreader) — reading is not passive consumption but an active measurement process governed by the reader's prior registration history. Reading conditions (lighting, fatigue, speed, familiarity) are registrational conditions, not contextual background.

**Chapter 8: Registration, Recognition, and Reconstruction**  
Distinguishes three compressed processes routinely conflated:
- Registration (I → R): the finite event of symbolic instantiation at the generonic boundary
- Recognition (R → BS): entry of the registration into an established symbolic basin; can be ambiguous (multiple competing basins); can fail (registration without recognition); can overshoot (hallucinated completion — recognition without complete registration)
- Reconstruction (R1, ..., Rn → T̂): the integration of sequential registrations into a coherent symbolic trajectory; T̂t+1 = Recon(T̂t, Rt+1, HA, Ct)
- Continuation: the trajectory is available for further re-registration or symbolic action
- Full sequence: registration → recognition → reconstruction → continuation

### Part IV — Symbolic Persistence Without Abstract Types (Chapters 9–11)

**Chapter 9: The Symbolic Lineage**  
The classical account of symbolic persistence posits an abstract type instantiated by material tokens. M14 replaces this with symbolic lineage: LS = (R1, R2, ..., Rn) where Ri ~A Rj — admissible relatedness, not identity to an abstract type. The lineage is made by use, not by resemblance to a fixed form. Basin membership: Ri ∈ BS — all members of a basin are admissibly related. Copying and drift: Ri+1 = Reg(Ri | Ai+1, Ci+1, Hi+1) — each re-registration has a slightly different trajectory from its predecessor. Admissibility is not fixed by the mark alone but is socially and instrumentally ratified — the politics of admissibility.

**Chapter 10: Re-reading and the Changing Reader**  
R(S, t1) ≠ R(S, t2): the same symbol registered at two different times by the same system produces two different registrations, because the registering system has changed. First reading changes second reading — the registration history HA is different. Re-reading is measurement continuation: the reader uses a history that now includes the first reading trajectory. Commentary changes basins: a critical apparatus alters the conditions for re-registration. The impossibility of a final reading — no registration is the last registration.

**Chapter 11: Memory, Recall, and Endogenous Reconstruction**  
Not every recurrence of a symbol is re-registration. Memory is endogenous reconstruction: HA → Srecalled, where the registering arrangement reconstructs from its own history rather than from an exogenous stimulus. Distinction: reading = exogenous re-registration; recall = endogenous reconstruction; expression = new external registration. Recognition can be retrospective: a recurrence triggers access to prior registration trajectory (what one suddenly remembers why a word sounds familiar). Oral traditions are re-registration technologies: each performance is a new registration governed by the tradition's admissibility criteria.

### Part V — Writing, Speech, and Communication (Chapters 12–15)

**Chapter 12: Writing as External Registration**  
Writing is the outward movement: T_writer → R_external. The writer's internal symbolic trajectory is projected into a physical line — an external registration designed for exogenous re-registration by others. What writing omits: trajectory thickness, registration conditions, duration, uncertainty — all compressed into the mark. The text is a new generonic object: once written, it exists as an external symbolic arrangement capable of producing re-registrations independently of the writer's intentions. Authorship is finite responsibility for what is made available for re-registration.

**Chapter 13: Speech and Listening**  
Speech is acoustic registration: T_speaker → acoustic signal → Rlistener. The acoustic stream is a generonic medium — temporally bounded, sequentially constrained, subject to noise. Temporal dependence: acoustic registrations decay; each segment is registered before the next arrives. Noise functions as registration degradation, increasing δ and potentially producing sub-Alphonic events. Conversation as coupled generonic dynamics: A1 → B1 → A2 → B2, where each utterance is a new registration event responding to the prior exogenous registration. Gesture and multimodal registration: speech is routinely accompanied by gestural and postural registration that modulates the acoustic signal's admissibility.

**Chapter 14: Why Meaning Is Not Transferred**  
The container metaphor (MA → message → MB) is replaced by the registration picture. Communication: TA → Rexternal → RB → TB. No common content passes from A to B; what passes is a chain of re-registrations subject to the conditions, arrangement, and history of each registering system. Constraint without transfer: the external registration constrains the space of admissible re-registrations without determining any particular TB. Misunderstanding is normal, not exceptional — it is the default condition when TA and TB differ in registration history. Repair is iterative measurement: the parties produce further registrations to narrow the space of admissible reconstruction.

**Chapter 15: The Reader Is the Author**  
Treader = F(R1, ..., Rn; Hreader, C, δ). The reader is the author of the trajectory they reconstruct from the text's registrations. Two forms of authorship: external authorship (Ae — what the writer inscribed) and local authorship (Al — what the reader reconstructs). The text resists: some reconstructions are inadmissible within the registration space defined by the text's marks. Authorship as finite responsibility: the external author is responsible for what they project into re-registration; the local author is responsible for what they reconstruct. Co-authorship across time: each commentary, edition, and re-reading is a new local authorship that enters the lineage.

### Part VI — Functional Symbolic Trajectories (Chapters 16–18)

**Chapter 16: From Registration to Functional Symbolic Trajectory**  
T = (R1, R2, ..., Rn; C, δ, H, A). A Functional Symbolic Trajectory is an ordered sequence of registrations with full contextual thickness. Each registration Rt is locally produced; the trajectory is relational rather than abstract. Trajectory thickness: at each step, Tt lies within a finite region Ωt rather than at a point. Functional halting: Halt(Tn | PA) = 1 when the trajectory satisfies admissibility conditions for completion. Registration lineage vs. trajectory: lineage tracks relatedness across time; trajectory tracks sequential local production.

**Chapter 17: Semantic Thickness and Overlapping Basins**  
Symbols do not have single meanings — they have basin membership profiles. Multiple basins: B(1)S, ..., B(k)S — a symbol may lie at the intersection of several basins simultaneously. Uncertainty constitutive: δS(t) = spread(ΩS(t)) — semantic uncertainty is not ignorance to be eliminated but a feature of finite symbolic resolution. Context does not eliminate history: the context shifts basin weights but cannot override registration provenance. Metaphor as basin coupling: BA ↔ BB — the metaphorical extension couples two basin structures through admissible registration relatedness. Semantic metrics: dsem(Ti, Tj | C, H, A) — distances between trajectories are defined relative to conditions, history, and admissibility, not by abstract semantic space. Thickness is a strength of the framework, not a deficiency.

**Chapter 18: Registration and Takens Reconstruction**  
The Takens delay embedding theorem applied to registration sequences. Measured sequence R1, ..., Rn; delay vector xt = (Rt, Rt-τ, ..., Rt-(m-1)τ); reconstructed trajectory M̂t = Recon(xt | HA, Ct). Reading as Takens reconstruction: each registered word provides a coordinate; the expectation field P(Rt+1 | xt, HA) is the local geometry of the reconstructed manifold. Attention as pairwise embedding: attentional mechanisms are re-registration operations that compute registration proximity in embedding space. Basins and training: during learning, basin structures are shaped by accumulated registration history. The logit decoder as registration generator: output tokens are new external registrations generated from endogenous reconstruction. Shared temporary attractor in dialogue: interlocutors whose registration trajectories converge enter a temporary shared basin.

### Part VII — Scientific Registration (Chapters 19–22)

**Chapter 19: Instruments, Data, and the First Registration**  
The instrument participates in the generonic event: the apparatus is the generonic geometry. Analogue response: the instrument produces a continuous response to the interaction; symbolic threshold: the registration event occurs when the response crosses the Alphonic threshold. Data are already registrations — they are not neutral observations but finite symbolic instantiations produced by specific arrangements under specific conditions. Nested registrations: instrument reading → observer re-registration → data record → statistical analysis. Observer's re-registration: the observer is a registering arrangement who re-registers the instrument's output. Measurement as interactional stabilisation: the registration stabilises aspects of the interaction into a finite symbol available for further re-registration.

**Chapter 20: Derived Symbols Are Not New Measurements**  
Taxonomy of scientific symbols: Re (exogenous registration — instrument output), Sd (endogenous derived symbol — computed from registrations), Im (model interpretation — world claim derived from model), Oc (world claim — claim about the state of affairs in the world). The Planck length example: the symbol '1.616 × 10⁻³⁵ m' is not a registration but a derived symbol derived from model interpretation derived from theory. Precision can conceal distance from measurement: a highly precise derived symbol may be remote from any registration. Prediction is not registration: model predictions are Sd or Im, not Re.

**Chapter 21: Registration, Model, and World**  
The model is not the world; the model is a finite symbolic arrangement that produces further derived symbols and interpretations. Model invariance ≠ world invariance: a symmetry in the model is a symmetry of the model's symbolic structure, not a discovered property of the world. Model as stabilising instrument: a validated model provides a stable arrangement for producing admissible derived symbols. When models become nouns: the risk of forgetting the registration lineage and treating Im as Re. The world beyond symbolic measurement: finite symbols do not exhaust the world. New measurements change the symbolic world: registrations produced by new instruments may be inadmissible with prior lineages, requiring lineage reconstruction.

**Chapter 22: Scientific Reading as Re-registration**  
A scientific paper is not the experiment — it is a compressed registration of registration chains. Figures as generonic compressions: graphs and tables are external registrations that compress trajectory information into two-dimensional marks. The authority of compressed prose: institutional forms of compression create admissibility within the scientific community. Reading critically: five questions the scientific reader must pose — (1) What was the registration event? (2) What was the registering arrangement? (3) What was omitted by compression? (4) What derived symbols are presented as registrations? (5) What is the provenance of the registration lineage? Reproducibility as lineage reconstruction: replication attempts to re-register the original measurement event under specified conditions. The reader as scientific instrument: scientific reading is a re-registration that produces new derived symbols and interpretations.

### Part VIII — Text-within-Text (Chapters 23–25)

**Chapter 23: The Symbolic Realm as Renewed Physical Instantiation**  
Text is not immaterial — it is renewed physical instantiation at each reading. The symbolic realm is not separate from the physical world but is constituted by finite physical events of registration and re-registration. Recursive generonic chain: a book refers to other books, which refer to other books — each reference is a new registration lineage. Digital symbols remain physical: electronic storage and transmission are physical registration processes. Text acts through re-registration, not through abstract meaning transmission. No final symbolic foundation: the chain of re-registrations has no absolute starting point within the symbolic realm. The role of exogenous renewal: the symbolic realm depends on continued physical acts of re-registration to persist.

**Chapter 24: Cultural Continuity as Re-registration**  
Culture does not store meanings unchanged — it sustains registration lineages through continued re-registration events. Tradition as basin: a cultural tradition is a basin structure maintained by a community's admissibility criteria. Archives preserve registrations, not the past itself — accessing an archive is a re-registration of prior marks, not time travel. Loss and silence: when re-registration chains break, the symbolic lineage ends; recovery is not restoration but new construction from surviving marks. Revival is new construction: a revived cultural practice is a new registration lineage related admissibly to a prior one. Cultural responsibility: communities are responsible for the registration conditions they sustain or destroy.

**Chapter 25: Translation as Cross-basin Re-registration**  
Translation: RA (source-language registration) → RB (target-language registration) where RA ≠ RB and the languages partition the registration space differently. No identical semantic cargo: what the translator transfers is not a meaning but an admissible re-registration in a different symbolic domain. Translator as reader and writer simultaneously: the translator performs exogenous re-registration (reading) and then external registration (writing). Provenance and visible uncertainty: a translation that makes its registration conditions explicit and acknowledges uncertainty is more epistemically honest than one that presents RB as if it were RA. Multiple admissible translations: for most source registrations, there are multiple admissible target registrations — the choice between them is a registrational decision governed by conditions and admissibility. Machine translation as automated re-registration: the model produces RB from a learned embedding of admissible source-target registration pairs.

### Part IX — Formal Foundations (Chapters 26–27)

**Chapter 26: A Preliminary Calculus of Registration**  
Ten formal sections:

**26.1 — Primary Registration**  
RA = GA(I | CA, τA, αA, δA, HA, AA)  
Where GA is the generonic operator, I is the interaction, and the remaining parameters are the apparatus, conditions, duration, Alphonic constraint, uncertainty, provenance, and admissibility of the registering arrangement A.

**26.2 — Re-registration**  
RB = GB(RA | CB, τB, αB, δB, HB, AB)  
The re-registration is produced by a distinct generonic operator GB operating on RA as its stimulus, not on the original interaction I.

**26.3 — Non-identity of Re-registration**  
RA ≠ RB; RA ~A RB  
Identity fails; admissible relatedness holds within the domain of admissibility A.

**26.4 — Registration Update**  
HA' = Update(HA, RA): the registration of RA modifies the registering arrangement's history. The arrangement after registration ≠ the arrangement before.

**26.5 — Recognition**  
Recog(R, BS) = 1 if R ∈ BS (basin membership); Recog(R, BS) = 0 otherwise. Basin membership is not binary in general — Recog(R, BS) ∈ [0,1] for graded admissibility.

**26.6 — Functional Symbolic Trajectory**  
T = (R1, R2, ..., Rn; C, δ, H, A)  
Halt(Tn | PA) = 1 when the trajectory satisfies admissibility conditions for completion under PA.

**26.7 — Endogenous Derivation**  
Sd = Derive(R1, ..., Rn | HA, PA): a derived symbol produced from prior registrations without a new exogenous event. Sd ∉ {Re} — it is not itself a registration.

**26.8 — Prediction and Comparison**  
P̂(Rt+1 | xt, HA): prediction is a derived symbol, not a registration. Comparison: dsem(Ti, Tj | C, H, A) — semantic distance between trajectories is defined relative to conditions, history, and admissibility.

**26.9 — Compression**  
Comp(R) = m (mark alone); Thick(R) = {m, A, C, τ, α, δ, H, A}; DR = Thick(R) \ Comp(R). Admissible compression: Comp_A(R) — the compressed form that retains unfoldable lineage within the domain A.

**26.10 — Finite Caution**  
All operators (GA, GB, Recog, Derive, Halt) are finite — they operate on finite symbolic arrangements under finite conditions with finite resolution. No operator produces exact results, infinite precision, or complete recovery of Thick(R) from Comp(R).

**Chapter 27: Principles of Symbolic Registration**  
Fifteen principles governing registration and re-registration:

**Principle I — Finite Instantiation:** Every symbol is a finite instantiation produced by a finite registering arrangement under finite conditions. No symbol is abstract, immaterial, or context-free.

**Principle II — Generonic Origin:** Every registration originates at a generonic boundary. There is no registration without a registering arrangement and an interaction that crosses its boundary.

**Principle III — Registration Non-identity (R ≠ I):** The registration is not identical to the interaction that produced it. The generonic boundary is not a transparent window.

**Principle IV — Re-registration Non-identity (RA ≠ RB):** A re-registration is a new finite instantiation, not a copy or transmission of the original. RA ≠ RB even when RA ~A RB.

**Principle V — Historical Relatedness:** Symbolic persistence is grounded in admissible relatedness within a lineage, not in identity to an abstract type. LS = (R1, ..., Rn) where Ri ~A Rj.

**Principle VI — Registration Precedes Representation:** The order of symbolic events is interaction → registration → representation → model. Representation cannot precede the registration that grounds it.

**Principle VII — Derived Symbols Are Not Registrations:** Symbols produced endogenously from prior registrations (Sd) are derived symbols, not registrations. Conflating Sd with Re is a source of systematic epistemic error.

**Principle VIII — Compression Removes Trajectory:** Compressing a registration to its mark (Comp(R) = m) removes the apparatus, conditions, duration, uncertainty, and provenance. Registration drag DR = Thick(R) \ Comp(R) is the cost of compression.

**Principle IX — Reading Is Re-registration:** Reading is not the reception of abstract meaning but exogenous re-registration of externally instantiated symbols. The reader is a registering arrangement producing new finite instantiations.

**Principle X — Meaning Is Reconstructed:** There is no common content that passes from writer to reader. Meaning is reconstructed locally by the reader's registering arrangement from the registration lineage available to it.

**Principle XI — Provenance Is Constitutive:** The provenance H of a registration is constitutive of its identity: (m, H1) ≠ (m, H2). Provenance cannot be stripped without producing a different symbol.

**Principle XII — Re-registration Changes System:** Every re-registration modifies the registering arrangement's history: HA' = Update(HA, RA). The arrangement after re-registration is not the arrangement before.

**Principle XIII — Model-World Distinction:** A model is a finite symbolic arrangement that produces derived symbols and interpretations. Model invariance is not world invariance. The world is not exhausted by any registration lineage.

**Principle XIV — Exogenous Renewal:** The persistence of symbolic objects — texts, institutions, traditions, corpora — depends on continued exogenous re-registration. Symbols that are not re-registered do not persist.

**Principle XV — Finite Admissibility:** Admissibility conditions (A) are finite, historically situated, and subject to revision. No admissibility condition is universal or permanent.

**Compact sequence:** I → R → L → T → M → R̂ ~ Rnew  
(Interaction → Registration → Lineage → Trajectory → Model → Reconstruction ~ new re-registration)

### Part X — Consequences and Applications (Chapters 28–32)

**Chapter 28: Implications for Linguistics and Philosophy of Language**  
Registration theory operates below the level of semantics: before a sign can mean, it must be registered. The sign is not a dyadic relation (signifier/signified) but a registration lineage — LS = (R1, ..., Rn). Reference as modelled continuation: referring is using a registration to continue a trajectory toward a target basin, not pointing to a pre-existing object. Rules as registered practices: linguistic rules are admissibility criteria — they are themselves registrations, sustained by the practice of applying them. Private language dissolved: no registration is private in the relevant sense — registrations are produced by finite arrangements with histories of exogenous interaction.

**Chapter 29: Implications for Education and Learning**  
Learning is basin construction through accumulated registration. Why isolated definitions fail: a definition given without registration context provides a compressed symbol without admissibility grounding — the student has Comp(R) without Thick(R). Repetition vs. variation: repeated identical re-registration reinforces a single basin entry; variation in registration conditions builds basin robustness and thickness. Error as basin evidence: a student's error is not a failure but evidence about the basin structure their registering arrangement has constructed. Reading instruction is registration instruction: the challenge is not decoding but building registration history sufficient to ground admissible re-registration. Technical education: the laboratory is a registration device for building the apparatus-specific registration history that makes disciplinary symbols admissible.

**Chapter 30: Implications for Artificial Language Systems**  
Machine registration: a neural network is a generonic arrangement that produces new registrations (output tokens) from learned registration history (training). Tokenisation as compression: the tokeniser compresses each input segment to a mark; the model operates on compressed registrations — DR is never zero. Training as basin shaping: the training process adjusts the model's internal basin structure through accumulated registration events. Endogenous output vs. exogenous checking: the model's output is endogenously derived (Sd), not a registration of the world (Re) — it requires exogenous checking to establish admissibility. Hallucination as unsupported continuation: the model produces output tokens that continue a trajectory into basins for which there is no registration support — continuation without registration grounding. Model as instrument: an LLM is a generonic arrangement, not a knower — it produces re-registrations of its training corpus, subject to Alphonic conditions and admissibility criteria imposed by its architecture and training.

**Chapter 31: Implications for Scientific Language**  
Scientific grammar can cross the registration/derived-symbol boundary silently. Vocabulary of lineage: disciplined scientific language distinguishes between what was registered (Re), what was derived (Sd), what is a model interpretation (Im), and what is a world claim (Oc). Five reformulation examples: for each, the original claim is identified as imprecise, and the registration-aware alternative is provided. Scientific disagreement taxonomy: disputes can be classified as disagreements about Re (what was registered), Sd (how derived symbols were computed), Im (model interpretation), or Oc (world claims). The taxonomy enables more productive resolution.

**Chapter 32: Registration in Geofinitism and Finite Symbolic Mechanics**  
Registration is the missing layer in Geofinitism — the account of how the finite symbolic material of FSM enters the symbolic domain. Connections:
- **Five Pillars:** P1 (Geometric Container) — the generonic boundary as the finite geometric container for registration; P2 (Approximations and Measurements) — registration as finite measurement event; P3 (Dynamic Flow) — the registration lineage as finite trajectory; P4 (Useful Fiction) — the model and derived symbol as useful fictional constructions; P5 (Finite Reality) — the world exceeds any registration lineage
- **Symbolic drag:** registration drag DR = Thick(R) \ Comp(R) is the formal account of symbolic drag as presented in M12 and ATT_82
- **FSTs:** registration is the event by which FSTs begin; each step in a Functional Symbolic Trajectory is a registration
- **Text-within-text:** M14 is the generonic grounding for the text-within-text phenomenon documented in P21
- **TBT:** the Takens-Based Transformer reconstructs registration trajectories from finite measured sequences (Ch. 18)
- **Foundational contribution (7 items):** (1) names the missing event; (2) provides the generonic account of symbolic entry; (3) formalises non-identity of re-registration; (4) replaces type/token with lineage; (5) grounds FSTs in finite events; (6) provides the measurement-theoretic account of reading; (7) connects Geofinitism to the philosophy of language, philosophy of science, and education theory
