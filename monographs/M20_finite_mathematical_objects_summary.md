## Core Claim

A finite symbolic trajectory is best understood not as a completed classical object but as a nodal rope: a finite ordered sequence of beads and operational links, each carrying the uncertainty, history, provenance, and consensual permissions required at the selected alphonic boundary. Classical mathematical objects — matrices, complex planes, period lattices, elliptic curves, Riemann surfaces, formal proofs — cannot be transferred directly into FSM. Each must first be unfolded into the finite sequential symbolic operations through which a particular calculation actually enacts them. The resulting rope, and not the completed classical object, is what is offered for placement on a finite three-dimensional spherical construction.

The principal conjecture — the Spherical Nodal-Rope Conjecture — states in its strong reconstructive form that for every operationally admissible finite symbolic trajectory at resolution α, there exist a finite spherical construction S_{α,R}, a folding Φ_α, and a finite decoder D_α such that the decoder recovers an operationally equivalent rope while preserving all declared bead, link, order, distinction, provenance, and uncertainty conditions. This is an open conjecture. It may be false. Its value lies both in what a successful construction would certify (a finite FSM realisation proof) and in what the failure conditions identify (the precise symbolic, uncertainty, or reconstruction constraints that resist three-dimensional spherical encoding).

## New and Extended FSM Concepts

| Concept | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Bead (full form) | Smallest admitted FSM state: B_i = (σ_i, t_i, H_i, U_i, C_i, Γ_i). Not a bare token — carries symbol, operational order, history requirement, uncertainty neighbourhood, consensual permission, and admissible continuations. None of these entries is presumed exact beyond the alphonic limit |
| Alphonic neighbourhood | U_i = B(c_i, r_i), r_i ≥ α. Finite geometric carrier asserting every registration occupies a non-zero neighbourhood. The centre is a representative location under the chosen construction, not an exact semantic essence |
| Lexil | A finite symbolic bead currently functioning chiefly as a lexical or developed linguistic unit. Excluded transfer: claim that words possess fixed context-free meanings. Lexils commonly carry comparatively broad semantic neighbourhoods in a rope |
| Nexil | A finite symbolic bead or link currently functioning chiefly as an operational constraint or relation. Excluded transfer: claim that mathematical signs are intrinsically unambiguous. Nexils commonly narrow the admissible next operations |
| Local semantic uncertainty decomposition | U_i = U_i^ref × U_i^role × U_i^hist × U_i^branch × U_i^next. Uncertainty belongs to the bead and cannot be recovered reliably from the visible symbol alone. The product sign is an organisational device until the factors and interactions are experimentally specified |
| Link (full form) | E_i = (o_i, Δt_i, H_i^E, U_i^E, C_i^E). Records the finite operation by which one registered state gives permission for another: formal inference, matrix multiplication, a substitution, a choice of branch, retrieval of a definition, or a linguistic transition. Not mere visual adjacency or unexplained arrow |
| Operational time | The index i supplies an operational order that need not presume a completed real-valued time axis. Minimum condition: the enacted sequence can be distinguished sufficiently to reproduce the calculation at the chosen resolution |
| Nodal rope | R_α(P): alternating finite sequence of beads and links representing the operational unfolding of a symbolic performance at resolution α. Its length is relative to the available lattice of the agent performing or reconstructing it. The adjective flexible means a later spatial realisation may bend the rope without changing its operational order or declared relations |
| Rope equivalence | R_α(P) ≡_{α,C} R'_α(P): typed finite equivalence whose tests must accompany its use; not exact bead-for-bead identity but indistinguishability under declared resolution, consensus conditions, terminal registration, and permitted reconstruction tests |
| Nested rope call | B_i ↝ R_α(Q): bead invokes a previously validated finite procedure Q. A full audit recursively unfolds all such calls until the chosen alphonic and consensual boundary is reached. Compressed only when the reconstruction contract declares Q already available |
| Finite spherical construction | S_{α,R}: finite shell of alphonic neighbourhoods satisfying a declared radial band and coverage test. Not the completed exact point-set sphere {x ∈ ℝ³ : ‖x‖ = R}. Vacant regions are not automatically failures — failure depends on the proposed function of the realisation |
| Candidate folding Φ_α | Map from nodal rope to finite spherical construction assigning each bead a finite shell neighbourhood and each link a finite route. Admitted only relative to stated tests: bead images exist, operational precedence is recoverable, distinct states remain distinguishable, crossings do not create undeclared connections, branch choices and nested calls retain provenance |
| Decoder D_α | Finite decoding procedure satisfying D_α(Φ_α(R_α(P))) ≡_{α,C} R_α(P). Central safeguard against decorative geometry: without a tested decoder the sphere is merely an illustration; with one, the spherical object becomes a finite alternative registration of the symbolic trajectory |
| FSM realisation certificate | Π_FSM(P, α, C) = (R_α, S_{α,R}, Φ_α, D_α, T_{α,C}). Finite constructive certificate that a declared symbolic trajectory has been unfolded, spatially realised, and reconstructed without loss beyond the accepted boundary. Certifies only what its types state — not a replacement for classical proof of a theorem |
| Spherical Nodal-Rope Conjecture (weak form) | For every finite symbolic trajectory P and stated alphonic resolution α, there exists a finite three-dimensional spherical construction on which R_α(P) can be placed with finite error. Follows too readily if arbitrary enlargement and mere placement are allowed; retained only as a preliminary geometric question |
| Spherical Nodal-Rope Conjecture (strong reconstructive form) | For every operationally admissible P at resolution α, there exist finite parameters R, N, ε_R, a spherical construction S_{α,R}, a folding Φ_α, and a finite decoder D_α such that Eq. 14.2 holds and all declared bead, link, order, distinction, provenance, and uncertainty conditions are preserved. The substantive open conjecture; may be false |
| Critical correction | Required sequence: classical compressed object → finite operational unfolding → nodal rope → three-dimensional spherical test. The Riemann surface may constrain the classical rules being unfolded; it is not the object mapped directly to the FSM sphere. Classical mathematics supplies compressed operational constraints; FSM asks how those constraints are finitely enacted |
| Five failure conditions | (1) Loss of order: two distinct operational orders decode to the same unmarked route; (2) Collision: uncertainty neighbourhoods overlap so required beads or links cannot be distinguished at the selected α; (3) Hidden decompression: decoder calls an undeclared external theorem, dictionary, or algorithm; (4) Changing the object: folding replaces steps without reconstruction permission; (5) Failure of the universal conjecture: an admissible finite rope for which no finite spherical construction and decoder preserves declared constraints |
| Classical Object Unfolding Register | Appendix B: systematic table of classical objects (matrix, tensor, complex plane, period lattice, complex torus, elliptic function, elliptic curve, Riemann surface sheet, branch point, point at infinity, formal proof) with their compressed permissions, finite unfolding requirements, and spherical test objects |
| Trajectory Boundary Marker | App E records the deliberate stopping point: projection layers, delay coordinates, Takens-type embeddings, integer lattices in three dimensions, retinal projection, and Lexil trajectories in Nexil-constrained spaces are reserved for a subsequent volume (TM-FSM-2026-08-18) |
