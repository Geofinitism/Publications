## Overview

M09 is a systematic application of Geofinitism to the foundations of mathematics — taking linear algebra, eigenvalue theory, algebraic solvability, and polynomial root-finding as its domain and demonstrating that all of these are best understood as **finite calculation trajectories** whose geometry can be examined directly. The central move is the same as the one that runs through the entire FSM corpus: strip away the compressed notation, unfold the actual sequential process, and apply phase-space tools.

The monograph's three propositions (§5.1) crystallise the argument:

> 1. **Process Primacy**: Mathematical objects are not static entities but compressed records of finite processes. Matrices compress instruction sequences; eigenvalues compress stabilisation phenomena; algebraic formulas compress calculation trajectories.
> 2. **Trajectory Geometry**: The unfolding of these compressed processes reveals genuine geometric structure — calculation trajectories that can be examined using the same phase-space techniques developed for physical systems.
> 3. **Descriptor Status**: Classical mathematical objects function as descriptors of these trajectories. They are useful fictions that summarise invariant or emergent features of the underlying sequential processes.

The decisive methodological claim is in Remark 1.1: "The FSET establishes that Takens' theorem is a limiting case of finite-symbol embedding: T = lim_{ε→0} F_ε. This inverts the traditional proof hierarchy: **finite representability is the ground; continuous smoothness is an idealisation.**"

## Chapter 1 — Eigenvalues as Stabilised Descriptors

### The Core Reinterpretation

Conventional linear algebra treats matrices, eigenvalues, and eigenvectors as primary objects. M09 insists instead on the **unfolded sequence of explicit calculations** as the primary object. Eigenvalues are not pre-existing Platonic properties; they are **stabilised descriptors** — invariants that emerge after sufficient sequential operations have been performed.

### Key Formal Definitions

**Definition 1.1 (Calculation State):** S_t = (v₁(t), v₂(t), ..., v_{n_t}(t)) — the tuple of all intermediate numerical values at time step t.

**Definition 1.2 (Elemental Operation):** o: ℝ×ℝ → ℝ from {+, −, ×, ÷}.

**Definition 1.3 (Calculation Trajectory):** Γ = S₀ →o1 S₁ →o2 S₂ →o3 ··· →oT S_T — the finite sequence of states generated by applying a deterministic sequence of elemental operations.

**Definition 1.4 (FST):** γ_s = T(s; C, H_s, R, α, δ) — the full FST framework applied to calculation sequences, where C is context, H_s is history, R is reconstruction operator, α is resolution, and δ is uncertainty bound.

Three descriptor roles for eigenvalues are distinguished:
- **Invariant** (Def 1.5): I(A_k) = I(A₀) for all k — eigenvalues preserved under every similarity transformation
- **Emergent Quantity** (Def 1.6): becomes visible only after sufficient steps — the diagonal entries of QR iteration stabilise at eigenvalues
- **Descriptor** (Def 1.7): characterises the entire trajectory or its terminal behaviour

### The LRC Circuit as Demonstration

The series LRC circuit with state matrix A = [[-R/L, -1/L], [1/C, 0]] provides the running example. The state variables I_L and V_C are themselves **measured quantities with finite resolution and uncertainty**. The matrix A is a symbolic compression of the differential equations; the eigenvalue computation is a finite process that generates stabilised descriptors from this compression.

### QR Algorithm as Explicit Finite Time Series

Rather than solving the characteristic polynomial (impossible in radicals for degree ≥ 5), the QR algorithm generates A_k+1 = R_k Q_k — an explicit finite time series:

A₀ → A₁ → A₂ → ··· → A_N

For the overdamped LRC case (L = 1, C = 1, R = 3), A₀ = [[-3, -1],[1, 0]], with eigenvalues λ₁ = (-3+√5)/2 ≈ -0.382, λ₂ = (-3-√5)/2 ≈ -2.618. After 10 QR iterations the sub-diagonal element is driven to numerical zero and the diagonal stabilises at the eigenvalues. The process did not "discover" eigenvalues by solving an equation abstractly — it **generated them as stable descriptors** once the time series of matrix transformations had unfolded far enough.

### Finite-Symbol Embedding Theorem (Theorem 1.1)

The FSET (Haylett 2025, cited as M02 and P04) justifies delay embedding of finite symbolic sequences:

> Let Γ = {s_t}^T_{t=1} be a finite sequence from a finite alphabet S, generated by an underlying deterministic dynamical system F: M → M with measurement function h: M → S. Under **Observational Separation** (h(x) ≠ h(y) for distinct x, y) and **Finite Complexity** (m < ∞), there exists d ≤ 2m+1 and delay τ such that Φ_{d,τ}(s_t) = (s_t, s_{t-τ}, ..., s_{t-(d-1)τ}) is injective up to resolution ε.

**Remark 1.1:** T = lim_{ε→0} F_ε. This inverts the traditional proof hierarchy: **finite representability is the ground; continuous smoothness is an idealisation.**

**Definition 1.8 (Computational Phase-Space Embedding):** For a computational trajectory Γ = (v₁, v₂, ..., v_T) of scalar values: Φ_{d,τ}(v_t) = (v_t, v_{t-τ}, ..., v_{t-(d-1)τ}) ∈ ℝ^d.

**Definition 1.9 (Finite Stability):** A system is finitely stable if: (1) the physical trajectory remains within bounded measurement bounds; (2) the computational trajectory used to analyse it converges to its stabilised descriptors within a finite, predetermined number of steps.

## Chapter 2 — Matrices as Temporary Containers

Chapter 2 descends one layer deeper than Chapter 1 — from the sequence of whole matrices A₀ → A₁ → ··· to the **granular sequence of elemental calculations** themselves.

A matrix y = Ax is, at root, a compact instruction set: expanding a 2×2 multiplication reveals four multiplications and two additions. The matrix provides the blueprint; the sequence of elemental calculations is the actual construction process. The geometric content — rotation, scaling, shear — emerges from the **ordered accumulation** of many individual scalings and additions, not magically from the matrix symbol.

**Algorithm 2 (Unfolded Matrix Multiplication)** provides pseudocode that records the full trajectory Γ as a list of (i, j, k, MULT/ADD, operand₁, operand₂, result) tuples. This trajectory can then be treated as a dynamical signal and embedded in phase space — turning the same tools used on physical LRC circuits back onto the computational process itself.

**Eigenvalues as late-emerging descriptors**: within the granular view, eigenvalues do not exist as ready-made objects at the calculation's start. They become visible only after sufficient elemental operations have been executed and the running totals have aligned. This ordering — detailed sequential operations first, compact descriptors later — **reverses the usual pedagogical presentation** and restores a more natural order of understanding.

## Chapter 3 — Algebraic Unsolvability as Trajectory Geometry

Chapter 3 extends the calculation-trajectory framework to purely algebraic tasks: the search for polynomial roots by radicals.

**Definition 3.1 (Radical Trajectory):** A calculation trajectory is a radical trajectory if every operation is drawn from ℛ = {+, −, ×, ÷, ⁿ√·} and the sequence length is finite.

For degree ≤ 4, radical trajectories exist: the trajectory of intermediate values remains controllable — entanglements that cannot be undone by further scalings, additions, or root extractions do not arise. Each new radical introduces a controlled branching or scaling that remains reversible within the same formal language.

For degree ≥ 5, **no general finite radical sequence exists**. The reason, viewed as calculation trajectories: the sequence of operations develops folding, stretching, and entanglement characteristic of nonlinear dynamical systems. The trajectory would need to pass through regions whose geometry lies outside the transformations generatable by radicals — generating an infinite regress.

**The Galois Connection (§3.4.1):** The non-solvability of S₅ (the Galois group of the general quintic) manifests, in calculation-trajectory terms, as the impossibility of constructing a radical trajectory that can navigate the required geometric relations.

**Phase-space reading of Abel's theorem (§3.5):** Apply delay embedding to the stream of intermediate numerical values. For solvable low-degree cases, the reconstructed attractor remains simple — paths that can be traversed by the geometric operations corresponding to radicals. For the quintic, the phase portrait develops complex folding and mixing. The dynamics of the calculation process itself become too entangled.

## Chapter 4 — Phase Space Visualizations

Chapter 4 presents delay-embedded phase portraits of calculation sequences, using d = 2, τ = 1 (False Nearest Neighbours for d; Average Mutual Information for optimal τ).

**Quadratic x² - 5x + 6 = 0** (roots x = 2, 3): 7-step sequence; compact trajectory with few turns. Clean termination at the roots.

**Cubic x³ - 6x - 6 = 0** (Cardano): 10-step sequence; visibly longer trajectory with multiple distinct stages (discriminant, cube roots u and v, quadratic factor for remaining roots).

**Quintic x⁵ - x - 1 = 0** (Newton's method from x₀ = 1): extended iterative trajectory, spiraling before convergence to x ≈ 1.1673; markedly more dynamically rich than the radical-based trajectories.

The visualizations show a clear progression: low-degree → short simple paths; cubic → increased length and branching; quintic numerical iteration → extended geometry. The obstruction to solving higher-degree polynomials by radicals is **visible at the level of the calculation process itself**.

## Chapter 5 — Synthesis and Future Work

**Three propositions (§5.1):** Process Primacy; Trajectory Geometry; Descriptor Status (see Overview above).

**Role of Idealisation (§5.2.2):** Geofinitism does not reject classical idealisations but reframes them as useful fictions. The FSET formalises the relationship: classical Takens embedding is the limit of finite-symbol embedding as resolution → 0.

**Measurement as Foundation (§5.2.3):** Every mathematical symbol carries measurement provenance, resolution bounds, and irreducible uncertainty. This measurement-first approach provides a foundation for mathematics grounded in finite experience rather than Platonic abstractions.

**Connections to the Geofinitism programme (§5.3):**
- **FSM**: trajectories and descriptors here are specific instances of the broader FSM programme (M07–M08, ATT_52, ATT_63–65)
- **Language Dynamics**: phase-space embedding of computational sequences parallels language-as-NDS (P05)
- **Takens-Haylett Theorem**: FSET provides formal licence for applying Takens to discrete symbolic systems (P04, M02)
- **Generon Framework**: numbers and mathematical objects as generons — processes that when executed produce measured results (ATT_77, ATT_23)

**Open questions (§5.4):** Formalising calculation trajectory invariants (trace, determinant, condition number, convergence rates as trajectory properties); phase-space embedding of different algorithms (QR vs Jacobi vs power iteration); Finite Stability Certificates; connections to algebraic complexity theory; MARINA language generation as same underlying process — finite symbolic trajectory generation (P01).

## Significance for the School

M09 occupies a distinctive position in the corpus: it is the first monograph to take a classical mathematical domain — linear algebra and algebraic solvability — and work through it systematically from Geofinitist first principles, producing new formal definitions and a worked example at every level. Where earlier FSM monographs (M07–M08) built the formal apparatus of FSM from the ground up, M09 applies that apparatus to concrete mathematical content and shows what it yields.

Three results are particularly notable. First, the reframing of eigenvalues as stabilised emergent descriptors (Defs 1.5–1.7) gives precise vocabulary to something that has been implicit across many ATT essays (ATT_51–54, ATT_65) and the FSM papers: that mathematical objects are compression endpoints, not starting points. Second, the treatment of Abel's impossibility theorem as a trajectory geometry result — the quintic trajectory develops folding and entanglement that escapes the expressible class — offers an intuitive, first-principles understanding of a result that usually requires Galois theory as a prerequisite. Third, the explicit demonstration that Takens embedding applies not only to physical systems but to **the computational processes used to study physical systems** closes the loop the FSET opened: the same geometric tools apply at every level of the hierarchy.

The bibliography explicitly cites ATT_08, ATT_49, ATT_52, ATT_77, ATT_81, M01, M02, M07, P01, P04, P05, P06, P14 — placing M09 squarely within the existing corpus while extending it into new mathematical territory. The Generon Framework connection (§5.3) — numbers as generons, the calculation trajectory as a generonic process — is the deepest link to the foundational Geofinitism ontology.
