## Core Claim

Once a physical process has crossed a measurement boundary and become a registered symbolic stream, all subsequent operations — sampling, ordering, delay construction, matrix formation, decomposition, projection, classification, interpretation, and exposition — are themselves finite symbolic operations. The analyser is therefore not external to the symbolic domain. Analysis is another symbolic dynamics acting upon, and interacting with, the symbolic trajectory under study.

This monograph develops *Finite Symbolic Dynamics* (FSD), a proposed subdiscipline within Finite Symbolic Mechanics, whose purpose in Part I is not to offer a finished formal system but to establish a coherent conceptual foundation. The central commitments are: trajectory is registered before geometry is constructed; the analytical procedure is endogenous to the symbolic world; higher matrix or index order does not by itself establish higher measured physical dimensionality; compression removes trajectory and a compressed result is not identical to the trajectory from which it was derived; and provenance is part of the dynamical structure, not supplementary metadata. The framework proposes ten initial principles, a provisional definition, and a research programme for subsequent parts.

## Initial Principles of Finite Symbolic Dynamics

| Principle | Statement |
|-----------|-----------|
| 1. Finite Registration | All admissible symbolic states are finite registrations |
| 2. Trajectory Precedes Constructed Geometry | A registered sequence precedes any phase geometry constructed from it |
| 3. Analysis Is Endogenous | Analytical operations performed after registration are themselves finite symbolic transformations |
| 4. Matrix Order Is Not Physical Dimension | Higher relational or index order does not by itself establish higher measured physical dimensionality |
| 5. Transformations Carry Provenance | A symbolic result retains dependence upon the trajectory of transformations by which it was generated |
| 6. Compression Is Not Identity | A compressed representation is not identical to the trajectory from which it was derived |
| 7. Phase Space Is Constructed | A symbolic phase space is a relational construction generated under a stated transformation |
| 8. Analytical Systems May Be Coupled | The analyser may change in response to the symbolic trajectory and may therefore constitute a second interacting dynamical system |
| 9. Symbolic Outputs Become Future Inputs | A result, once registered, may enter subsequent symbolic trajectories and influence later dynamics |
| 10. Operations Should Remain Linguistically Visible | Where possible, the language of exposition should preserve the operations that generated a representation rather than presenting the resulting noun as self-explanatory |
