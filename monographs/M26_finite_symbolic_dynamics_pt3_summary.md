# M26 — Finite Symbolic Dynamics: Function, Resolution, and the Terminus of Symbolic Growth

**Running title:** Finite Symbolic Dynamics  
**Subtitle:** Part III: Toward a Stable Operational Grammar for Finite Dynamics  
**Author:** Kevin R. Haylett, Manchester, UK  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Pages:** 49

## Core Claim

A Functional Symbolic Trajectory earns its growth by performing declared operations under identifiable conditions. Its local stopping condition is not exhaustion of possible distinctions but *functional sufficiency* — the point at which further symbolic elaboration is presently unnecessary for the declared task. Two complementary termini govern finite symbolic work:

- **Alphonic terminus** (α): a hard boundary where no finer admissible distinction is available within the declared symbolic system.
- **Functional terminus** (Γ\*_functional): an elastic boundary where further elaboration exceeds present functional need and may be reopened by new measurement, failed function, or changed operating conditions.

The central question of the monograph is: *What makes a symbolic trajectory functional, and what permits it to stop growing?*

## Part I — Functional Symbolic Trajectories and Their Termination (Chapters 1–6)

**Chapter 1: What Makes a Symbolic Trajectory Functional**  
FSD begins from the finite registered state: S_i ∈ S_finite \[1.1\]. A trajectory S_0→S_1→...→S_n \[1.2\] grows through declared transformations. Not every subdivision is warranted (Δx → Δx/2 → Δx/4... \[1.3\]). A trajectory of trajectories Γ_0→Γ_1→Γ_2→... \[1.4\] must respect the Alphonic floor Δx ≥ α \[1.5\]. When Δ(symbolic structure) >> Δ(functional gain) \[1.6\], growth is flagged. Key claim: "The growth of the descriptive FST is itself a dynamical observable." \[1.7\]

**Chapter 2: The Container FST and Its Derivatives**  
A research programme can itself be represented as an accumulating trajectory — the Container FST: Γ_C = {S_0, T_1, S_1, ..., T_n, S_n} \[2.1\]. A derived FST D_j = C(Γ_C^[a:b]) \[2.2\] compresses a window of the container; crucially D_j ≠ Γ_C^[a:b] \[2.3\] — the compression is not the segment. Derived FSTs are inputs to the next stage: D_j → Γ_{j+1} \[2.4\].

**Chapter 3: Functional Adequacy of a Symbolic Model**  
The model-reality relation is functional and trajectory-mediated: M ⟺^Γ R \[3.1\]; the measured state is produced by M_t →^Γ M̂_{t+1} \[3.2\] and compared to M_{t+1} \[3.3\]. Larger trajectories are not automatically more adequate: |Γ_2| > |Γ_1| does NOT imply F(Γ_2) > F(Γ_1) \[3.4–3.5\].

**Chapter 4: The Two Termini**  
The Alphonic terminus marks the limit of first-order distinction: ...→4α→2α→α→\[no finer admissible distinction\] \[4.1\]. The functional terminus Γ→Γ\* \[4.2\] marks the elastic stopping point: the locally sufficient trajectory. A new measurement can reopen it: M_new: Γ\*→Γ\*' \[4.4\]. Central principle: "The possibility of making a distinction is not sufficient reason to make it." \[4.5\]

**Chapter 5: The Epicycle Problem and the Stability of Generating Commitments**  
When local additions to a framework improve local description while straining global structural coherence, the result is an epicyclic proliferation. The appropriate response is to inspect the generating commitments rather than add another partition. Complexity growth is diagnostic, not merely descriptive.

**Chapter 6: Functional Sufficiency and the Basin of Stabilisation**  
A stable conceptual basin functions analogously to an attractor basin: a framework occupies a region in which local FSTs can reach functional termini and be reopened without structural collapse. Functional sufficiency is the condition for a locally closed trajectory that remains globally open.

## Part II — Finite Number Representation as a Functional Test Case (Chapters 7–12)

**Chapter 7: Numerical Representation as a Functional Stress Test**  
Arithmetic is treated as an FSD test case because counting can be instantiated through visibly finite physical interactions. A count: {B_1,...,B_n}→n \[7.1\]; the primitive explanatory language: distinguish→count→group→shift→register \[7.2\]. A finite interactional identity state: ℑ_n →^𝒪 ℑ_{n+1} \[7.3\].

**Chapter 8: Base Is Not Resolution**  
A finite positional register: R_{b,m,e} = (d_0,...,d_{m-1};b,m,e) \[8.3\] where b is base, m is character depth, e is scale of most significant position. The represented value: 𝒱(R_{b,m,e}) = Σ_{j=0}^{m-1} d_j b^{e-j} \[8.4\]. Smallest adjacent distinction: Δ_{b,m,e} = b^{e-m+1} \[8.5\]. Central result: numerical resolution = f(base, character depth, scale) \[8.6\]. Working convention: b=10, m=9 \[8.7\]. The rejected equivalence: fixed base + indefinite character extension ≡ finite measured number \[8.2\].

**Chapter 9: Numerical Alphons, Nexils, and Higher-Order Representation**  
A numerical Alphonic distinction at position j: a_j^(b) ∈ 𝒜_b \[9.1\]; admissible change: a_j^(b) →^{admissible change} a_j'^(b) \[9.2\]. A complete numerical register is a higher-order symbolic object: ℛ_{b,m,e} = [a_0^(b),...,a_{m-1}^(b)] \[9.3\]. A Nexil measured at the Alphonic context becomes: N_α(M_t) →^{ℛ_{b,m,e}} ℛ_{b,m,e}(N_α) \[9.6\]. The possibility of a "numerical Nexil" notation 𝒩_t = (ℛ_{b,m,e}, 𝒰_t, 𝒫_t, 𝒜_t) \[9.7\] is left open pending demonstrated repeated work.

**Chapter 10: Symbolic Shift Operational Arithmetic**  
Arithmetic as finite state transition: R_n = [d_0,...,d_{m-1}]_n \[10.1\]; an operation T acts as R_n →^T R_{n+1} \[10.2\]. Successor, carry, and shift are explicit symbolic operations: d_j ↦ d_j+1 (if d_j < b−1) \[10.3\]; at saturation: b−1 ↦ 0 and carry via σ_j: (d_j,d_{j-1}) ↦ (0,d_{j-1}+1) \[10.5\]. A chain of operations is an FST: R_0 →^{T_1} R_1 →^{T_2} R_2 →... \[10.6\]. Substrate-independent functional equivalence: φ_{AB}(R_n^{(A)}) = R_n^{(B)} \[10.7\].

**Chapter 11: Carry, Overflow, Scale Shift, and Loss of Distinction**  
A saturated nine-character register: R=[9,9,9,9,9,9,9,9,9] \[11.1\] forces a declared transition. Scale shift: 9.99999999×10^e →^{+Δ} 1.00000000×10^{e+1} \[11.2\] preserves character depth while changing least distinction from 10^{e-8} \[11.3\] to 10^{e-7} \[11.4\]. Symbolic collision: Π_{m'→m}(x) = Π_{m'→m}(y) \[11.6\] when two states distinguishable at depth m' become indistinguishable at working depth m; residue: ρ_{m,m'} = 𝒱(R_{b,m',e}) − 𝒱(Π_{m'→m} R_{b,m',e}) \[11.7\]. Foundational rule: "symbolically generated character depth does not retroactively increase measurement depth." \[11.8\]

**Chapter 12: Higher-Order Arithmetic and the Growth of Numerical FSTs**  
Composed operations produce a numerical FST: Γ_N = {N_0,T_1,N_1,...,T_n,N_n} \[12.2\]. State descriptor: N_i = (R_{b,m,e_i}, U_i, P_i, A_i) \[12.3\]; update N_{i+1} = T_i(N_i) \[12.4\] subject to R_{b,m,e_i}→R_{b,m,e_{i+1}} \[12.5\]. Local Dynexil analysis via delay bundle: D_t^{(k,τ)} = [N_t, N_{t-τ},...,N_{t-kτ}]_{U,P} \[12.6\]. Finite recurrence: if N_i = N_j (i<j) under a deterministic rule, then N_{i+r} = N_{j+r} \[12.7–8\] — the cycle is a property of the finite symbolic dynamics, not an artefact.

## Part III — Functional Economy and Readiness for External Application (Chapters 13–20)

**Chapter 13: Functional Economy as a Constraint on Language**  
A term earns stability when it compresses multiple functional trajectories without hiding distinctions required for function \[13.1\]. The elastic functional region: over-compression ↔ \[functional region\] ↔ over-elaboration \[13.2\]. Vocabulary is a dynamical resource: a new term changes not only the list of symbols but the number of possible paths through the symbolic landscape.

**Chapter 14: A Provisional Functional Grammar of Finite Symbolic Dynamics**  
The minimal operational sequence: register→relate→transform→retain provenance→test function→stop or continue \[14.1\]. The present minimal vocabulary comprises: FSM (foundational constraints), Generonic boundary and Transfictor (sensing-to-symbol operation), Nexil (first-order finite symbolic registration from measurement at the Alphonic context), FSD (evolution and relational organisation of registrations), FST (identifiable functional path through symbolic states), Dynexil (bounded local dynamical projection or delay-structured descriptor). This list is a working grammar, not a protected ontology; terms may be merged, removed, or restricted \[14.3\].

**Chapter 15: Functional Stability Across Transformations**  
A relational feature K is more stable when K(Γ^(1)) ~ K(Γ^(2)) ~ ... ~ K(Γ^(q)) \[15.2\] across admissible representations. Resolution sensitivity measure: S_R(m) = d(Γ_m, Γ_{m+1}) \[15.4\] where d is a declared finite trajectory comparison. For nonlinear trajectories, the First Registered Distinction Time provides a finite alternative to infinitesimal separation: T_D = min{t : ℛ(X_t) ≠ ℛ(Y_t)} \[15.5\].

**Chapter 16: Conditions for Application to an External Dynamical System**  
Readiness condition: the framework can relate declared exogenous registrations, finite transformations, local trajectory descriptions, and downstream comparisons without first requiring uncontrolled lexical growth. The application chain: exogenous interaction → finite registration → finite numerical representation → endogenous transformation → ... \[16.1\]. No hidden infinite precision: the working trajectory applies the admitted numerical registration at each declared transition N_i →^{T_i} N_{i+1}^{candidate} →^{ℛ_{10,9,e}} N_{i+1} \[16.2\]. For the Navier–Stokes programme: spatial h ≥ α \[16.4\], numerical b=10, m=9 \[16.5\]; combined state: S_i^n = (local registered variables, α, R_{10,9,e}, U_i^n, P_i^n, A_i^n) \[16.6\].

**Chapter 17: Preparing the Navier–Stokes Stress Test**  
Navier–Stokes is identified as the next appropriate major application because its classical formulation begins from a continuum field requiring smoothness, differentiation, and possible singular behaviour — all targets for FSM/FSD reformulation. The goal is not to solve the Millennium Prize problem but to determine what problem remains when continuum assumptions are replaced by finite admissibility conditions. The study constitutes a two-dimensional stress test: physical/computational (stable, reproducible trajectories?) and linguistic/framework-level (no uncontrolled conceptual proliferation?) \[17.1–2\].

**Chapter 18: Research Questions Arising from Part III**  
Ten research questions are identified: (1) functional termination with stable stopping conditions; (2) framework growth — distinguishing productive elaboration from epicyclic proliferation; (3) base, character depth, and scale as independent declared properties; (4) substrate independence of arithmetic relations; (5) whether a specialised numerical Nexil term earns its place; (6) whether the Dynexil retains useful local function across domains; (7) macroscopic stability across numerical depth variations; (8) nonlinear separation via finite trajectory measures; (9) Navier–Stokes investigation without conceptual explosion; (10) "Can FSD remain functionally compact as the range of its applications grows?" \[18.1\].

**Chapter 19: Provisional Principles of Part III**  
Fifteen working principles, including: (1) Finite registration does not itself terminate symbolic growth; (2) Symbolic growth is itself observable (as an FST); (3) Function constrains elaboration; (4) Alphonic and functional termini are different; (5) Functional termination is elastic; (6) Complexity growth is diagnostic; (7) Base is not resolution; (8) Character depth is part of numerical provenance; (9) Scale shifts change resolving power; (10) Computation does not create measurement depth; (11) Arithmetic is an operational symbolic trajectory; (12) Higher symbolic order is not higher physical dimension; (13) Dynexil remains local; (14) Vocabulary remains reducible; (15) External application is a stress test of the grammar.

**Chapter 20: Conclusion — From Unlimited Description to Functional Sufficiency**  
Two complementary termini: measurement refinement → α; symbolic elaboration → Γ\*_functional \[20.1\]. The Navier–Stokes investigation can now be approached without first elaborating a specialised FSD vocabulary for fluids. The condition for stopping is not that nothing more can be said, but that nothing more presently needs to be said for the declared function to hold. The global trajectory remains open. The local trajectory may stop.

**Working Notes: Transition to the Navier–Stokes Programme**  
Sets out the held position from which the next monograph begins: finite spatial representation with explicit Alphonic lower bound h≥α; base-10 nine-character numerical register with explicit scale transitions; measurement-derived initial states distinct from computationally evolved states; Dynexil analysis downstream and local; vocabulary monitored throughout. The Navier–Stokes programme is the first substantial external stress test of whether the language developed in Parts I–III has reached a useful local functional terminus.
