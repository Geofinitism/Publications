## Overview

M05 unites two complementary works into a single monograph. The first is the **FSM Conjecture** — a sustained epistemic examination of the Riemann Hypothesis, the completed real numbers, and the non-equivalence of endogenous and exogenous measurement. The second is the **FSM Catalogue** — a systematic inventory of twenty-five conjectures exposing the non-measurable primitives underlying classical mathematics. Together they constitute *the Finite Horizon*: the visible limit of symbolic extension at which endogenous symbolic constructions must answer to finite measurement, uncertainty, provenance, and admissibility.

The monograph's thesis is not a rejection of classical mathematics. It is the identification of the precise boundary — the Finite Boundary — at which internally coherent symbolic assertions are silently promoted into claims about measurable reality. Classical mathematics, M05 argues, lacks measurement axioms entirely. The FSM Conjecture and FSM Catalogue together supply them.

## The Central Distinction

The formal core of M05 is the Haylett distinction:

$$\mathcal{M}_{exo} \not\equiv \mathcal{M}_{endo}$$

where:
- **M_exo** — exogenous measurement: a finite process producing a symbol from contact with, or constraint by, something outside the symbolic system (the Geofinite Continuum)
- **M_endo** — endogenous measurement: a symbolic operation derived from prior exogenous measurements via finite, rule-governed operations

This is not a distinction between "measured" and "unmeasured." It is the distinction between **primary contact** (exogenous) and **derived transformation** (endogenous). Classical mathematics performs a **silent promotion** when it treats an endogenous symbolic result as though it were already an exogenous claim about measurable reality. The Riemann Hypothesis is the flagship case. Every conjecture in the FSM Catalogue is a miniature instance of the same boundary violation.

## Key Formal Contributions

### The Admissible Equivalence Relation
Classical equality = is replaced throughout by:
$$A \sim B\,|\,(\alpha, \delta, C, H, I)$$
where α is the Alphonic limit, δ the tolerance threshold, C the convention, H the measurement history, and I the inscription. Identity is tolerance-bound, convention-relative, and provenance-marked.

### The Trinity Meta-Framework
The three positive measurement axioms that classical mathematics never possessed:

| Pillar | Dimension | Question |
|---|---|---|
| Arc of Commitment | Temporal | Does the conjecture have a finite temporal sequence from inscription to boundary? |
| Admissibility | Operational | Are its primitives finitely representable and measurable? |
| Consensual Stability | Social | Is there community agreement on conventions, uncertainty, and boundaries? |

If any pillar is absent, the conjecture is a move inside a symbolic game, not a correspondence claim.

### The Adjunct — Five Classical Foundations Without Measurement
| Foundation | No measurement axioms because… |
|---|---|
| ZFC Set Theory | Axioms define membership, extension, union, power sets — all purely symbolic relations; no physical substrate |
| Peano Arithmetic | Defines 0, successor, induction — counting without measurement; no uncertainty, no instrument |
| Euclidean Geometry | Defines points, lines, planes as idealized limit-fictions — no finite-region replacement |
| Measure Theory | Assigns lengths to sets via σ-algebras — measures sizes of mathematical objects, not physical measurements |
| Probability Theory | Assigns numbers to events — models distributions within a formal space, not physical detection events |

The shared structure: all five systems define relations between symbols, with no axiom specifying how any symbol makes contact with the physical world. **Measure theory is not measurement theory.**

### Conjecture 24 — Takens Reconstruction
The bridge from FSM critique to FSM construction:

> Every static mathematical structure that includes an "imaginary" or "ideal" dimension can be reconstructed as an embedding of a finite dynamical process using delay coordinates. The so-called "imaginary" numbers are not imaginary. They are reconstructed coordinates from a dynamical process whose measurement arc has been forgotten. Takens' theorem makes the reconstruction explicit. ∂ makes the forgetting visible.

Euler's identity e^iπ + 1 = 0 is not a static miracle — it is a frozen trace of a rotational dynamical arc.
