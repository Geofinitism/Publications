# M29 Summary: A Theory of Finite Representation, Part I

**Extent, Registration and the Licence to Represent**  
Kevin R. Haylett · September 2026 · 49 pp.

---

## Draft note

This is the opening draft of a larger work. Its purpose is to establish a ground from which a Theory of Finite Representation can be developed without prematurely restricting the theory to a single instrument, branch of physics, mathematical controversy or historical episode. The term *finite* is used here not as a synonym for small or bounded, but to indicate that an actual representation has an extent, occupies a carrier, is distinguished across an interactional window and arrives with some history.

---

## Abstract

Science is often described as though a world of already formed objects were presented directly to an observer and then copied into mathematics. In practice, physical interaction, instrumental change, registration, numerical transformation, classification, modelling and language form an extended trajectory. This monograph begins a Theory of Finite Representation by making that trajectory explicit. It distinguishes three co-related basins: physical interaction and registration; endogenous mathematical and linguistic construction; and the bridge rules through which symbolic structures are licensed to make physical claims.

The proposed Axiom of Finite Representation does not prohibit classical mathematics from constructing ideal continua, points without extent, exact equalities or completed infinities within its own licensed basin. It instead governs the crossing: whenever an endogenous construction is applied to the registered world, its claim must return through finite representation, uncertainty, provenance and declared conditions of admissibility.

The theory is developed through finite extent, the registration boundary, Alphonic distinction, Functional Symbolic Trajectories, symbolic drag, licensing, equality and interactional correspondence. Symbolic compression and decompression are then developed through a bead-chain model. A compressed bead can stand for a longer trajectory, while decompression reconstructs a new chain through the reader's or system's existing endogenous lattice. A scientific object becomes extant not merely because it has been named, but because a stable compression can be repeatedly reconstructed from accepted registrations and bridge operations. Part I closes with a provisional formal architecture and a set of working principles intended to support a wider second volume on language, mathematics, instruments, images, statistics, artificial intelligence and the formation of scientific objects.

## Prologue: It Exists (pp. 1–2)

Opens with a concrete example: a 1727 volume of Robert Greene's *The Principles of the Philosophy of the Expansive and Contractive Forces* arriving through the post. The volume is not interchangeable with its reproductions; its paper has changed colour, marks have been added, its full history cannot be recovered. This distinction motivates the core question: how does a representation become extant? The prologue establishes the opening proposition — *a representation is never simply present; it becomes extant through a finite carrier, a history of interactions and a community capable of reconstructing its distinctions* — and sets the programme: to recover the trajectory between physical interaction and scientific statement.

## Chapter 1: The Forgotten Middle (pp. 3–5)

### 1.1 From world to statement

Introduces the *elementary chain* (Eq. 1.1):

> physical interaction → instrumental change → registration → finite symbol → transformation → classification → model → scientific statement

Every arrow performs work; the chain is productive, not a transparent conduit. Scientific compression removes the middle, making it appear that the final noun was directly observed.

### 1.2 Registration and inference

Separates three questions routinely compressed into one: what changed within the physical interaction and apparatus; what was registered from that change; what was inferred, named or modelled from the registration. Confusing these produces the appearance that named objects were delivered intact to detectors. The security of a scientific object lies in the strength, recurrence and declared conditions of the pathway, not in pretending symbolic construction never happened.

### 1.3 Compression and symbolic drag

*Symbolic drag* names the residual dependence of a compressed symbol upon the path that formed it. A quantity moved into a new equation does not arrive free of its measurement conditions. The theory must ask not only whether a transformation can be performed, but what the new symbol continues to carry and what its compression has abandoned.

> **The forgotten middle:** The distance between physical interaction and scientific statement is not a blank. It is a finite productive trajectory in which distinctions are created, transformed, compressed and licensed.

## Chapter 2: Three Basins (pp. 6–8)

### 2.1 Why separation is required

A clearer architecture requires separating three co-related basins. If properties of one domain are silently transferred to another, the bridge has already been crossed before its conditions can be examined.

### 2.2 The physical basin

Contains interactional trajectories, changes of state, apparatus, carriers and finite extents across which registrations are formed. At this level the first admissible statement is deliberately sparse: something interacted, and some difference became physically available to a registration system.

### 2.3 The symbolic basin

Contains finite marks and the operations performed upon them: words, numerals, equations, diagrams, tables, classifications, proofs, programmes and narratives. Mathematics is exceptionally powerful at constructing endogenous trajectories within this basin. The Theory of Finite Representation does not need to enter the mathematical basin and confiscate its objects.

### 2.4 The bridge basin

Contains the operations by which a symbolic construction is related to a physical registration: calibration, operational definition, measurement protocol, model fitting, prediction and experimental comparison. The two-directional bridge (Eq. 2.1):

> [physical interactional trajectory] ⟺ (declared bridge rules) ⟺ [functional symbolic trajectory]

The arrow is double because scientific practice crosses in both directions. It is not an equals sign.

### 2.5 Correspondence without miniature worlds

A model need not resemble the world in the manner of a scale object. It must produce relations that can be connected to possible registrations. The Theory of Finite Representation asks how bridges are constructed and what each bridge licences.

> **Three-basin rule:** No property declared inside one basin crosses into another without a bridge operation. Correspondence may be strong, repeatable and productive without being converted into identity.

## Chapter 3: Finite Extent (pp. 9–11)

### 3.1 Registration takes place

To register is to undergo and preserve a difference. Registration therefore occupies an extent. Let Δ denote an interactional window; the statement Δ > 0 (Eq. 3.1) does not establish an absolute smallest interval — it states that the particular registration did not occur across a literally absent extent.

### 3.2 Measurable and minimum measurable extent

For an apparatus A operating under conditions C, the minimum measurable extent Δ_min(A, C) (Eq. 3.2) is the smallest interactional window within which a chosen distinction can be repeatably registered. This is not an immutable property of nature; it changes with apparatus, criterion and environment.

### 3.3 Extent before object

At the registration boundary the order of ordinary language is reversed: a finite difference is registered, relations among registrations are stabilised, only then does a symbolic object become available. The theory asks us to avoid placing the completed noun at the start of its own construction.

### 3.4 The restrained physical claim

> **Principle of Finite Extent:** Whatever the unregistered physical world may be, anything entering an empirical scientific trajectory must produce a finitely distinguishable change across a non-zero interactional extent.

This principle does not declare that nature is composed of indivisible finite units. A claimed physical entity may be modelled by an ideal continuum, but the evidence supporting that claim must still become available through finite registration.

## Chapter 4: The Registration Boundary (pp. 12–14)

### 4.1 From interaction to distinction

The *registration boundary* is the functional region in which a usable symbolic distinction is generated. The elementary registration operation (Eq. 4.1):

> E →^{G_{α,Δ,δ}} s₀

where E is a physical interactional trajectory, G is the registration operation, α states the operative distinction condition, Δ is the finite interactional window, δ records uncertainty, and s₀ is the first finite symbol admitted to the subsequent trajectory.

The full registration state (Eq. 4.2):

> R₀ = (k, α, Δ, δ, H, C)

where the added terms record the conditions that make k usable. Ordinary practice stores only the first component and parts of the uncertainty.

### 4.2 Analogue continuity and symbolic decision

Even an apparently continuous trace is read through finite width, sampling, display, resolution and comparison. The theory does not need to deny continuity in the pre-symbolic response; it asks how a distinction is drawn from it. Analogue-to-digital conversion performs an explicit crossing.

### 4.3 Registration is not passive copying

Instruments are more active than windows. Their materials, geometry, thresholds and timings select which interactions can produce stable changes. The registered state is relational: formed by the encounter between an interactional trajectory and a system capable of being changed by it. The registered state is tethered to, but not identical with, the event.

### 4.4 The first symbol is already historical

s₀ already has a history: the apparatus was made and calibrated, thresholds were set, environmental conditions shaped the response. Provenance is necessarily partial — its role is not to offer impossible omniscience but to preserve enough of the trajectory for the claim to be examined, repeated, reconstructed or challenged.

> **Registration boundary:** A registration is a finite relational event in which physical interaction produces a sufficiently stable distinction to condition a later symbolic state. The resulting symbol is tethered to, but not identical with, the event.

## Chapter 5: The Axiom of Finite Representation (pp. 15–17)

### 5.1 The instantiated symbol

An instantiated symbol occupies a finite medium. It has a form sufficiently distinct from neighbouring forms to be recognised. It is created and preserved across a finite interval. It has a bounded recoverable history. Even when its content refers to infinity, the present mark does not become infinite.

> **Axiom of Finite Representation:** Every instantiated representation has finite extent, finite distinguishability, bounded resolution and a physical or historical provenance.

The axiom concerns representation as performed and carried, not the formal objects that representations may refer to.

### 5.2 A scientific strengthening

> **Scientific form:** Any symbol admitted into an empirical scientific trajectory must be finitely instantiated and must arise through, or be traceable to, a finite chain of registrations and declared symbolic transformations.

This does not require complete historical recovery; incompleteness itself becomes part of the admissibility judgement.

### 5.3 Six senses of the finite

The word *finite* carries six connected meanings within the axiom: (1) the carrier has finite physical extent; (2) the inscription is finite — only a bounded sequence of marks is present; (3) the distinction is finite — the mark is recognised through some operative resolution; (4) the precision is finite — registered within an uncertainty structure; (5) the operational trajectory is finite — the present representation was reached through a bounded sequence of available operations; (6) the recoverable provenance is finite — only part of the symbol's history remains accessible. These six senses are related but not identical.

### 5.4 Numbers as measurements

Within measurement, a number is a registered symbolic value — not an imperfect approximation to an infinitely exact number hidden behind the apparatus. The reported symbol is the finite outcome of the operational trajectory. Further measurement may alter the value, reduce uncertainty or disclose structure within the earlier window, but it does not transform the earlier registration into a metaphysical failure.

### 5.5 The axiom at the crossing

The axiom governs any crossing by which a mathematical construction acquires empirical application. A physical theory that declares the world *is* such a continuum has crossed the bridge — the application licences correspondence, not automatic identity.

## Chapter 6: The Conditions of Representation (pp. 18–20)

### 6.1 From pillars to a working state

Five conditions are required for finite representation: finite symbol, Alphonic distinction, uncertainty, provenance and admissibility. A working representational state (Eq. 6.1):

> R = (s, α, δ, H, C)

where s is the present finite symbol, α the distinction condition, δ its uncertainty, H its accessible history and C the conditions under which it is admitted.

### 6.2 Finite symbol

A finite symbol is a bounded difference capable of participating in a later trajectory. Its identity is not given by material sameness. The symbol already contains a compressive operation: variations in size, ink, position and carrier are disregarded so that a stable class can be reconstructed.

### 6.3 Alphonic distinction

The Alphonic condition concerns the smallest difference that counts as non-single within the present operation. Write α(A, C) for the operative distinction condition of apparatus or observer A under conditions C. Two physical variations that fall within the same admitted state are *symbolically single* for that operation, even if a different apparatus could separate them.

### 6.4 Uncertainty

Uncertainty is not appended to a value after the value has been declared. In finite representation it belongs to the formation of the distinction. The symbol s without δ is a stronger compression than the scientific state warrants. Uncertainty need not be interpreted as ignorance of a perfectly exact hidden value; it can describe the finite span over which the present distinction is stabilised.

### 6.5 Provenance

Provenance H is the accessible history by which a representation became present: physical production, instrumental settings, transformations, selection, storage, transcription and interpretive commitments. Complete provenance is impossible. The requirement is not total history but honest bounded history.

### 6.6 Admissibility and consensus

A private mark becomes scientifically effective only when it can enter a shared trajectory. Admissibility C gathers the rules, practices and communal judgements under which a representation is accepted for a particular purpose. Consensus is not a guarantee of physical correctness; it is the stabilising mechanism through which symbols become usable across a community.

> **Representational state:** The usable scientific symbol is not s alone but the conditioned state R = (s, α, δ, H, C). Compression may hide the other components; it does not abolish them.

## Chapter 7: Functional Symbolic Trajectories (pp. 21–23)

### 7.1 Symbols in motion

A symbol is functional because it alters what can happen next. A Functional Symbolic Trajectory (FST) is written (Eq. 7.1):

> s₀ →^{f₁} s₁ →^{f₂} s₂ →^{f₃} … →^{f_n} s_n

where each f_i is an admissible transformation under some conditions C_i. The linear writing should not be mistaken for a claim that the underlying process has only one dimension; a trajectory can branch, revisit earlier structures, draw upon memory and interact with other trajectories.

### 7.2 Unfolding the compressed object

A scientific object often appears at the end of a long FST (Eq. 7.2):

> detector change → value → corrected value → feature → class → object

Unfolding is not a demand to abolish objects. It is a method for examining their bridges: where did the exogenous registrations enter, which operations were endogenous, which distinctions were amplified, which variations were compressed into one class.

### 7.3 The reader becomes an author

An FST does not move intact from writer to reader. The reader receives a finite sequence of marks and reconstructs relations through a previously formed linguistic and experiential basin. Reading is therefore a new trajectory constrained by, but not identical with, the written one.

### 7.4 Trajectory loss and statistics

When many registrations are gathered into a distribution, much of their individual history is compressed. Order may be discarded, local dependence removed and different paths rendered exchangeable. From the present perspective, an important part of the phenomenon is *trajectory loss*: states remain as values while the longer paths that produced them are no longer recoverable.

### 7.5 Symbolic drag revisited

The endpoint s_n carries constraints inherited from earlier states even when their explicit form has been removed. Symbolic drag is not uniform: some prior constraints fade while others dominate the new basin.

> **Trajectory principle:** A finite symbol is a temporary stabilisation within a history of transformations. Its function and admissibility depend upon the trajectory that brought it into the present and the continuations it makes available.

## Chapter 8: The Bridge Axiom (pp. 24–26)

### 8.1 The missing rule of application

The missing rule of application is not another equation within a physical model. It is a rule governing the model's application.

> **Bridge Axiom:** No endogenous mathematical or linguistic construction acquires physical identity solely from its internal coherence. Its empirical application requires a declared correspondence with finite registrations under stated conditions of distinction, uncertainty and provenance.

Internal coherence is necessary but not sufficient for a formal object to become a physical object.

### 8.2 The inward crossing

In the direction from physical interaction towards mathematics, the crossing has the form (Eq. 8.1):

> E →^{G_{α,Δ,δ}} s₀ →^{F_H} s_n →^{L_C} M

where M is a mathematical or linguistic construction licensed from the symbolic trajectory.

### 8.3 The outward crossing

In the reverse direction a model produces an expected finite representation (Eq. 8.2):

> M →^{P_C} R̂ |_C R_new

where P_C is a prediction operation, R̂ is the expected registered state and R_new is a new physical registration. The interactional bar records a conditioned comparison, not an identity.

### 8.4 Bridge strength

Bridge strength has several dimensions: recurrence (if the correspondence is repeatedly reconstructed), independence (if different apparatus and groups recover related registrations), transportability (if the relation survives a change of context), discriminating power, and provenance-richness. These are not reducible to a single score.

### 8.5 Alternative trajectories

Geofinitism can be understood as a chosen exploratory trajectory. It begins with finite extent and finite distinction, then asks what mathematics, physics and language can be built while preserving those commitments. Its value need not depend upon the immediate destruction of every classical path; it offers another route through the landscape and allows the resulting bridges to be compared.

## Chapter 9: Equality, Identity and Licence (pp. 27–29)

### 9.1 The most compressive sign

The equals sign is one of the most powerful operations in symbolic culture — it allows different inscriptions to be treated as one value, different expressions to be substituted, and long trajectories to terminate in a compact declaration. Difficulty arises when formal equality crosses into measurement without a change of status.

The statement x₁ = x₂ (Eq. 9.1) can hide a longer empirical claim, more accurately written with the interactional bar x₁ |_C x₂ (Eq. 9.2): the registered values are admitted as corresponding under conditions C.

### 9.2 Formal equality

Formal equality belongs to a symbolic system whose objects and rules have already been licensed. It can be exact because exactness is part of the game's construction.

### 9.3 Measured equivalence

Given R₁ = (s₁, α₁, δ₁, H₁, C₁) and R₂ = (s₂, α₂, δ₂, H₂, C₂) (Eq. 9.3), declaring them equivalent is selecting a comparison operation C* under which those differences do not alter the present task: R₁ |_{C*} R₂ (Eq. 9.4). Another task may require the differences to be restored.

### 9.4 The declaration hidden in the sign

Every equality rests upon a licence to disregard difference. The equals sign does not merely report sameness; it declares which future substitutions are permitted. The interactional bar M |_C R (Eq. 9.5) keeps the encounter open: the model and registration remain different trajectories, joined under conditions C.

### 9.5 Licence as productive constraint

A licence is a set of commitments that makes a basin of trajectories possible while excluding others. Equality can therefore be read as a particularly strong compression: two previously distinguishable symbolic paths are licensed to occupy one operative position.

> **Equality and the bridge:** Formal equality is licensed identity within an endogenous system. Empirical comparison is conditioned correspondence between finite trajectories. The two relations may cooperate, but they must not be silently exchanged.

## Chapter 10: Symbolic Compression and Decompression (pp. 30–34)

### 10.1 From chain to label

A finite chain of beads (Eq. 10.1): B = (b₁, b₂, …, b_n) can be compressed into a labelled bead β through the current endogenous lattice Λ (Eq. 10.2):

> (b₁, b₂, …, b_n) →^{κ_{Λ,C}} β

The new bead does not contain the earlier chain as a small physical object. It is licensed to represent that chain in later operations.

### 10.2 Exogenously tethered and endogenously generated beads

An exogenous interaction E must first produce a finite registration (Eq. 10.3): E →^{G_{α,Δ,δ}} b_i^X ∈ Λ_t, instantiated within the endogenous lattice Λ_t. An endogenous bead is formed by operating upon beads already available in the lattice (Eq. 10.4): (b₁^N, b₂^N, …, b_m^N) →^{Φ_{Λ_t,C}} b_{m+1}^N. The superscripts X and N identify provenance rather than material kind.

### 10.3 Decompression as reconstruction

Decompression (Eq. 10.5): β →^{D_{Λ_t,C}} B̂_t = (b̂₁, b̂₂, …, b̂_q) reconstructs a chain through the current lattice. The hat over B is essential: the compressed bead does not determine one exhaustive reconstruction. Two readers can decompress the same word into related but non-identical chains (Eq. 10.6): D_{Λ_a,C}(β) ≠ D_{Λ_b,C}(β). Communication remains possible because language stabilises enough shared constraints for the reconstructed chains to correspond.

### 10.4 Reading through a handler

The endogenous lattice Λ_t functions as the handler of the incoming chain. Reading is a recurrent process of registration, decompression, comparison, construction and reinjection (Eq. 10.7):

> E_in →^G B^X →^{D_{Λ_t,C}} B̂_t →^{Ψ_{Λ_t}} r^N → Λ_{t+1}

The cycle can be abbreviated as: register → decompress → compare → construct → reinject (Eq. 10.8).

### 10.5 Phonemes, tokens and new words

A phoneme can be treated as a minimum reusable endogenous linguistic record. A word bead is a compression of a phonemic chain (Eq. 10.9): (p₁, p₂, …, p_k) →^{κ_{Λ,C}} w. The same architecture recurs at several symbolic scales (Eq. 10.10): phoneme chain → word bead; word chain → phrase bead; sentence chain → proposition bead; registration chain → object bead; argument chain → theory bead.

### 10.6 Writing and reinjection

A bead generated endogenously can be written, spoken, diagrammed or otherwise impressed into a new external carrier (Eq. 10.11): r^N →^{W_{α,Δ,δ}} E_out. Reading and writing together form a recurrent loop (Eq. 10.12): external chain → internal registration → decompression → new endogenous chain → external inscription.

### 10.7 Information loss and symbolic drag

Symbolic compression is related to information theory but cannot be reduced to bit-count. The relevant question is not merely how much information remains, but which differences the present licence has made unavailable. The later quantitative programme is to follow compression and decompression along an FST, mark where exogenous provenance entered, identify which distinctions were removed, and examine how different handlers reconstruct the resulting beads.

> **Compression–decompression principle:** Compression licenses one admitted bead to represent a longer chain. Decompression uses that bead to perturb a current handler into reconstructing a further chain. The reconstruction is conditioned by its carrier, provenance, licence and lattice; it need not reproduce the trajectory compressed.

## Chapter 11: How Objects Become Extant (pp. 35–37)

### 11.1 From registrations to nouns

An object becomes scientifically extant when a sufficiently stable symbolic compression can be reconstructed from accepted registrations and transformations (Eq. 11.1):

> {r₁, r₂, …, r_n} →^{F_H} feature →^{L_C} object

Once formed, the object can organise new measurement; a catalogue designed to count it, or a theory extended to predict its behaviour, feeds back into the physical arrangement.

### 11.2 Construction and arbitrariness

To say that an object is constructed does not mean it is invented without physical constraint. Construction names the positive work through which an object becomes available: selection, distinction, comparison, compression, naming and stabilisation. The physical world participates through the registrations it permits the apparatus to form; the symbolic community participates through the operations it recognises.

### 11.3 The flying pig and the distant object

The flying pig's trajectory begins predominantly inside a symbolic basin; its features are licensed by narrative coherence and shared language. The scientific object's trajectory is claimed to begin with exogenous interactions. The two share symbolic construction but have different bridge structures. This comparison prevents a false division in which imagination contains construction while observation contains none.

### 11.4 Scale and the stability of nouns

Middle-scale objects hide the trajectory of object formation because human perception, action and language have repeatedly stabilised them. At very small or very large scales, the chain becomes more visible. The problem is not solved by banning nouns; it is addressed by marking their range and preserving the route by which they were licensed.

### 11.5 What becomes scientifically extant

> **Becoming extant:** A scientific object becomes extant when a stable symbolic compression can be reconstructed from admitted registrations and can productively constrain future symbolic or physical operations.

## Chapter 12: A Provisional Theory (pp. 38–41)

### 12.1 The complete opening trajectory

The preceding chapters can be gathered into one provisional architecture (Eq. 12.1):

> E →^{G_{α,Δ,δ}} s₀ →^{F_H} s_n →^{L_C} O

Here E is a physical interactional trajectory, G forms a finite registration under a distinction condition, an FST F_H transforms that registration while carrying accessible history H, and a licensing operation L_C admits the stabilised state as an object, quantity or claim O. The reverse, predictive trajectory (Eq. 12.2):

> O → M →^{P_C} R̂ |_C R_new

### 12.2 Seven working principles

The opening theory can be stated through seven working principles:

1. **The Principle of Finite Registration** — Empirical access begins when a physical interaction produces a finitely distinguishable and sufficiently stable change. No scientific measurement is available without an interactional extent.

2. **The Axiom of Finite Representation** — Every instantiated scientific symbol occupies a finite carrier and has bounded distinguishability, resolution and recoverable provenance, regardless of whether its content refers to an ideal or infinite mathematical object.

3. **The Trajectory Principle** — No scientific symbol is independent of the transformations through which it was produced. Compression can conceal a trajectory but cannot make the endpoint historically uncaused.

4. **The Bridge Principle** — An endogenous symbolic construction acquires empirical application only through declared correspondences with possible finite registrations. Internal coherence alone does not create physical identity.

5. **The Non-Identity Principle** — A representation is not identical with the physical trajectory to which it refers. Strong, repeatable and useful correspondence does not require that difference to be abolished.

6. **The Compression Formation Principle** — Scientific objects are stabilised by compressing multiple registrations and symbolic trajectories into a representative bead under a licence that declares certain differences irrelevant to the present operation.

7. **The Recoverable Decompression Principle** — Critical analysis proceeds by decompressing symbolic beads towards their registrations, transformations, uncertainties, provenance and bridge conditions. The reconstruction will always be partial and handler-dependent, but its limits should be declared rather than hidden.

### 12.3 The status of truth

A scientific implication can be written P → Q | (C, α, H, δ) (Eq. 12.3): the trajectory from P to Q is admitted under consensus or licensing conditions C, distinction condition α, accessible provenance H and uncertainty δ. These conditions do not make the claim merely subjective; they state the finite architecture through which the claim can be reconstructed.

### 12.4 Admissibility rather than prohibition

The theory is most useful when it asks whether a move is admissible under declared conditions, not when it issues universal prohibitions. A continuum may be admissible in a mathematical proof and productive in a physical model; the question is which finite relations support its use.

### 12.5 What the theory does not yet claim

Part I does not provide a universal metric for symbolic distance, a complete calculus of compression and decompression or a final physical value for the Alphonic limit. It does not prove that all continua are physically false or that scientific objects are reducible to language. The theory has established an architecture: three basins, a registration boundary, five conditions of representation, finite trajectories, bridge licensing and a compression–decompression dynamics.

> **Provisional statement of the theory:** Scientific knowledge develops as a recurrent coupling between finite physical registrations and finite symbolic trajectories. Objects and claims become extant through licensed compressions; inquiry proceeds by producing new distinctions and by decompressing the histories that compression has concealed.

## Chapter 13: Directions of Travel (pp. 42–44)

### 13.1 Language

Language can be examined as a trajectory rather than a transparent carrier. Nouns contract processes into objects; verbs unfold relations across time; metaphor transports constraints between basins. This direction includes the historical question of how particular symbolic licences became dominant.

### 13.2 Mathematics and proof

A proof can be unfolded as a finite symbolic trajectory — its apparently static page becomes an ordered sequence of admissible transitions. Embedding these trajectories offers a practical programme: parameters can be stepped, states enumerated and delayed coordinates constructed. The resulting attractor is not merely an illustration of the proof; it is a representation of the proof's functional dynamics.

### 13.3 Instruments and conversion

Instrumentation makes the registration boundary materially accessible. The ADC is especially important because it performs an explicit crossing from a range of physical responses to a finite symbolic alphabet. Experiments with light, pinholes and CCD registration can investigate how patterns stabilise across finite windows.

### 13.4 Images and astronomical objects

A scientific image appears immediate because human vision compresses its structure rapidly into objects. But the displayed colours, intensities and boundaries often arise from processing choices that are not themselves visible in the image. Astronomy offers a particularly long trajectory from remote interaction to scientific noun; the Vera C. Rubin Observatory is suggested as a future case study.

### 13.5 Statistics and trajectory loss

Statistics gains power by compressing individual histories into collective structure. The Theory of Finite Representation can ask which trajectory information survives each statistic and whether different dynamics have been collapsed into similar endpoints.

### 13.6 Artificial intelligence

A language model operates upon finite tokens, vectors and transformations, yet its symbolic trajectories can reconstruct patterns extending far beyond any single token. Attention can be examined as a form of relational delay embedding, and a Takens-based transformer can explore whether trajectory reconstruction offers an alternative architecture. Language alignment changes when language is treated as a nonlinear dynamical system of finite trajectories rather than a transparent vessel; the model and its human reader form a coupled trajectory rather than a one-way transfer of finished propositions.

### 13.7 The programme of Part II

Part II should follow these crossings comparatively. A provisional title is *A Theory of Finite Representation, Part II: Compression, Decompression and the Formation of Objects*. It would seek operational measures of symbolic compression and decompression, methods for embedding FSTs, accounts of trajectory loss, comparative instrument studies and extended examples of noun formation.

## Epilogue: Another Trajectory Begins (p. 45)

Finite representation begins with the freedom and responsibility to choose another starting commitment, but the resulting trajectory must remain inspectable. We must say where distinctions entered, which histories were preserved, what uncertainty was carried, what compression formed the object and what bridge licensed the return to physics. A finite representation does not terminate the world in a symbol. It creates a bounded and historically situated place from which another trajectory can begin.

## Working Glossary (pp. 46–47)

Key terms defined:

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Admissibility** | The conditions under which a representation or transition is accepted for a particular operation within a symbolic or scientific community |
| **Alphonic distinction** | The operative boundary at which a relevant difference is registered as non-single and becomes available to a symbolic trajectory |
| **Bead** | A finite symbolic distinction capable of occupying a position in a chain, carrying provenance and conditioning a later operation |
| **Bridge** | A declared mapping or correspondence between a physical interactional trajectory and a functional symbolic trajectory |
| **Compression** | An operation through which one admitted bead is licensed to represent a longer chain of beads under stated conditions |
| **Decompression** | The handler-dependent reconstruction of a further bead-chain from a compressed symbolic bead; the reconstructed chain need not be identical to the trajectory originally compressed |
| **Extant** | Available to be encountered and reconstructed within a present physical and symbolic system |
| **Finite extent** | The non-zero interactional span across which a physical distinction is produced and stabilised |
| **Finite representation** | An instantiated, bounded and historically situated distinction capable of conditioning a later symbolic operation |
| **Functional Symbolic Trajectory** | A finite pathway of symbolic states and transformations through which constraints, uncertainty, meaning and admissibility are carried |

## Named Propositions and Principles

| Name | Statement |
|---|---|
| Opening proposition | A representation is never simply present. It becomes extant through a finite carrier, a history of interactions and a community capable of reconstructing its distinctions. |
| The forgotten middle | The distance between physical interaction and scientific statement is not a blank. It is a finite productive trajectory in which distinctions are created, transformed, compressed and licensed. |
| Three-basin rule | No property declared inside one basin crosses into another without a bridge operation. Correspondence may be strong, repeatable and productive without being converted into identity. |
| Principle of Finite Extent | Whatever the unregistered physical world may be, anything entering an empirical scientific trajectory must produce a finitely distinguishable change across a non-zero interactional extent. |
| Registration boundary | A registration is a finite relational event in which physical interaction produces a sufficiently stable distinction to condition a later symbolic state. The resulting symbol is tethered to, but not identical with, the event. |
| Axiom of Finite Representation | Every instantiated representation has finite extent, finite distinguishability, bounded resolution and a physical or historical provenance. |
| Scientific form | Any symbol admitted into an empirical scientific trajectory must be finitely instantiated and must arise through, or be traceable to, a finite chain of registrations and declared symbolic transformations. |
| Bridge Axiom | No endogenous mathematical or linguistic construction acquires physical identity solely from its internal coherence. Its empirical application requires a declared correspondence with finite registrations under stated conditions of distinction, uncertainty and provenance. |
| Representational state | The usable scientific symbol is not s alone but the conditioned state R = (s, α, δ, H, C). Compression may hide the other components; it does not abolish them. |
| Trajectory principle | A finite symbol is a temporary stabilisation within a history of transformations. Its function and admissibility depend upon the trajectory that brought it into the present and the continuations it makes available. |
| Equality and the bridge | Formal equality is licensed identity within an endogenous system. Empirical comparison is conditioned correspondence between finite trajectories. The two relations may cooperate, but they must not be silently exchanged. |
| Compression–decompression principle | Compression licenses one admitted bead to represent a longer chain. Decompression uses that bead to perturb a current handler into reconstructing a further chain. The reconstruction is conditioned by its carrier, provenance, licence and lattice; it need not reproduce the trajectory compressed. |
| Becoming extant | A scientific object becomes extant when a stable symbolic compression can be reconstructed from admitted registrations and can productively constrain future symbolic or physical operations. |
| Provisional statement of the theory | Scientific knowledge develops as a recurrent coupling between finite physical registrations and finite symbolic trajectories. Objects and claims become extant through licensed compressions; inquiry proceeds by producing new distinctions and by decompressing the histories that compression has concealed. |
