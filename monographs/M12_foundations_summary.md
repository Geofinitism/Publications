## Core Thesis

This monograph enacts a fundamental reversal of the classical order of geometric construction. In Euclid, the **point** is the primitive from which all extension is built. In Finite Symbolic Mechanics, the **point** is the terminal symbolic compression of a prior finite measurement process. The monograph reconstructs the point from measured solid extent, rather than constructing extent from the point.

> "Euclid constructs the solid from the point. FSM reconstructs the point from measured solid extent."

The smallest admissible geometric object is not the dimensionless point but the **Geometric Alphonic Sphere (GAS)**: the minimal isotropic three-dimensional registration at the Alphonic Limit. The Alphonic Limit (α) is finite, instrument-dependent, epoch-dependent, and revisable — not a universal constant.

The central reversal:
- **Classical ordering:** Point → Line → Surface → Solid  
- **Geofinite ordering:** Measurement interaction → Alphonic registration → Finite geometric relations → Ideal point as compression

The point is not rejected. It is relocated as the **terminal symbolic compression** of a prior finite measurement process.

## Part II — Foundational Sentences (Chapters 4–6)

### Chapter 4: Definitions

**Definition 1 — Measured Distinction**  
A finite three-dimensional registration that is separable from at least one alternative finite three-dimensional registration within the current measurement system.

**Definition 2 — Alphonic Limit (α)**  
The smallest admissible three-dimensional distinction achievable by the current measurement system for a specified purpose at the current epoch. The Alphonic Limit is finite, instrument-dependent, epoch-dependent, and revisable.

> α = α(I, M, C, t)

where I = instrument, M = method, C = context, t = epoch. The Alphonic Limit is **not** the Planck length or any universal constant. It is indexed to the measurement ecology in which it operates.

**Definition 3 — Geometric Alphonic Sphere (GAS)**  
The minimal isotropic three-dimensional registration at the Alphonic Limit, formed when no directional preference is admitted.

> Vα = (4/3)π(α/2)³

Isotropy is understood as **withheld directional commitment**, not as an assertion of spherical form in nature.

**Definition 4 — Instrumental Alphonic Limit**  
The smallest three-dimensional distinction a particular instrument can register. May be anisotropic: αx ≠ αy ≠ αz. The effective instrumental registration is:

> Ωα = Ω(αx, αy, αz; I, M)

**Definition 5 — Alphonic Neighbourhood**  
A finite arrangement of Alphonic registrations sufficient to establish relational distinction — the minimum geometric configuration needed for direction, containment, or comparison to be defined.

**Definition 6 — Geometric Character**  
A stable, distinguishable three-dimensional arrangement that functions as a member of a finite symbolic repertoire. Characters are mutually non-equivalent: Ci ≁ Cj for all i ≠ j.

**Definition 7 — Character Alphonic Scale (χ)**  
The minimum three-dimensional extent required for a stable, distinguishable character. χ > α necessarily; no character can be smaller than the distinction it names. Character volume obeys:

> Vχ ≥ kVα (k > 1)

The full character descriptor: χ(C) = (VC, GC, UC, HC, TC) — volume, geometry, uncertainty, history, trajectory signature.

**Definition 8 — Radial Registration**  
A measured relational statement between two finite three-dimensional regions. The radius is a **scalar compression** of a volumetric relation; it is not a property of an isolated point.

**Definition 9 — Geometric Compression**  
The removal of spatial, instrumental, historical, or uncertainty constraints from a finite geometric description to produce a portable symbolic representation. Compressed objects are legitimate when their compression is declared.

## Part III — Constructing Geometry from Finite Extent (Chapters 7–9)

### Chapter 7: The Geometric Alphonic Sphere

The GAS is the atom of admissible geometric registration — not the atom of matter. Four levels of description are separated:

> α = α(I, M, C, t)

Sphere packing here is interpreted as **admissibility geometry**, not as decoration. The question is what arrangements are physically admissible as character registrations.

The centre C is an operation applied to a region, not a pre-given point:

> C = C(Ω)

The finite shell: r − δr ≤ ρ ≤ r + δr, where δr is determined by measurement uncertainty.

### Chapter 16: The Finite Circle and Circumference

The finite circle is defined as the set of registered positions satisfying:

> (r − δr)² ≤ x² + y² ≤ (r + δr)²

The circumference is represented as a finite sequence of base-B symbols:

> ΓN = (B1 … BN)

A finite circle is a **stabilised relation among finite registrations** — not a failed ideal. The status of π in finite geometry: π is a compressed symbolic representative of a ratio between finite geometric quantities; it is not invalidated by finiteness, but its application always involves reconstruction of suppressed constraints. The number of symbols required to represent a circumference is base-dependent; closure in finite sequences depends on base.

## Part VII — Base, Alphabet and Geometric Capacity (Chapters 18–19)

### Chapter 18: Finite Base Dependence

With alphabet of size B and sequence length n, the number of distinct representations is:

> N(B, n) = Bⁿ

The **Geometric Burden of an Alphabet**: every symbol has a physical footprint. The representational cost function:

> R(B, N) ~ L(B, N) · Vχ(B)

where L(B, N) is the sequence length required (decreasing in B) and Vχ(B) is the character volume (increasing in B).

The **Base Trade-Off**: higher base reduces sequence length but increases character volume. Lower base reduces character volume but increases sequence length. These trade against each other. This is **base non-invariance in measured geometry**: two representations of the same value in different bases do not have the same physical cost.

> "Equal value, unequal trajectory."

### Chapter 19: Character Volume and Trajectory Length

The fuller cost function:

> R = R(B, L, Vχ, E, T, U, H)

incorporating energy cost E(B), time cost T(B), uncertainty cost U(B), and historical training cost Ctrain(B). The optimal physical base is determined by minimising R over available physical implementations — it is not fixed abstractly.

Characters are understood as **packed finite regions** in physical space. A sequence is an **extended geometry**: writing a number in base B is an act of spatial arrangement subject to all the constraints of Definitions 1–9.

> "What is removed from sequence length must be placed into character structure."

**F.4 — Finite Symbolic Trajectories (FSTs):** Every FST is a sequence of characters; each character has extent χ > α; a trajectory through character space is a trajectory through a finite geometry subject to Propositions 3–7.

**F.5 — Base Non-Invariance:** M12's account of finite base dependence (Proposition 7, Conjecture 7) is the geometric basis of the FSM claim that base is not a neutral notational choice.

**F.6 — Symbolic Drag:** M12's Conjecture 8 places the FSM concept of symbolic drag on a geometric foundation: it is the residual correction burden from suppressed constraints in compression.

**F.7 — Five Recurring Commitments:** The common commitments of M12 (Ch. 6) are the geometric expressions of the five commitments that recur throughout the FSM corpus.
