## Core Claim

The physical sciences have inherited a language in which *matter* is a primitive: matter exists, possesses properties, occupies space, and then undergoes change. This paper proposes replacing that starting point with **Measured Dynamical Extent (MDE)**: a finite registered extent within which relational dynamics exhibit distinguishable patterns of stability. The reversal is foundational:

> Classical: thing → properties → behaviour  [Eq. 1]  
> MDE: extent → interaction → dynamics → stability → registration → symbolic state  [Eq. 3]

Matter begins by asserting a category. Measured Dynamical Extent begins by asking what can be distinguished.

Physical categories — solid, liquid, gas, plasma, condensate, vacuum — are not initially treated as different kinds of substance. They are treated as symbolic descriptions of different regimes of measured dynamical stability. A physical "thing" is consequently not a primitive but a compressed symbolic designation for a sufficiently persistent pattern of registered relations. The thing arrives at the end of description, not the beginning.

## The Difficulty with Matter (§2)

The word *matter* performs an enormous amount of conceptual work: a rock is matter, water is matter, air is matter, plasma is matter, a crystal is matter. Yet these categories exhibit radically different dynamical behaviour. The unity provided by the word matter is not a unity of observed behaviour but a higher-level classification imposed across many different regimes.

The problem is compressive reversal. The correct direction is:

> registered trajectory → symbol  [Eq. 4]

But the compressed term may silently become interpreted as:

> symbolic entity → cause of registered trajectory  [Eq. 5]

The map has been allowed to become the source of the territory it describes. MDE is intended to interrupt this reversal.

## Extent Before Substance (§3)

The word *extent* is deliberately weaker than *matter*. It does not initially tell us what occupies the extent. It does not require atoms, particles, a field, or a continuum. It states only that some finite distinction possesses measurable spread or persistence sufficient to participate in registration. Extent also prevents the geometrical point from entering unnoticed as a primitive physical object: no physical registration is itself without extent.

The qualification *dynamical* adds a second requirement. The measured extent is not assumed to be frozen. Its relations may persist, oscillate, fluctuate, reorganise, bifurcate, decay or stabilise. Even what appears static at one observational scale may exhibit extensive dynamics at another. A crystal resting on a laboratory bench is macroscopically stable; at another scale it exhibits thermal motion, lattice vibration, defects, local rearrangements. The statement "the crystal is stationary" therefore means approximately: "no change relevant to the present measurement has exceeded the operative threshold of distinction during the specified interval." Static description becomes a limiting case of measured dynamical description.

## Formal Definition and Notation (§§4–5)

**Measured Dynamical Extent (MDE):** A finite registered extent within which relational dynamics exhibit a distinguishable pattern of stability under specified conditions of spatial resolution, temporal resolution, interaction, uncertainty and provenance.

A useful preliminary representation:

ε_M = ε | (Δx, Δt, I, α, δ, H)  [Eq. 9]

where Δx = operative spatial resolution; Δt = operative temporal resolution; I = relevant interaction or measurement relation; α = operative Alphonic condition; δ = retained uncertainty; H = provenance or measurement history.

This notation preserves what conventional compressed notation removes. A statement about extent is always made under finite conditions; the state registered at one spatial or temporal resolution need not be the state registered at another. Consequently:

S = S(Δx, Δt, I, α, δ, H)  [Eq. 10]

Physical state is relationally and instrumentally situated. This does not mean the state is arbitrary: different observers using compatible instruments, procedures and admissibility conditions may obtain highly reproducible registrations, and consensus may become strong. But reproducibility does not remove the measurement conditions under which reproduction occurs.

The framework also distinguishes:
- ε_D — inferred dynamic extent (the first, unmeasured term)
- ε_M — measured dynamical extent (registered)
- S — resulting symbolic state (compressed)

These are three different epistemic conditions that should not be silently merged. The relationship is schematically: ε_D →^G ε_M → S, where G denotes a generonic interaction or registration boundary [Eq. 8].

## Phases as Stability Regimes (§§6–7)

The concept of physical phase receives an immediate reinterpretation:

> **A physical phase is a recurrently distinguishable regime of stability within measured dynamical extent.**

This moves the emphasis away from substance and towards behaviour. A crystalline phase is characterised by persistent relational organisation over specified scales. A fluid phase retains local interaction while permitting continual large-scale rearrangement. A gaseous phase exhibits another characteristic range of persistence, separation and interaction. A plasma displays another relational regime. A condensate exhibits unusually extended coherence. The crucial point is that none of these descriptions requires the phase name to be treated as an irreducible kind of thing. Instead:

> dynamical organisation → persistent regime → registered distinction → phase name  [Eq. 11]

The name is downstream of the stability.

A phase diagram is consequently reconsidered as a map of stability regimes:
- Conventional: conditions → which phase of matter exists? [Eq. 12]
- MDE: conditions → which dynamical organisation remains stable? [Eq. 13]

A phase boundary is no longer merely the symbolic dividing line between two named kinds of material state. It identifies the region in which one pattern of stability ceases to remain admissibly persistent and another emerges: S₁ → B → S₂ [Eq. 14], where B denotes a transition region potentially involving bifurcation, metastability, hysteresis, critical behaviour, or competing attractors.

## Stability Does Not Mean Stillness (§8)

The phrase *pattern of stability* is preferable to an everyday notion of stability as absence of change:

> stability ≠ stasis  [Eq. 15]  
> stability ~ persistence of distinguishable relational organisation  [Eq. 16]

The symbol ~ is used deliberately to indicate a relation rather than an assertion of exact identity. Oscillation may be stable. Rotation may be stable. A chaotic attractor may possess stable statistical structure. A turbulent flow may retain persistent large-scale organisation while undergoing rapid local change. A living organism may retain recognisable identity while continuously exchanging material and reorganising internally.

## Scale and the Production of State (§9)

A central consequence: the apparent state of an extent depends upon the scale over which its stability is registered. There is no scale-independent symbolic description called simply "the state of the crystal." There are registered states under declared conditions. The compression SOLID [Eq. 17] suppresses a large quantity of temporal, spatial and instrumental qualification. This suppression is frequently useful, but FSM requires that the qualification has been compressed rather than eliminated. A symbolic state is therefore better understood as a finite statement about persistence across an admissible observational window.

## Vacuum as a Measured Stability Regime (§10)

Vacuum provides an especially revealing case:

> vacuum = nothing  [Eq. 18 — the conventional reading]

But an experimental vacuum is not produced by measuring nothing. It is produced through apparatus, pumping, containment, pressure measurement, temperature control, electromagnetic conditions, residual-gas estimation, detector thresholds and finite observational intervals. The word *vacuum* compresses an extensive experimental trajectory. MDE permits a deliberately restrained interpretation:

> **Vacuum is a named measured stability regime of dynamical extent.**

No further ontological claim is required. It does not assert what vacuum ultimately consists of. It does not require the introduction of virtual particles, fields, hidden media or a null substrate. VACUUM [Eq. 19] is not synonymous with an experimentally established "nothing." It is a symbolic classification of a particular measured regime. This distinction may be small linguistically, but it is foundationally important.

## The Alphonic Limit Must Be Dynamical (§§11–12)

The introduction of MDE requires a reconsideration of the Alphonic Limit. Within FSM, the Alphonic Limit prevents the physical point of zero extent from entering measurement unnoticed. However, if measurement is dynamical, then spatial extent alone is insufficient. No registration occurs at literally zero duration. A detector changes state across an interval. A distinction persists sufficiently to affect another system. A registration is therefore not merely spatial — it possesses temporal extension. The Alphonic Limit cannot consistently be imagined as the smallest static sphere or bead from which the world is assembled; such an interpretation would simply recreate atomism at a finer descriptive level.

**Alphonic Dynamical Extent:** The minimum finite dynamical extent over which a relational difference can remain sufficiently distinguishable to participate in an admissible registration.

This changes the foundational question. Rather than asking "what is the smallest thing that exists?" or "what is the smallest volume that can be measured?", we ask: "what is the minimum dynamical extent capable of becoming a stable finite distinction?"

The Alphonic Limit consequently functions as a **stability limit** rather than merely a resolution limit. Below some operative condition, the difficulty may not be that "nothing exists" but that no relational difference remains sufficiently stable, relative to the interaction and instrument, to become a finite registered symbol:

> difference + extent + persistence + interaction → registration  [Eq. 20]  
> registration → symbol  [Eq. 21]

The foundational commitment is therefore not to a particular smallest object, but to finite distinction itself.

## The Generonic Boundary (§13)

The generonic boundary becomes particularly important under this interpretation. The term denotes the transition at which an analogue, exogenous or continuously changing interaction becomes a finite registered distinction. It is not necessarily a geometrical surface; it may be implemented physically by a detector threshold, analogue-to-digital conversion, chemical transition, mechanical displacement, neuronal response or other finite state change:

> dynamic interaction →^G registered distinction  [Eq. 22]

The generonic operation converts dynamical extent into symbolic availability. FSM distinguishes between the trajectory and its terminal symbol. MDE belongs primarily to the trajectory side of this boundary; named physical entities belong increasingly to the symbolic side.

## The Emergence of the Thing (§14)

The reversal introduced by MDE can now be stated fully:

> Classical: Thing → Properties → Behaviour  [Eq. 23]  
> MDE: Dynamical Extent → Interaction → Stability → Distinction → Registration → Name  [Eq. 24]

A rock may therefore be interpreted not initially as a primitive object possessing stability, but as a symbolic compression applied to a region of dynamical extent whose relational organisation persists sufficiently across the relevant observational scales. The categories crystal, droplet, gas cloud and plasma may all be regarded as terminal symbolic compressions of different histories of registration. This does not make the categories unreal — their utility and reproducibility may be extremely high. It merely relocates their status: they are not primitive constituents of the description but stable symbolic achievements.

## Nouns as Stability Compression (§15)

The physical argument connects to a broader linguistic problem. A noun frequently presents a process as though it were a thing. The word *river* appears stable while the water changes continuously. The word *flame* names a persistent dynamical organisation despite rapid replacement of participating material. The word *organism* maintains symbolic identity across metabolism, growth, repair and exchange. The word *storm* compresses a vast moving atmospheric trajectory into a portable nominal unit:

> dynamic relations → recurrent stability → symbolic compression → noun  [Eq. 25]

The noun is useful precisely because the underlying trajectory does not need to be reconstructed every time the term is used. But the success of compression can conceal its own origin. Once established, the noun may appear more fundamental than the dynamics from which it arose. MDE attempts to restore the trajectory beneath the noun.

## Relation to FSD and FSTs (§§16–17)

MDE and FSD have complementary roles. If MDE describes the measured dynamical extent from which state distinctions arise, then FSD describes the ordered transitions among those distinctions:

> ε_D(t) →^G {S₀, S₁, ..., Sₙ}  [Eq. 27]

The FSD trajectory represents a measured projection or compression of the dynamical extent. Measured Dynamical Extent concerns the finite registered physical regime; Finite Symbolic Dynamics concerns the ordered symbolic evolution of the registrations generated from that regime.

The same operation continues into language and reasoning:

> dynamic extent → registration → symbol → symbolic trajectory → compressed concept  [Eq. 28]

The Functional Symbolic Trajectory (FST) therefore extends the same structural concern beyond the immediate physical measurement. The word *matter*, for example, is not merely a dictionary entry — it carries a historical trajectory of prior distinctions, uses, expectations, equations, experiments and conceptual commitments. Replacing *matter* with *Measured Dynamical Extent* is therefore not simply terminological substitution: it changes the trajectory available to subsequent reasoning, making explicit what the noun suppresses (that the description is measured; that the described extent is dynamical; that stability is conditional rather than absolute; that the physical state is finitely registered; that the resulting category is symbolic).

## Interactional Density (§18)

A preliminary concept: interactional density ρ_I, characterising different regimes of MDE:

> ρ_I ~ registered interactional change / measured dynamical extent  [Eq. 29]

Different dynamical regimes may be distinguishable not simply through conventional measures such as mass density, temperature or pressure, but through the organisation, persistence and concentration of interactions across an extent. A crystalline state and gaseous state may differ not because one contains "more matter" in any fundamental sense, but because the spatial and temporal relations between registrations occupy different stability regimes. Interactional density may eventually provide one descriptor within a larger finite state vector:

> **D**_E = (ρ_I, τ_S, λ_R, C, δ, ...)  [Eq. 30]

where τ_S represents a measured persistence interval, λ_R a relational scale, and C some admissible measure of coupling or coherence.

## Significance

PE16 works at the deepest level of the FSM framework — not a new technical result but a reconfiguration of the foundational grammar of physical description. By replacing matter (an ontological primitive) with Measured Dynamical Extent (an operational concept defined through registration, stability and provenance), it enables solid, liquid, gas, plasma, condensate, and vacuum to be reconceived as stability regimes rather than kinds of substance. This move is philosophically consistent with the FSM commitment to finite distinction as foundational and ontological priority as unnecessary.

The Pensée also extends the Alphonic Limit from a purely spatial resolution floor to a dynamical stability floor — the minimum dynamical extent over which a distinction can remain stable enough to participate in registration. This is a significant refinement of the concept that was first established in PE03 and formalised in M18.

The connection to nouns as stability compression (§15) provides a bridge to the language-dynamics side of the corpus: words like *river*, *flame*, *organism*, and *matter* itself are all MDE compressions, and the FST that word carries into subsequent reasoning depends on whether the compression is treated as transparent or opaque. PE16 thus connects foundational physics (phases, vacuum, the nature of matter) to the broader linguistic and representational concerns of the Pensée series.
