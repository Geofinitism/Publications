## Core Claim

The seventeenth-century conversion of ongoing processes into portable, stable nouns created a lasting momentum of symbolic stabilisation that became a forward constraint on all subsequent scientific thought. The halted noun — the compressed, trajectory-free symbol — is both the great achievement of early modern science and the cognitive and institutional inheritance that FSM operates inside.

Pascal's unease about definition and Wittgenstein's private-language argument are two articulations, three centuries apart, of the same finite condition: no symbol can be grounded in a private or absolute terminus. Every admissible mark already requires a surrounding trajectory that is, in principle, shareable. FSM formalises this convergence through the minimal Functional Symbolic Trajectory holding the registration mark ∼*o*, the Alphonic Limit that forbids a zero-point of pure privacy, and the Generonic Boundary that renders further private ascent inadmissible.

> "The historical momentum of the halted noun therefore acts as both the condition of possibility and the forward constraint of the present work."

## Overview

PE12 is an eleven-page, two-chapter position paper that situates the operational apparatus of FSM within its historical and philosophical inheritance. Chapter 1 traces the seventeenth-century programme of symbolic stabilisation — from Baconian natural history and the Royal Society's practices of witnessing and publication, through Hooke's *Micrographia* and Boyle's experimental histories, to Pascal's registered unease — and shows how that programme produced the forward constraint of compression. ## Chapter 1 — Momentum, Constraint, and the Forward Pressure of Compression

### §1.1 Overview

The seventeenth-century transformation of scientific practice established a durable momentum: the systematic conversion of recurrent processes into stable, portable nouns. The chapter traces that trajectory from Baconian natural history and the Royal Society's practices through Boyle, Hooke, and Pascal, shows how the resulting symbolic stabilisation became a forward constraint, and frames the subsequent operational chapters of FSM as the contemporary recovery of that trajectory.

### §1.2 The Momentum of Stabilisation

Modern science acquired much of its power by turning verbs into nouns. A changing world of interactions was divided, named, catalogued, measured, and represented mathematically. The decisive operation was the same in every case: selected distinctions were detached from the complete trajectory of the encounter and stabilised as portable symbols. A plant became a specimen; a specimen became a description; a description became a class; the class received a name that could circulate after the specimen had decayed. A moving body became a trajectory; the trajectory became a relation between measured positions and times; the relation became a mathematical function that could be analysed in the absence of the body. At every stage the language gained stability and lost trajectory.

The institutional programme — Baconian accumulation of particulars, Royal Society practices of witnessing and repetition, Hooke's instrumentally mediated images in *Micrographia*, Boyle's experimental histories retaining a disciplined symbolic trajectory judged sufficient for another investigator to understand, the *Philosophical Transactions* as a continuing route for observations to enter institutional memory — was so successful that the existence of the compression could be forgotten. That forgetting is the origin of the forward constraint.

### §1.3 The Noun as Historical Technology

To call the noun a scientific technology is to recognise what it permits: it compresses a potentially unbounded process into a repeatable symbolic unit that can be entered into an inventory, placed within a hierarchy, joined to a measurement, connected to a property, represented by a variable, and carried into calculation. It permits a distinction to persist after the original event has ended.

Pascal, standing inside the same seventeenth-century transformation, already registered an unease. A word is defined through other words; those words depend upon prior use or further definition; at some point the symbolic chain terminates operationally within a community capable of continuing the discourse, not by reaching the thing itself. The written mark *point* does not lose extension because a mathematical point is defined as having none — the written mark remains extended. The defined term does not become identical to the process, object, or relation to which it is applied. The warning was repeatedly overtaken by success; the gap remained, and the institutional success of the noun steadily widened it.

### §1.4 Forward Constraint: Momentum Becomes Path-Dependence

Once the halted noun had proved its power, the habit of irreversible compression acquired institutional momentum. Textbooks, curricula, mathematical formalisms, journal conventions, and laboratory practice all reinforced the same direction of travel: process → registration → pattern → classification → entity. Ordinary prose then reversed the sequence: entity → property → observed effect. The entity now appeared as the cause of the registrations from which it had been inferred.

This reversal is a cognitive and institutional constraint. The more successfully the noun travels, the more difficult it becomes to recover the omitted trajectory. The cost of keeping the full Functional Symbolic Trajectory explicit — the cognitive load of provenance, the temporal cost of recounting every averaging step, the communicative bandwidth required to carry residual uncertainty — rises until further recovery becomes inadmissible inside a working community. That point is the Linguistic Generonic Boundary, itself a historical product of the seventeenth-century programme. FSM does not stand outside this history; it operates inside the momentum that history created.

### §1.5 Integration with the Operational Apparatus

The preceding operational chapters formalise the contemporary face of this historical trajectory. The establishment of registration as the finite hinge between interaction and text, and the Generonic Boundary as the wall beyond which further holding becomes inadmissible, corresponds to the same act that Boyle and Hooke performed when they selected, from an experimental event, the symbolic trajectory judged sufficient for travel. The Generonic Boundary is the institutional and cognitive limit that the Royal Society's compression strategy made durable.

The minimal Functional Symbolic Trajectory holding the registration mark ∼*o*, and the necessity of provenance *P* and relational frame *R*, are the operational counterparts of the essay's insistence that a scientific object is what remains after an interaction has been registered, symbolised, and stabilised sufficiently to re-enter language. The Alphonic Limit is the measurement-theoretic expression of Pascal's unease: the symbolic chain cannot be driven to a context-free terminus. The forgetting of compression is not a modern aberration; it is the continuation of a historical strategy that began when the noun first proved it could make the world travel.

### §1.6 The Task of Recovery

The seventeenth-century project made the world portable by stabilising processes into things. The contemporary task is to preserve that achievement while recovering the motion hidden by its success. Compression remains necessary; the requirement is that compression remain reversible when the omitted trajectory becomes consequential.

An admissible noun is one that preserves enough of its originating trajectory for the intended purpose. The questions that restore admissibility — What distinctions produced the noun? How were they registered? Which instruments and symbolic procedures were involved? Over what duration and at what scale does the classification remain stable? What residual was omitted? — are precisely the questions that FSM renders operational through the apparatus of registration, minimal FST, Alphonic Limit, and Generonic Boundary.

### Conclusion (Chapter 1)

The historical trajectory that began with the systematic conversion of processes into portable nouns created a lasting momentum that made modern science cumulative and communicable; it also installed a forward constraint that renders the recovery of the omitted trajectory increasingly costly. FSM inherits this double inheritance. The operational chapters that precede and follow are not an external critique of scientific language; they are the formal attempt, from within the momentum itself, to keep the halted noun unfoldable.

## Chapter 2 — Pascal, Wittgenstein, and the Shareable Trajectory: Definition, Private Language, and the Finite Condition of the Symbol

### §2.1 Overview

Pascal's unease about definition and Wittgenstein's private-language argument converge on a single finite condition: no symbol can be grounded in a private or absolute terminus. Every admissible mark already requires a surrounding trajectory that is, in principle, shareable. Chapter 2 formalises that convergence inside FSM. Pascal shows that the definitional chain terminates operationally inside communal use; Wittgenstein shows that even the apparent starting-point of the chain cannot stand alone. Both insights are recovered operationally in the minimal FST holding ∼*o*, in the Alphonic Limit that forbids a zero-point of pure privacy, and in the Generonic Boundary that renders further private ascent inadmissible.

### §2.2 Pascal's Residual

Pascal's unease, articulated from within the seventeenth-century programme of stabilisation, is precise. A definition appears to promise absolute symbolic security: a word is fixed by other words, those by still others, until the chain seems to rest on something solid. Yet the chain never reaches the thing itself; it terminates operationally inside a community that already knows how to continue the discourse.

The written mark "point" retains extension even when the mathematical definition declares that a point has none. The word "vacuum" coordinates a set of experimental and conceptual distinctions; it does not produce an absolute absence. The word "water" does not contain every possible molecular organisation, history, temperature, or use. The instrument cannot become identical with what it distinguishes. Definition is a finite, communal act of compression. The residual — the gap between the symbol and the process — is not a philosophical inconvenience to be eliminated; it is the permanent signature of every successful scientific noun.

### §2.3 Wittgenstein's Public Criterion

Wittgenstein radicalises the same insight. A private language, in the strict sense he rejects, would be one whose terms refer to immediate private experiences and are intelligible only to the single speaker. Such a language is impossible because meaning requires the possibility of error, of correction, of going on in the same way — and these require public criteria. An ostensive definition performed solely for oneself cannot fix a rule; there is no independent standard against which the private speaker can check whether the next use is correct.

Meaning is therefore not a private mental object attached to a mark. It is the stabilised continuation of a practice inside a form of life. The mark must be holdable by more than one trajectory. The private-language argument is the delayed philosophical recognition of the residual that the historical compression of the scientific noun had systematically omitted.

### §2.4 The Shared Finite Condition

Both thinkers diagnose the impossibility of a context-free, privately grounded symbol. Pascal shows that the definitional chain is finite and terminates inside shared use; Wittgenstein shows that even the apparent starting-point of that chain (the private ostension) cannot stand alone.

In FSM this is exactly the structure of the minimal Functional Symbolic Trajectory holding ∼*o*:

FST_min(∼*o*) = (∼*o*, P_∼o, R)

The raw registration ∼*o* already requires provenance *P* and relational frame *R*. Without them it is not yet a symbol; it is only a physical disturbance of finite extent. The trajectory that holds it is, by construction, the beginning of a shareable practice. A purely private ∼*o* would fail the fourth condition of the minimal FST (closure under the Generonic process) because there would be no residual that could be returned to a common measurement epoch.

The Alphonic Limit reinforces the same point from the side of measurement: even the smallest admissible distinction still occupies volume and still costs a surrounding trajectory. There is no zero-point at which a mark becomes self-interpreting and private. The Generonic Boundary is the point at which further private ascent becomes inadmissible inside the current measurement epoch.

### §2.5 Historical Momentum as Forward Constraint

The seventeenth-century programme succeeded by making the halted noun travel, institutionalising a momentum in which definitional chains were shortened for portability, private or residual elements were discarded, and the communal trajectory was treated as background rather than as constitutive. Wittgenstein, writing three centuries later, encounters the late form of that momentum — the philosophical temptation to locate meaning inside a private mental object — and dismantles it. The private-language argument is therefore not an external critique of the scientific noun; it is the recognition, inside philosophy, of the residual that the historical compression had rendered difficult to see.

FSM inherits both the achievement and the constraint. Every registration, every density map, every atomic label still moves inside a linguistic field shaped by the halted noun. The operational task is to keep the trajectory that Pascal and Wittgenstein both insisted upon — the finite, shareable, residual-bearing trajectory — visible whenever the claim requires it.

### §2.6 The Operational Requirement

The two uneases converge on a single operational demand:

> No symbol is admissible unless the Functional Symbolic Trajectory that holds it remains, in principle, recoverable and shareable.

This demand does not abolish compression. It renders compression reversible. It restores provenance as constitutive rather than optional. It treats the residual not as noise to be eliminated but as the permanent signature of every finite symbol. And it places the historical momentum of the halted noun under explicit constraint: the forward pressure that once made the world portable must now be balanced by the capacity to unfold the noun back into the trajectory from which it was formed.

### Conclusion (Chapter 2)

Pascal's residual and Wittgenstein's public criterion are two articulations, three centuries apart, of the same finite condition that FSM formalises. Every symbol requires a shareable trajectory; every trajectory is bounded by the Alphonic Limit and the Generonic Boundary; every historical success of the halted noun remains a forward constraint that must be kept visible. The chapters that follow can therefore treat the recovery of the verb not as an optional philosophical luxury but as the necessary continuation of the very trajectory that made scientific nouns possible in the first place.

## Significance

PE12 provides the historical and philosophical grounding for FSM's insistence on recoverable, shareable trajectories. By placing the FSM apparatus inside the long history of the scientific noun — from the Royal Society's practices of stabilisation through Pascal's unease and Wittgenstein's private-language argument — it shows that FSM is not an external imposition upon scientific language but a recovery, from within its own momentum, of the trajectory that successful compression has rendered increasingly difficult to see.

The Pensée establishes three things for the wider corpus: (1) the halted noun is a deliberate historical technology whose costs are now as real as its achievements; (2) Pascal and Wittgenstein independently arrived at the same finite condition that FSM formalises operationally; and (3) the requirement of shareability and recoverability is not a philosophical luxury but a structural demand that follows from the very conditions that made symbolic knowledge possible.
